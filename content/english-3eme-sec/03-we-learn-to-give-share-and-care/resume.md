# 📜 Summary: We Learn to Give, Share and Care

- **The vocabulary of giving** — **philanthropy** (concern for human welfare shown by giving money, property or work), **altruism** (unselfish concern for others, against **egoism**), **activism**, **volunteerism** / voluntarism, **charity**, **donation**, **self-sacrifice**, **generosity** (against **meanness**), **solidarity**, **endowment** (a permanent fund whose income pays year after year), **fund-raising**. Plus **benevolence**, the **common good**, **welfare**, **the needy**, and **in-kind contributions**: goods, commodities or services given **instead of money**.
- **Agent nouns — the person from the act** — **-or**: donate → **donor**. **-er**: endow → **endower**, raise funds → **fund-raiser**, sacrifice one's interests → **self-sacrificer**. **-ist**: philanthropy → **philanthropist**, activism → **activist**, altruism → **altruist**. A noun in **-ism** gives its person in **-ist**; a verb usually gives **-er**, a small closed group **-or**. Traps: "donator" ✗ → *donor*, "philanthroper" ✗ → *philanthropist*, "volunteerer" ✗ → *volunteer*. The one who receives is the **beneficiary**.
- **Compound adjectives** — one hyphen, in front of the noun: **fund-raising** concert (noun + -ing), **well-run** charity (adverb + past participle), **ever-increasing** costs, **state-of-the-art** equipment (a whole phrase), **hard-working** volunteers. The first word **never takes -s**: "funds-raising" ✗.
- **The emphatic form** — **do / does / did + base verb**, to push a fact back at somebody who doubts it: _The committee **does publish** its accounts._ Only in an **affirmative** sentence, only in the **simple present or simple past**, and the verb after it stays in the **base form** ("did gave" ✗, "does needs" ✗). Where there already is an auxiliary — *be*, a modal, a perfect — you add nothing and the auxiliary itself takes the stress: _She **has** paid_ ("do are" ✗, "do can" ✗). Putting the key word first does the same work: _**Now** is the time._
- **need** — an ordinary full verb: **need + noun** (_the dispensary **needs** a second nurse_), **need + to + base verb** (_volunteers **need to** sign_), **don't / doesn't need**, **do / does … need?** No *to* before a noun, and no continuous ("are needing" ✗). Noun: *a need*, *in times of need*; adjective: *needy*.
- **Phrasal verbs** — **care for** / **look after** (take care of), **give up** (abandon), **grow up** (attain maturity), **catch up with** (meet people you have not seen for a while), **win** somebody **over** (gain their favour and support), **stay up** (remain awake), **come round** (pay an informal visit), **take over** (assume responsibility), **gossip about**, **bring out**. **Separable** ones let the object move, but a **pronoun goes in the middle**: _won them **over**_ ✓, "won over them" ✗. **Inseparable** ones (*care for, look after, come round, grow up, catch up with*) never split.
- **should have + past participle** — the sensible action that **did not happen**: _They **should have asked** first._ The negative says the opposite, that it happened and was wrong: _They **shouldn't have left** her alone._ Against plain **should + base verb**, which advises about now or the future. The participle is compulsory ("should have go" ✗).
- **Present perfect against simple past — the table of the year** — Present perfect = **have/has + past participle**, for a past still joined to today: (1) started in the past and **has continued**; (2) went on **until very recently**; (3) **repeated** in a period reaching now; (4) **just finished** or with a **result** that counts now. Its markers: *up to now, so far, all this year, recently, lately, already, just, ever, never, yet*. Simple past = the past form, for short finished actions, longer past situations and repeated happenings — and for **a time named and finished**: *yesterday, last week, in 2002, two years ago*, which the present perfect **always refuses**. **since** = the point where the period begins, **for** = how long it lasts; both open a period reaching today, so the main verb is a present perfect, and a **clause** after *since* takes the simple past: _She **has volunteered** there **since she left** school._ Hence _She **has done** so **since she was five**_ is a **present perfect**, not a simple past — read the main verb, *has* + *done*. **How long has…?** → present perfect; **When did…?** → simple past.
- **The other simple past** — after **if, suppose, as if, if only, it's (high) time, would rather, wish**, the past form describes something **unreal or overdue** now, not the past: _I **wish** I **had** more time._ · _**It's high time** this street **had** a pavement._ ("I wish I have" ✗, "it's high time we do" ✗)
- **A speech that persuades** — purpose: entertain, inform or **persuade**. Shape: greet and catch attention → introduce the organisation and its **key mission** → develop with figures and examples → **state the counterargument and refute it** → call for action and close. Four hooks: **ask a question**, **tell an anecdote**, **cite a quote**, **give statistics**. A speaker pauses at the end of a **thought group**, not at the printed commas.
- **Discourse markers** — open a list: *firstly, first of all, to begin with, to start with, in the first place, for one thing*; continue: *secondly, thirdly, for another thing, another thing is*; add: *moreover, in addition, similarly, as well as that, on top of that*; add something **stronger**: **besides**; place in time: *earlier, later, before that, after that*; close: *finally*.
