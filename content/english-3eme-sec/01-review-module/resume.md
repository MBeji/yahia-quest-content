# 📜 Summary: Review Module

- **Reporting somebody's words** — drop the inverted commas, open a **that-clause**, and move three things: the **person** (every _I / my_ of the speaker becomes _he / his_ or _she / her_), the **tense** (present → simple past, simple past and present perfect → past perfect, **will → would**) and the **pointers** (_here_ → _there_, _this_ → _that_, _now_ → _then_, _today_ → _that day_). You **tell somebody** something, with no _to_; you **say** something, and _to_ only when you name the listener — "She said me…" ✗, "She told to me…" ✗.
- **let and make** — both take an object and then the **base verb**, never _to_: _My parents **let me choose**._ · _The storm **made us cancel** the trip._ **make** gets its _to_ back in the passive — _The company **was made to close** the factory._ ✓, "was made close" ✗ — and _let_ has no such passive: English says _be allowed to_.
- **used to** — **used to + base verb** names a past habit or state that **has stopped**: _People **used to write** letters._ Negative **didn't use to**, question **Did … use to**. Do not confuse it with **be used to** + noun or **-ing**, a **present** state: _She **is used to working** nights._ Traps: "use to be" (the -d is gone), "used to being" (the -ing belongs to the other structure).
- **Comparing** — **-er/more … than** puts A above B, **the -est/the most** puts A at the top of a group, **as … as** puts A and B level, **not … enough** (after the adjective) says A falls short. Irregular: _good → better → the best_, _bad → worse → the worst_, _far → further → the furthest_. The **double comparative** links two changes: _**The longer** you wait, **the harder** it gets._ — both _the_'s are needed, no superlative and no "more better".
- **The present perfect against the simple past** — a **named, finished moment** (_in 1998_, _ago_, _last year_) takes the **simple past**; **no moment named** and a result that counts today takes the **present perfect**; a period **reaching today** takes the present perfect with **since** (starting point) or **for** (length), and the **present perfect continuous** (_have/has been + -ing_) when the activity has not stopped. "Since 1998 the library is open" ✗ → _has been open_ ✓.
- **The passive** — **be + past participle**, with the tense carried by **be**: _The bridge **was built** in 1970._ · _The road **has been repaired**._ After a modal the shape is fixed, **modal + be + participle**: _This machine **can be used**._ A verb with **two objects** passivises from the person: _The finalists **were given** a second chance._ ✓ — from the thing it needs _to_. Never drop _be_, never keep an -ing form.
- **Holding a text together** — **linkers** name the relation: cause (_because, as, since_), result (_so, therefore_), contrast (_but, however, yet, although_), addition (_and, besides, moreover_); _because_ is followed by the cause and _so_ by the result. **Order** follows the time markers (_in 1902 … that winter … within two years … then … today_), and a sentence opening with _then_, _so_ or _however_ can never come first. **Reference words** (_he, it, this, these, there_) point back — a deleted part goes exactly where a reference word has lost what it points at.
- **The business letter** — five blocks down the page: the **head** (the two addresses and the date), the **salutation** (_Dear Mr Sassi,_ — the last line before the body), the **body** (why you write, the facts, what you ask for), the **closing formula** (_Yours sincerely,_) and the **signature block** (signature, name, job title).
- **Word building** — read the **slot**: a **noun** after _the, a, every_ or before _of_ (-tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ty), an **adjective** before a noun or after _be_ (-al, -ful, -less, -ous, -ive, -y), an **adverb** beside a verb (-ly), a **verb** after a subject (-ise, -en, -ify). Some nouns change their end instead (_grow → growth_, _long → length_, _descend → descent_), and one verb feeds a family (_employ → employer, employee, employment, unemployed_). English also **stacks the describing words in front of the noun**: a **lung-testing machine**, a **space station**.
