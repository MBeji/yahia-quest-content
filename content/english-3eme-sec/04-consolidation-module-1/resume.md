# 📜 Summary: Consolidation Module 1

- **The words of the household** — **members** (the people of the family), **within** (inside), a **household** (everybody under one roof), **responsibilities**, to be **assigned** (given to you by somebody else), to be **expected** (what others count on), to be **required** (what the situation obliges — stronger), to **fulfil** a role (American *fulfill*), to **arise** (to come into being: _problems arise_), **assistance** (help), **understanding**, **overburdened** (given far more to carry than one person can). Traps: **arise** takes no object, **rise** goes up, **raise** always has an object; and *required* is not *expected*.
- **The verb that goes with its noun** — **take out** the rubbish (American *the trash*) · **make** decisions · **maintain** discipline · **provide** affection · **take care of** children · **overcome** a problem. The pair is learnt whole; nothing in *maintain* announces *discipline*. **take care** keeps its **of**, **look after** takes none: "takes care the twins" ✗ · "looks after of the twins" ✗.
- **One root, three columns** — the word family is a row **verb · noun · adjective**: _decide / decision / decided_ · _grow / growth / grown_ · _behave / behaviour / behavioural_ · _expect / expectation / expected_ · _frustrate / frustration / frustrated–frustrating_ · _satisfy / satisfaction / satisfactory–satisfied_ · _cooperate / cooperation / cooperative_ · _volunteer / volunteerism / voluntary_ · _sacrifice / sacrifice / sacrificial_. **A cell can be empty**: English builds no ordinary verb on **charity** (→ charitable) or on **philanthropy** (→ philanthropic) — "to philanthropise" ✗. One verb can feed two facing adjectives (**frustrating** causes it, **frustrated** feels it), and one form can sit in two columns (*sacrifice*). Also _health → healthy_, _support → supportive_, _develop → development_, _difficult → difficulties_, _effective → effectively_ — and **good → well** ("works good" ✗). A verb used as **subject** goes into **-ing**: _**Establishing** clear roles helps the family._
- **Punctuation and capitals** — a capital opens every sentence and a full stop (or a question mark) closes it; **proper nouns** take a capital, and so does **an adjective built on one** (*African*, *Tunisian*); a **comma** follows a long opening phrase and separates the items of a list; the **apostrophe** marks possession (_his father's freedom_); **I** is always a capital. Trap: an important word is **not** a proper noun — _he became a seaman and a property owner_, no capitals.
- **Putting a paragraph back together** — each piece carries the mark that places it: a piece opening on **and / then / so** can never come first; a piece opening on an **-ing** form hangs on the piece before it; a person named **in full** comes before every *he* or *she*; a list opening on **the** completes a verb printed earlier; the **first date** usually opens the whole thing. Method: find the only piece that can open, then ask of each remaining piece what must be said before it makes sense.
- **The two pasts, and how to say why** — **present perfect** when the period runs up to today (_he **has been** in hospital for three weeks_), when the result counts now, and with **yet** (_he **hasn't recovered** yet_); **simple past** for a moment named and finished (_he **fell** last Tuesday_) and for a chain of finished events. **for** does not choose the tense on its own — _has been … for three weeks_ (still there) against _stayed … for three weeks_ (came out); **since** opens a period that is still running and calls a present perfect, its own clause usually staying in the simple past. **it's (high) time** takes a past form for what is overdue now (_it's high time we **planned** something_), like *wish*, *if only*, *as if*, *suppose* and *would rather*.
- **Four rewrites** — _I'm sorry I don't have enough money_ → _**I wish I had** enough money_ (**wish + past**, and the sentence stays about today). _Parents have to be understanding; **otherwise** they may have problems_ → _**If parents aren't** understanding, they **may have** problems_ (*otherwise* becomes *if + not*, and *may* stays). _Shall I phone you at 3?_ → _I'd **rather you phoned** at 4_ (**would rather + subject + past** when somebody else acts; _I'd rather stay_ when the subject does not change). _Some parents don't **allow** their children **to have** their own way_ → _Some parents don't **let** their children **have** their own way_ (**allow → let** costs you the *to*).
- **The phrasal verbs of the consolidation** — **decide on** (settle on one choice) · **gaze at** (look fixedly) · **turn out** (end in a particular way) · **pick up** (lift from the ground) · **look for** (seek) · **dream of** · **provide for** (supply somebody's needs) · **flow in** (come in from many places) · **set off** (make somebody suddenly very angry) · **catch up** (do the work you should already have done) · **give up** · **grow up** · **look after** · **take over** · **gossip about** · **bring out**. Warnings: **catch up** (be behind with work) is not **catch up with** (meet people you have not seen); **set off** usually means *start a journey* or *trigger a device*; **pick up** and **give up** split around their object and a **pronoun goes in the middle** ("gave up it" ✗), while *look for*, *look after*, *gaze at*, *dream of* and *provide for* never split.
- **The modals** — **must** (obligation, forceful advice) · **should** (advice) · **should have** (the sensible thing that did not happen) · **shall** (with *I* and *we*: a future you commit to; in a question, an offer) · **may** (permission, and possibility) · **need**. **need the full verb**: takes *-s*, uses *do*, and stands before a noun or before **to** + verb (_she **needs** a nurse_ · _you **need to see** a doctor_). **needn't the modal**: absence of necessity, no *-s*, no *do*, bare verb — _you **needn't send** it by air_ — and **needn't have + past participle** for something done that was not necessary — _you **needn't have made** such a big cake_. **needn't is not mustn't**: *mustn't* forbids, *needn't* only says nothing obliges you. *needn't have done* tells you the thing **was** done; *didn't need to* only says the necessity was absent. The **bare verb is the border**: _must see_ ✓ but _need **to** see_ ✓.
- **From the definition to the word** — burdened too heavily → **overburdened** · laugh (verb) → **laughter** (noun) · provide a regular income for an institution → **endow** · help or kindness to somebody suffering → **comfort** · force or influence exerted on somebody → **pressure** · give voluntarily → **donate** · a person who offers to do something → **volunteer** · not instrumental but → **affective** · protests and demonstrations to achieve a goal → **activism** · help to the poor → **charity**. Read the little words in brackets first: _laugh (verb) → … (noun)_ asks for the **noun of the family**, not for a definition.
- **A life in a few words** — the vocabulary any biography needs: lose one's **sight** and one's **hearing**, learn to **communicate**, **graduate** from a college, be remembered for one's **accomplishments**, serve the **causes** one believes in, travel for speaking **engagements**, be **awarded** a medal — the **highest** honour that can be conferred on a civilian. Two grammar points hide there: **is awarded** and **can be conferred** are **passives**, and **the highest** is the superlative of the short adjective *high*.
