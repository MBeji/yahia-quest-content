# 📜 Summary: Science and Technology — a Blessing or a Curse?

- **The frame** — an invention is a **blessing** (positive impact) or a **curse** (negative impact). The module argues both ways and never decides for you.
- **Three verbs** — **discover** = find something that already existed but was unknown (*to discover electricity*); **invent** = make or create something new that never existed (*to invent the telegraph*), and also to **make up** / **fabricate** a story or an excuse; **originate** = begin something new, above all an idea, a method or a movement. **science** = knowledge gained through **observation and experimentation** (**pure** for its own sake, **applied** put to work); **technology** = the creation and use of **technical means** and their interaction with life, society and the environment. Around them: a **device**, a **lens**, a **laser**, a **robot**, **surgery**, **dialysis**, **disposable**, **cellular**, to **orbit**, to **set up**, **ingenuity**, **nano technology** (working on the scale of atoms and molecules).
- **The dates and names the book gives** — air conditioner **Willis Carrier, 1902** · penicillin **Alexander Fleming, 1928** · insulin **1922** · voice recognition machine **Bell Labs, 1936** · photocopier **Chester F. Carlson, 1937** · kidney dialysis **William Kolff, 1944** · mobile phone **1947** · transistor radio **Texas Instruments, 1953** · computer hard disc **1956** · laser **Gordon Gould, 1958** · compact disk **James Russel, 1965** · first man-made satellite orbiting the moon **1968** · cellular phones **1979** · hepatitis-B vaccine **1980** · disposable contact lenses **1987** · digital cellular phones **1988** · HTTP and HTML **Berners Lee, 1990** · DVD **1995**. First robot **1921**; **Microsoft set up by Bill Gates**. ⚠️ Two of these are the book's dates and history reads them differently: a machine reached lunar orbit in **1966** (1968 is the first *crewed* flight round the moon), and **1921** is when Čapek's play *R.U.R.* was staged — it coined the **word** *robot*, it did not build one.
- **Negative prefixes** — *il-*, *im-*, *ir-* and *in-* are **one prefix**, and the **first letter of the word** picks the spelling: **il**legal (l) · **im**moral, **im**possible (m, p, b) · **ir**regular, **ir**responsible, **ir**reversible (r) · **in**curable, **in**human, **in**estimable (the rest). The rule says **how to write it, not which words take it**: *successful* → **un**successful ("insuccessful" ✗), *known* → **un**known. **un-** and **dis-** follow no letter rule (**un**able, **un**acceptable, **un**wanted, **un**ethical · **dis**appear, **dis**abled, **dis**order). **mis-** = the action done **wrongly** (*misunderstand*, *misread*, *misuse*), **de-** = the action **reversed** (*defreeze*). The book's box also shows **ab**normal, with a prefix meaning "away from".
- **The laboratory word bank** — **ethics** (principles of proper conduct) · an **issue** (a question that arises for discussion) · **controversial** (likely to cause prolonged argument) · **DNA** (a molecule found within a chromosome) · a **chromosome** (a structure in the nucleus containing DNA) · **cloning** (creating a new organism) · **gene therapy** (inserting normal DNA into cells to correct a defect) · the **germline** (the genes passed on to one's children) · a **designer gene** (a gene altered or created by genetic engineering) · **genetic testing** (checking someone's genetic material to predict disease) · **DNA testing** (analysis of DNA, RNA, chromosomes and proteins) · **blood clotting** (liquid blood turning semisolid). Plus **artificial insemination**, **in vitro fertilization**, **recombinant DNA**, **surrogate**, **transgenic**, **hereditary**, **transplant**, **tumour**, **side effect**, **extinct**, **endangered**.
- **Opposites** — show ≠ **conceal** · deny ≠ **confess** · spread ≠ **shrink** · innocent ≠ **guilty** · agree ≠ **object** · enhance ≠ **reduce** · succeed ≠ **fail**. (*conceal* and *hide* are **synonyms**, not opposites.)
- **The nouns of the section** — treat → **treatment** · diagnose → **diagnosis** · choose → **choice** · disappear → **disappearance** · perform → **performance** · insert → **insertion** · suspect → **suspicion** · prove → **proof** · avoid → **avoidance** · confess → **confession** · innocent → **innocence** · curious → **curiosity**. *cure* and *release* keep the same form as their verb; *diagnostic* is normally an **adjective**, never the noun built on *diagnose* ("diagnosement" ✗, "provement" ✗, "choosal" ✗, "suspectation" ✗).
- **Compound adjectives — the eight patterns of the grammar summary** — adjective + noun (*long-range forecast*, *long-term threat*) · number + year + old + noun (*an 18-year-old woman*, *a 12-year-old girl*) · adverb + *-ing* (*rapidly-growing populations*, *rapidly-increasing cancer*) · noun + *-ing* (*foot-stamping dance*, *life-threatening reaction*) · adverb + past participle (*well-run philanthropy*, *genetically-modified pea*) · number + noun (*two five-pound notes*, *24-hour shopping*) · noun + adjective (*odour-free tablets*) · noun + past participle (*a snow-covered mountain*). Folding a clause into a block: cells that produce insulin → **insulin-producing** cells · chemicals that cause cancer → **cancer-causing** chemicals · a period of six months → a **six-month** period · 104 chemicals made by men → 104 **man-made** chemicals · 3000 twins who are two years old → 3000 **two-year-old** twins. The noun inside stays **singular** ("six-months" ✗). **-ing** if the thing acts, **past participle** if it is acted on.
- **Modals** — **can** (ability, possibility) · **can't** (not possible, not able) · **may** (permission, real possibility) · **might** / **could** (future possibility) · **must** (obligation, emphatic advice) · **mustn't** (**prohibition**) · **should** (advice) · **needn't** (**absence of necessity**). Always followed by the **infinitive without to** ("can to wake" ✗), never take **-s** ("she cans" ✗), and the verb stays in the **base form** ("may leaves" ✗). *mustn't* forbids; *needn't* frees. *can't* = not possible; *mustn't* = not allowed.
- **Stress patterns** — expressions written as **two separate words** (*gene therapy*, *burial services*, *breast cancer*) take the main stress, says the book, on the **second** word; compound nouns written as **one block or with a hyphen** (*make-up*, *book-case*, *test-tube*) take it on the **first part**.
- **Reading a science page** — **trigger** = activate · **dismiss** = reject · **rule out** = exclude · **leap** = increase suddenly · **prick** = make a tiny hole with a sharp point · **decapitate** = cut off the head · **poultry** = farm birds · **a patch** = a small piece worn on the skin. Plus **handset**, **threat**, **consumption**, **nuisance**, **available**, **promising**, **malignant**, **detect**, **infect**, **inflame**. **strain** has two meanings here: a **variety** of a plant (*a strain of beans*) and a **type of virus** (*the bird flu strain*). Three verdicts: **true**, **false** (the text says the opposite) and **not mentioned** (the text says nothing, either way).
- **Speaking** — approve / disapprove. Do you think that… · In my opinion · To my mind · As far as I am concerned · Frankly / Honestly · **I'd rather + base verb + than + base verb** ("I'd rather to use" ✗) · It sounds interesting, but… · I have my doubts about… · You haven't convinced me yet. Nine uses of a computer: **send e-mail, write a letter, do calculations, surf the internet, print, listen to music, watch DVDs, record CDs, play games**. Gadgets: **game console, MP3 player, digital camera, DVD, CD**.
- **Writing to persuade** — before writing, name the **type of text** (genre), the **expected readers** (audience) and the **purpose**. A persuasive text **addresses its reader and asks for something**; it does not merely explain or describe. The book's checklist: specific purpose, specific readers, related sentences, information in the right order, proper linkers, no unnecessary details, an opening that catches attention.
- **The debate** — **For**: gene therapy repairs damaged genes and replaces missing ones; against cancer it **targets only the diseased cells**; germline therapy could cure inherited disorders and pass the benefit on; designer babies could answer the wishes of parents of a sick child; recombinant DNA proves guilt or innocence, identifies the dead and determines ancestry. **Against**: side effects during testing (**fevers, abnormal blood clotting, partial paralysis, death**); the question of **access** — developed against developing countries; DNA testing turned into a **business** by funeral homes charging a fee, raising **privacy and ethics** questions; children are of **inestimable value in themselves**; genetic disorders are as much a **social construct** as a difference in genes; intelligence and athletic performance also depend on **upbringing, nutrition, education, experience and healthy living**; ageing, illness and death are parts of a human biography.
