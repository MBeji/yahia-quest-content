# 📜 Summary: A Change Is as Good as a Rest

- **The words of the topic** — **Leisure**: *canoeing* (a paddle), *harbour cruising* (round a harbour by boat), *hiking* (a long country walk), *sightseeing* (the monuments of a town, guided), *sunbathing*, *surfing*, *water skiing* (pulled behind a boat), *roller skating*, *sky diving*, *safari* (wild animals, from a vehicle), *dog sledge riding*, *cycling*. **Accommodation**: a **cottage** = a small house in the country · a **terraced house** = one of a row, joined to its neighbours · a **spacious en suite apartment** · a **tent** (that is *camping*) · a **resort** = a whole holiday place, not a building · *accommodation* = the general word. **Services** — hotel: swimming pool, games room, mini-gym, sauna, **ironing facilities**, **baby-sitting facilities**, car park, **24-hour laundry service**, free newspapers; room: colour TV, direct dial telephone, hairdryer, mini-bar, private bathroom, room service, air-conditioning, writing desk. **Coast**: *shoreline, bay, creek, cliff, reef, lagoon, cave, harbour, path, wreck, crystal clear*. **Feelings**: before — *excited, enthusiastic, hopeful*; after — *disappointed, regretful, embarrassed, fed up*. Plus **spoil**, **compensation**, **break down**, **get rid of**, and a **fortnight** = two weeks.
- **Comparatives and superlatives** — short adjective: **quieter than** / **the quietest**; long adjective: **more expensive than** / **the most expensive** (and *less / the least*). Two-syllable ones vary (*heavier*, but *more careful*). Irregulars: **good → better → the best**, **bad → worse → the worst**, **far → further → the furthest**, **little → less → the least**, **much/many → more → the most**. In front of a comparative English puts **much, far, a lot, a bit, a little** — but not ***very***: "very slower" ✗ → *much slower* ✓; and it does not double a comparative: "more quieter" ✗, "more better" ✗, nor add a regular ending to an irregular form. A comparative takes **than**, a superlative takes **the**: "the quietest than the port" ✗. After a superlative: **in** + a place (*the busiest café in the medina*), **of** + a group you can count or a period (*the best of the four*). Two things level → **as + adjective + as**. An advertisement or a timetable is **scanned**, not read like a story: every claim is checked against its own lines, and what it does not say is not yours to assume.
- **Prepositions of place and movement** — **along** (follows a line: *walk along the quay*) · **across** (from one side of a surface to the other: *row across the bay*) · **through** (in one side and out the other: *drive through the tunnel*) · **around** (round it, or here and there in it) · **into / out of** (an enclosed space) · **off** (leaves the way you were on: *never step off the path*) · **towards** (the direction, without arriving) · **back to**. And the three that place you: **at** a point (*at the harbour, at 18.00*), **on** a surface (*on the beach, on Friday*), **in** an area (*in the countryside, in July*).
- **The past perfect against the simple past** — **had + past participle**, the same *had* for every person. The simple past marks the moment you are telling about; the past perfect marks the one that was **already over** before it: _We reached the museum at four. It **had closed** at half past three._ Whatever order the sentence uses, the **past perfect is the earlier event**. With **before** and **after** the order is already written on the page, so the past perfect becomes optional. And when two actions are **close in time or closely connected**, the book says the past perfect is **not usually used**: _When he arrived at the hotel, he **asked** for a room._ For a length of time filled before that past moment: **had been + -ing**. And a **title** for the account must hold the whole of it: test it against the first line and the last, because one that fits one end and contradicts the other is a caption, not a title.
- **WH questions** — **where** (place) · **when** (moment) · **who** (person) · **why** (reason) · **how long** (length of time) · **how far** (distance) · **how often** (number of times) · **how much** (price, or a quantity you cannot count) · **how many** (a number you can count). Shape: **WH + auxiliary + subject + base verb** (*How far did you travel?*), except when the WH word is itself the subject (*Who paid for the trip?*). An **interview** is not a conversation: prepared questions on one topic, no opinion from the interviewer, time left for the answer.
- **Linkers** — announce bad news: *unfortunately*; open the list: *to start with, in the first place, firstly*; add: *added to this, besides, what is more*; close the list: *last but not least, finally*; cause: *as, since, because, because of, owing to*; result: *thus, therefore, so*; purpose: *so that, in order to*; contrast: *although, even though, in spite of, despite, however*. **The rule that decides half of them**: a **conjunction** takes a whole **clause** (*because the wind was strong*), a **preposition** takes a **noun** or an **-ing** form (*because of the wind*). Hence "because of the wind was strong" ✗, "owing the strike" ✗, "despite of the rain" ✗ — and *however* meaning *but* is an **adverb**, so it needs its own sentence. Do not confuse **at last** (relief after a wait), **eventually** (how a process ended) and **last but not least** (the final item of a list).
- **The letter of complaint** — blocks, from the top: your address · the date · the company's address · the salutation · the body · the complimentary close · your name. **The plan of the body, in three parts**: (1) the reason for writing, with the exact details of the holiday; (2) the details of the problems; (3) **what you expect from the company**. Register: firm and polite at once, on the frame _I **should be grateful if you would** …_ — three things spoil it, an **order**, a **threat**, and a request buried under **hedges** that never says what is wanted. The close: **look forward to + -ing**, because that *to* is a preposition — _we look forward to **seeing** you_ ✓, "look forward to see" ✗, "look forward for" ✗.
- **The survey report** — say how many people you asked, what the survey was about, what came out of it. The figures become words: **none of us** · **some of us** · **half of us** · **most of us** (more than half) · **nearly all of us**.
- **Vowels — the spelling does not decide the sound** — seven boxes, each held by a key word: **moon** (*food, through, routine, blue, lagoon*) · **now** (*found, south, around, mouth*) · **book** (*wood*) · **more** (*board*) · **throw** (*boat*) · **bus** (*flood, countryside* — the *ou* of *country* is the vowel of *bus*, not of *now*) · **poor** (*tour*). Read by the key word, never by the letters: *wood* and *flood* share *-ood* and sit two boxes apart, while *through* and *food* share no spelling at all and land together.
