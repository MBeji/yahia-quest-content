# 📜 Summary: In Time of Test, Family Is Best

- **Four family roles, two types** — **provision of resources** (money, food, clothing, shelter) and **maintenance and management of the family system** (leadership, decisions, finances, discipline, standards) are **instrumental**; **nurturance and support** (comfort, warmth, reassurance) is **affective**; **life skills development** can be either, task by task. The test: does the act move a **thing** or a **feeling**? Key words: nurture, shelter, resource, leadership, maintenance, establish, handle, standard, share roles, be overburdened, chores.
- **If + present + present** — the **automatic or habitual** result: every time the condition holds, the result follows. _If you keep your problems to yourself, nothing **gets** better._ Both halves stay in the present. Compare with the one-occasion prediction **If + present, will + verb** (_If she raises her voice tonight, the little one **will cry**_). A comma separates the halves only when the if-clause comes first — and **`will` never goes inside the if-clause**.
- **want + object + to-infinitive** — what one person wishes **another** would do: _My grandmother **wants my brother to phone** her every Sunday._ The word after want is an **object pronoun** (me, you, him, her, us, them) and the **to** is compulsory. English has **no that-clause after want**: "wants that I choose" ✗ → _wants me to choose_ ✓. Same pattern for _would like_ and _expect_.
- **make / let / have + object + base form** — **make** = force (_makes her sons tidy the kitchen_), **let** = allow (_let us choose our own subjects_), **have** = arrange for it to be done (_has the pupils on duty ring the bell_). None of the three takes **to**; only *want* does. Causative **have** is not the auxiliary of a perfect tense: _She has the pupils ring the bell_ (somebody else rings) ≠ _She has rung the bell_ (she rang it).
- **Word building on roles and feelings** — suffixes: **-ful** (careful, helpful), **-less** (careless, helpless), **-ly** for the adverb (openly, politely), **-ment** (agreement, treatment), **-ion** (depression, frustration), **-ness** (kindness, illness), **-ance** (annoyance). Reversing prefixes: **un-** (unhappy, unkind), **im-** (impossible, impolite), **in-** (inability), **dis-** (disagree, disrespect), **mis-** (misunderstand, misconduct). The prefix follows **no rule** — it is learnt with its word.
- **Pronunciation** — silent letters by family: **gh** (night, daughter), **k** before n (know, knee), **b** after m (comb, climb), **t** (listen, castle), **h** (hour, honest), **l** (calm, would), **w** before r (write, answer). And the same spelling can hide two sounds: _love / above_ share one vowel, while _daughter / laughter_, _heart / heard_ and _child / children_ each split into two.
- **Talking about the generation gap** — **complaining** (_I am really… · I feel like a… · They want me to… · I just wish…_), **interrupting** (_Before you go on, let me… · Could I stop you here for a moment?_), **disagreeing politely** in two moves, granting then objecting (_You have a point, but… · I see what you mean, but… · Well, that might be true, but…_), **giving an opinion** (_In my opinion… · To my mind… · As far as I am concerned…_). Idioms: **see eye to eye** (agree entirely), **get in somebody's hair** (annoy by being always around), **jump down somebody's throat** (scold far too quickly), **blow a problem out of proportion** (treat something small as huge), **barge in** (enter rudely), **feel like a puppet** (feel controlled), **can't help it** (be unable to stop oneself).
- **The four traps** — no that-clause after want; no **to** after make, let and have; no **will** inside an if-clause; and the apostrophe on the right side of the s (_my sister's room_ = one sister, _my sisters' room_ = at least two, _the children's room_ — never "childrens'").
