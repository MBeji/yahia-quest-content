# 📜 Summary: Family Life

- **Saying what you want** — **I want to** + base verb (_I want to study medicine._) and the softer **I'd like to** + base verb (_I'd like to work with children._); with a noun, _I want a quiet room_ / _I'd like a word with you_. The **to** is compulsory ("I want become" ✗), _would like_ never takes _-ing_ ("I'd like becoming" ✗), and _I like to read_ is a habit, not a wish.
- **Family words** — **siblings** (brothers and sisters), **stepmother / stepmum** (your father's wife, not your mother), **stepfather**, **stepson / stepdaughter**, **grandchild**, **Nan** (an affectionate name for a grandmother); do not confuse **stepmother** with **mother-in-law**, who is the mother of your husband or of your wife.
- **What a family gives** — **security**, **protection**, **support**, **guidance**, **care**, **appreciation**; and around them **trouble** (difficulty), **a path** (the way you choose), **to be willing to**, **to accomplish**, **to comment on**.
- **Narrating past events** — the past simple, regular in _-ed_ and irregular in three forms to learn by heart: _become – became – become_, _feel – felt – felt_, _grow – grew – grown_, _hold – held – held_, _keep – kept – kept_, _know – knew – known_, _make – made – made_, _say – said – said_, _stride – strode – stridden_. Never "growed", never "know – known – knew".
- **The present perfect: form** — **have / has + past participle**: _I have worked_, _she has worked_, _I haven't worked_, _has she worked?_, short answers _Yes, I have. / No, I haven't._ After _have_ or _has_ the verb is always a past participle ("has write" ✗, "has wrote" ✗).
- **The present perfect: uses** — a situation that started in the past and **still lasts** (_He has kept the same shop since he was twenty._), an **experience up to now** (_We have never been to Djerba._, _Have you ever met them?_), and a past event whose **result matters now** (_I have lost my keys._).
- **Its markers** — **since** + a starting point (_since 2018_, _since she left school_), **for** + a length of time (_for six years_), **yet** in negatives and questions (_hasn't arrived yet_), **already** in affirmatives, **ever / never** for experience, **just** for a moment ago, **so far** for a total; and two compulsory frames: _It's the first time I **have** travelled alone._, _This is the best film I **have ever** seen._
- **Present perfect or past simple?** — one question: **is the moment named and over?** Yes (_yesterday_, _last winter_, _in 2015_, _two months ago_, _when he was a child_) → **past simple**; no, or it reaches today → **present perfect**. _Aymen bought that bike in 2019._ / _Aymen has had that bike since 2019._ Careful: **for** goes with both tenses (_My grandfather worked here for forty years_ = he has stopped / _has worked here for forty years_ = he is still there), so only the tense tells you whether the story is over.
- **Describing a person** — **kind**, **caring**, **affectionate** on the warm side; **arrogant** and **selfish** on the cold side; **proud** goes both ways — warm in _proud of my results_, cold in _too proud to ask for help_.
- **Words from the story** — **delight** (great pleasure), **grin** (a wide smile), **melt** (be filled with tender emotion), **shriek** (a short high cry), **clutch** (hold tightly), **a bunch** (of flowers, of keys), **astonished** (speechless with surprise), **stare** (look long and fixedly), **weigh somebody up** (observe before judging).
- **The trap wall** — _I saw her yesterday._ (never "I have seen her yesterday"), _She has lived here since 2018._ (never "She lives here since 2018"), _for three years_ (never "since three years"), _They have finished._ (never "have finish"), _It's the first time I have seen snow._, _I want to be a teacher._
