# 📜 Summary: Attitudes and Values

- **The words of money and evil** — the **root** (the origin), **evil** (great wrongdoing, noun and adjective), **corruption** (obtaining by money or power what one has no right to; the person is **corrupt**), **tyranny** (cruel and unjust power), **dignity** (the respect every human being is owed), **decent** (good enough and honest), the **appeal** of something (the pull it has on you), **cheating**, **revenge**, **anarchy** (no law, nobody in charge), a **calamity** (a disaster). Three expressions: **to make ends meet** (earn just enough and nothing more), **right away** (immediately — not distance), and _money is **a good servant and a bad master**_. Pairs worth learning whole: _decent → decency_, _cruel → cruelty_, _corrupt → corruption_.
- **Opinion against fact** — a **fact** is what anybody can check (a date, a sum, a number); an **opinion** is a judgement somebody else may refuse. Open one with _I think…_, _In my opinion…_, _I strongly believe…_, _I honestly feel…_. A number is never a point of view.
- **Naming a result: therefore · as a result · that's why** — all three say cause → result. But **so** is a **conjunction** and holds two clauses together on its own, while **therefore** and **as a result** are **conjunctive adverbs**: they only point back, so the first clause must be **closed** before them.
- **The punctuation the examiner counts** — _The line was cut**, so** we waited._ (comma before _so_, nothing after) · _The line was cut**;** **therefore,** we waited._ (semicolon, then a comma) · _The line was cut**.** **Therefore,** we waited._ (full stop, then a comma) · _The line was cut**.** **That's why** we waited._ (no comma after it). ⚠️ The **comma splice** — "The line was cut, therefore we waited." ✗ — is the mistake that costs most marks here: your own language allows it, English does not.
- **Where the adverb stands** — at the **head** of the clause (_**Therefore,** the coach turned back._), between the **subject and the verb** (_The coach **therefore** turned back._), or between **two commas** round the subject (_The coach**, therefore,** turned back._). ✗ **Never** between the verb and what follows it: "The coach turned therefore back." **that's why** never moves: it is a little clause and stays at the head.
- **The register** — **therefore** belongs to careful **written** English (report, composition, examination paper); **as a result** is written too, a little less formal; **that's why** carries a contraction and belongs to **speech**. And one link takes **one** linker: "Because he was ill, that's why it was cancelled" ✗.
- **The words of freedom** — **freedom** (acting, speaking and thinking without being prevented), **rights** (what you may claim) against **duties** (what you owe in return), **peace**, a **community**, **to forgive** (stop holding a wrong against somebody), **precious** (of very great value), **love**.
- **Three sounds** — **/ʌ/** in _love, other, money, trouble_ (the letter _o_ very often says /ʌ/); **/aʊ/** in _sound, around, doubt, proud_; **/ʃ/** in _precious, expression, machine, sure_ (one sound, four spellings). _sound_ and _money_ share an **o** and nothing else.
- **The imperative** — **base verb**, no subject (_Close the window._); **don't + base verb** for a prohibition (_Don't close the window._); **let's + base verb** when the order includes the speaker (_Let's close the window._), negative **let's not**. Add **please** to soften, **do** to insist (_Do be careful._). Four values with one form: **instruction**, **advice**, **prohibition**, **invitation**. To a stranger the bare form sounds like an order — English softens it into _**Could you** …, **please**?_; half-way, a tag: _Keep it for me, **will you**?_ ⚠️ "You don't leave your bag here" ✗ (that is a statement) · "Not leave" ✗ · "Don't to leave" ✗ · "Let's to start" ✗ · "Let's don't start" ✗.
- **The question tag: three moves** — the **auxiliary** of the statement comes back (none in the statement → **do / does / did**: _The bank lent him the money, **didn't it**?_); the **polarity turns over** (positive → negative tag, negative → positive tag); the subject becomes a **pronoun**.
- **The special cases** — **I am** → **aren't I** (_I'm late, aren't I?_ — there is no _amn't_), but _I'm not late, **am I**?_ · an **imperative** → **will you** · **let's** → **shall we** · **there is** keeps **there** (_There's a problem, **isn't there**?_) · **everybody / somebody / nobody** → **they** (_Everybody has signed, **haven't they**?_) · **nothing / this / that** → **it** · a statement with **never, hardly, nobody, seldom** is already negative, so its tag is **positive** (_He never writes, **does he**?_).
- **The voice, and the answer** — the voice **falls** → you are sure and want confirmation; the voice **rises** → you do not know and are really asking. And yes or no answers the **fact**, never the shape of the question: _"You didn't lock the door, did you?"_ with the door locked → _**Yes, I did.**_ ✓ ("No, I locked it" ✗ makes an English ear understand the opposite.)
- **Judging a character** — praise: **honest**, **truthful**, **trustworthy**, **reliable** (does what was promised, on time), **faithful**, **tolerant**, **loving**, **correct**. Blame: **insincere**, **jealous**, **suspicious** (believes without proof that others do wrong), **deceitful**, **shameful**, **indifferent**, **immoral**, **corrupt**. Plus an **emergency** (a sudden danger needing action right away) and **to talk somebody round** (to bring somebody who was refusing to say yes).
- **Holding your ground** — grant, then turn, then explain: _You have a point, **but**… **To start with**…_ Give your opinion (_I strongly believe…_), disagree politely (_I'm afraid I don't agree._ · _I see what you mean, but…_), give your reasons (_That's the reason why…_ · _For this reason…_ · _When you consider that…_). Refusing without explaining is not a viewpoint.
