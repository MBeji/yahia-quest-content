# 📜 Summary: Rights and Duties

- **The words of a life without rights** — **a slave** (owned and forced to work for no pay), **a master**, **property**, **a trader**, **to hire** (to pay to use, or to pay somebody to work), **a field hand** (an outdoor farm worker), **labour**, **a whipping**, **a penalty** (the fixed punishment), **hardship** (difficult living conditions), **burial**, **evidence** (the facts that prove — uncountable), **restless**. An **autobiography** is written by the person who lived it, a **biography** by somebody else.
- **The passive voice — the formula** — **passive subject + be (in the tense of the active verb) + past participle (+ by + agent)**. Only **be** moves; the participle is frozen (_taken, given, told, paid, beaten, written_, or verb + _-ed_). Present: _This right **is protected**._ / _The workers **are paid**._ Past: _The Declaration **was adopted** in 1948._ / _The brothers **were sent** away._ Negative and question are built on _be_: _The report **is not published**._ / _**Was** the report **published**?_
- **From active to passive, three steps** — the active **object** becomes the passive **subject**; the verb becomes **be + past participle**; **be** takes the **tense of the active verb**. _The association published the report._ → _The report **was published** by the association._ It works backwards too: _The contract **was cancelled** by the court._ → _The court cancelled the contract._
- **Broken forms** — "were took" ✗ → _were **taken**_ (participle, not past simple) · "was pass" ✗ → _was **passed**_ · "The report published in May" ✗ → _was **published**_ (no _be_, no passive) · "The files was stolen" ✗ → _were_ (be agrees with the passive subject) · "is speaking" ✗ → _is **spoken**_.
- **by + the agent: keep it or drop it** — **keep** it when the agent is **news** (_The law was written **by three young lawyers from Sfax**_). **Drop** it when the agent is **unknown** (_My bag was stolen._), **obvious** (_The men were arrested._ — by the police), or **unimportant / everybody** (_English is spoken here._ — "by people" adds nothing). A fourth reason, the one the lesson lives on: the writer wants the sentence to be about the person who **suffered** the action. The passive is also the voice of **scientific writing** and of **reports of accidents** where the result matters more than the cause.
- **Reading a passive sentence** — two questions, two answers. **The subject** is the noun the verb **agrees** with and the one that **receives** the action (_In 1994, a school for girls was opened in the village…_ → the school). Not what merely stands in front of the verb: _In 1994_ does, and a **time phrase** is never a subject — no more than a **place phrase**, since a group that opens with a preposition (_in_, _at_, _by_, _for_, _of_) can never be one. **The doer** is what comes after **by** — and if there is no **by**, the honest answer is **the sentence does not say**; never invent an agent. In _The children were taught by a volunteer_, the children teach nobody.
- **Expressing displeasure** — _It is **unacceptable** that…_ · _I **object to**…_ · _What **shocks** me most is…_ · _I **strongly disapprove of**…_ · _I am **deeply concerned about**…_ — a judgement on a situation, not a rating of an experience.
- **The words of equality** — **gender** (male or female), **equality**, **empowerment** (giving somebody more control over their own life), **oppression**, **commitment** (a binding promise), **a promotion**, **the millennium** (a thousand years).
- **Expressing intention** — _We **are going to** publish…_ · _The association **intends to** open…_ · _They **plan to** train…_ · _She **is determined to** finish…_ · _**Our aim is to** give…_
- **a little / a few / a lot of** (the grammar summary of page 92) — **a few + a countable noun in the plural** = some, **not many but enough** (_a few minutes_) · **a little + an uncountable noun** = some, **not much but enough** (_a little time_) · **a lot of + either** = **more than enough** (_a lot of mistakes_, _a lot of work_). Choose in two moves: can I count it and put _-s_? then _a few_; if not, _a little_; and if the quantity is large, _a lot of_, which takes both kinds. Traps: "a few money" ✗ → _a **little** money_ · "a little friends" ✗ → _a **few** friends_ · "a lot of informations" ✗ → _a lot of **information**_ (also _advice, work, progress, evidence_) · "a lot people" ✗ → _a lot **of** people_. Without **a**, the meaning turns round: _**few** countries signed_ = almost none.
- **Building the words of rights** — oppress / oppression / an oppressor / oppressive · empower / empowerment · commit / commitment / committed · declare / a declaration · promote / a promotion · obey / obedience / obedient · equal / equality · free / freedom. **-er** and **-ee** split the family: the **employer** gives the work, the **employee** does it, and **employment** is the fact of having work (trainer/trainee, interviewer/interviewee).
- **Progress Check 2** — it does come back to this theme: passive gap-fills (_was given_, _was never told_, _was beaten_), **how many** against **how much**, notes expanded into passive sentences (_Two men **were taken** to hospital._), the right member of a word family, and punctuation — plus the comparatives, superlatives and linkers of the earlier themes.
