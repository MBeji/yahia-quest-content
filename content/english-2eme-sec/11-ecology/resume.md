# 📜 Summary: Ecology

- **The six defined terms** — **ecology** (the study of the relation of plants and living creatures to each other and to their environment) · **the environment** (the natural conditions — land, air, water — in which people, animals and plants live) · **global warming** (the rise in the atmosphere's temperature, caused when certain gases, especially carbon dioxide, hold the sun's heat) · **a rain forest** (a thick forest in tropical parts with a lot of rain, the Amazon for example) · **wildlife** (wild animals and birds) · **ozone depletion** (a thinning in the ozone layer that lets harmful rays through). ⚠️ The exercise prints **one definition too many** — *the scientific study of animals and their behaviour* — which is **zoology**: ecology studies **relations**, zoology studies **animals**.
- **More words of the theme** — **depletion**, **thinning**, **to endanger**, **extinct** / **extinction**, **fumes**, **to purify**, **to ban**, **deforestation**, **a drought**, **rainfall**, **consumption**, **to account for**, **to drill**.
- **Adverbs of degree** — **quite** (moderate) · **very** (high) · **too** (excessive), all **before** the adjective; **enough** (sufficient) **after** it. ⭐ *old **enough***, *warm **enough*** — "enough old" ✗. ⚠️ But with a **noun**, `enough` comes **before**: *enough time*, *enough water*. Adjective → after; noun → before.
- **`enough to` and `too … to` — the question of what is still possible** — adjective + **enough to** + verb: the quality is sufficient, so the action **happens** (*The case is light **enough to** carry*). **too** + adjective + **to** + verb: the quality is excessive, so the action **cannot** happen (*The plate is **too** hot **to** touch*). **not** + adjective + **enough to**: the quality is insufficient, so it **cannot** happen either (*The ice isn't thick **enough to** walk on*). ⭐ **To transform a pair of sentences, ask whether the action happens** — yes → *enough to*; no → *too … to*. ⚠️ **The negative case catches everybody**: *the ice is not very thick, we can't walk on it* fails, but not because the ice is *too* thick — ask which quality is **missing**. ⚠️ Never repeat the object: *too hot to touch* ✓, "too hot to touch **it**" ✗.
- **The four reports (*Time*, 30 October 1995)** — **ozone**: uncontrolled CFC damage lets more solar ultraviolet through, damaging **crops** and causing **cancer**. **Global warming**: about **1 °C** in the past century, possibly **5 °C** over the next; sea levels would cover coastal areas and river deltas, endangering more than **100 million** people. **The oceans**: pollution and **over-fishing** wipe out coral reefs and threaten whales and dolphins with **extinction**. **The rain forests**: only **6 %** of the land surface but **more than half** the world's species, and **0.25 %** or more of forest species lost each year.
- ⚠️⚠️ **The book contradicts itself four pages apart** — p.148 says *global warming is a fact today*, p.151 says *scientists are uncertain about the long-term impact*. Both date from 1995. Quote the **mechanism** (greenhouse gases accumulate because we release millions of tonnes of carbon a year) and the **figures with their date** — never the sentence about uncertainty as though it described science now.
- **Fourteen things that can be done** — reduce vehicles and factories · filter toxic **fumes** · **purify** waste water · find non-polluting energy · stop ozone-damaging chemicals · **ban** nuclear tests · **recycle** waste · safeguard rain forests and ban **deforestation** · protect **endangered species** · build **wildlife** conservation areas · plant trees · encourage voluntary work · legislate on the seas · educate people. ⭐ Say which four matter most **and why**, and you have the speaking task.
- **Water scarcity — the four paragraphs (2003 Independent Digital)** — **B** opens (the gravest challenge may be the **lack of fresh water**; only **2.5 %** of the earth's water is fresh, and it is under pressure **from several directions**) · **A** follows (**population growth is the main cause**: six billion people by 2000, consumption almost doubled in half a century, demand from industry and agriculture as well as drinking and washing) · **D** closes (*All that's bad enough. But… still further* — **climate change**, likely to account for a fifth of the increase) · **C** is **crossed out**. ⭐ **The connectors give the order**: *several directions* announces a list, *the main cause* is its first item, *all that's bad enough… still further* can only come last. ⚠️ **Why C is the intruder**: it is not off-topic, it is **off-argument** — the text explains a shortage and its causes, C praises the **benefits of irrigation**. An intruder that shares the subject is the hardest kind to spot.
- **`not only … but also`** — the relation is **addition**, and the two joined elements must be **of the same kind**: two adjectives (*not only deaf but also unable to speak*), two nouns (*not only English but also German*), two verbs (*they not only smoke but also drink*), two clauses. ⚠️⚠️ **The agreement trap the book uses twice without teaching it**: when two **subjects** are joined, the verb agrees with the **second** — *Not only his father but also his mother **is** dead*, *Not only Peter but also John **is** a pop fan*. English looks at the noun **nearest** the verb. ⚠️ **The structure has two slots, not three**: *broke in, stole the books and tore up the manuscripts* needs regrouping or another linker.
- **Water in Tunisia** — the quiz asks about bath or shower, watering in the evening or in full sun, a bowl or running water for the dishes, a bucket or a **hose** for the floor and the car, a well at home, water-saving taps and meters, how fast leaks are repaired, and whether the **SONEDE** is told when the mains leak. The speaking task asks what is being done: **dams**, **mountain lakes**, **drilling** for deep groundwater.
- **Comparing — the three figures** — **a simile** compares **explicitly** with **like** or **as** (*her voice is **like** a bell*) · **a metaphor** says one thing **is** another with no word of comparison (*her voice **is** a bell*) · **a personification** gives human qualities to what is not human (*the wind **whispered***). ⭐ **The one-second test**: look for *like* or *as*. Present → simile. A structure repeated with *like* at the head of several lines is a chain of similes, and it expresses a **similarity** — not a difference and not a contrast.
- **Progress Check 3 — the four organisation tasks** — **the topic sentence**: read what follows and find what it is all about; the paragraph usually refers back with *this* or *that*. **Insert the missing sentences**: each continues the idea **before** the gap. **Punctuate and capitalise**: find where one complete idea ends. **Develop hints into sentences**: add the articles, auxiliaries and linkers the notes left out, and keep the order given. ⭐ In the exercise the second sentence is *Growth must be checked to avoid **this** catastrophe*, and *this* is the clue — the missing sentence must name the catastrophe (*the world's population will soon reach a level where there will not be enough resources to sustain life as we know it*).
- ⚠️ **Two things not to carry away from the page** — every figure in lesson 27 dates from **1995** and every figure in lesson 28 from **2003**; and the exercise sentence *He's deaf and dumb* uses a phrase that is **offensive in English today** — say *deaf*, or *deaf and unable to speak*.
