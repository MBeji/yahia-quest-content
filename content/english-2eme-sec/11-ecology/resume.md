# 📜 Summary: Ecology

- **The words of a damaged planet** — **ecology** (the study of how living things and their surroundings depend on each other) against the **environment** (the land, air and water themselves) · **global warming** · **ozone depletion** (the slow using up of the ozone layer) and its **thinning** · a **rain forest** · **deforestation** (the cutting down of a whole forest) · **wildlife** (wild animals and birds, taken together, never _a wildlife_) · **fumes** (always plural) · **to purify** · **to ban** (to forbid by law) · **to endanger** · **extinct** · **to trap** · **damage** (uncountable). **Endangered** = still savable; **extinct** = no living member left.
- **Adverbs of degree** — they say **how much**, they modify an **adjective or another adverb**, and they stand **in front of** it. Four degrees: **quite** = moderate · **very** = high · **too** = excessive · **enough** = sufficient. **enough** is the exception of place: **after** an adjective or adverb (_wide **enough**_), **before** a noun (_**enough** water_). Also in the grammar summary: _fairly_ (near _quite_), _extremely_ (above _very_), _almost_, _nearly_, _just_. **too is not very**: _very long_ states a fact, _too long_ is a complaint — more than anybody wants.
- **too + adjective + to** vs **adjective + enough + to** — same shape, opposite outcome. **too ... to** = the action **does not happen** (_The plastic was **too small to be picked up**._ — nobody picked it up). **... enough to** = the action **does happen** (_The pump was **light enough to be carried**._ — they carried it). Name the person with **for** before the infinitive: _too technical **for the pupils** to follow_ · _wide enough **for a lorry** to pass_.
- **The same fact, the opposite adjective** — every _not ... enough_ has a _too_ twin: _**not thick enough** to walk on_ = _**too thin** to walk on_ · _**not old enough** to vote_ = _**too young** to vote_.
- **not only ... but also — the table your book prints empty** — **Structure**: _not only_ + member 1 + _but also_ + member 2. **Meaning and function**: **addition** (never opposition, never result); it adds a second fact and says it counts as much as the first. **Example**: _Demand comes **not only** from homes **but also** from industry._ They are a **pair of conjunctions**: one half never stands alone.
- **The one law** — the two members must be of the **same grammatical kind**: two verbs (_**not only** returned the money **but also** paid the interest_), two nouns (_**Not only** the parents **but also** the children_), two adjectives (_**not only** warm **but also** salty_). "not only clever but also he works hard" ✗ → _**not only clever but also hard-working**_.
- **Inversion** — when the sentence **begins** with _Not only_, the auxiliary comes **before** the subject, as in a question, and _do / does / did_ appears if there is none: _**Not only does** the plant clean the water, **but** it **also** produces electricity._ · _**Not only did** they plant trees, **but** they **also** dug a well._ "Not only the plant cleans..." ✗
- **Agreement** — with two subjects, the verb agrees with the **nearer** one: _Not only the pipes but also the tank **is** leaking._ · _Not only the tank but also the pipes **are** leaking._ And **but also splits** round a following subject: _..., **but** the town **also** pays for it._
- **The words of water** — **rainfall** · a **drought** (a long period with little or no rain) · **consumption** (the amount used up) · a **reduction** · **to reverse** (to make something go the opposite way) · a **dam** · **to drill** · **running water** (from a tap) · a **hose** · **to dump** · a **bill** · **to account for** = to be a given part of a total (_farming **accounts for** 80 %_ = farming uses 80 % of it).
- **Simile and metaphor — named by your book, defined nowhere in it** — a **simile** says one thing is **like** another and shows it: the sign is **like** or **as ... as** (_The dry field was **like** a sheet of paper._). A **metaphor** says one thing **is** the other, with **no** _like_ and **no** _as_ (_The river is the town's oldest road._). A **personification** gives a human action or feeling to a thing (_The wind **complained** all night._). The test is mechanical: look for _like_ or _as ... as_ — if it is there, simile; if it is absent and a comparison is still stated as a fact, metaphor.
- **The words of music** — **lyrics** (the words that are sung, always plural) · a **melody** (the line of notes) · **tunes** · a **compact disc** · **to drown** (to die under water, and, of a sound, to cover another sound completely).
