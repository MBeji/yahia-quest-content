# 📜 Summary: Media and Arts

- **The two lessons** — lesson 23 argues for the **library** (a piece of public speaking: a town without books cannot grow); lesson 24 reports a **collapse** with figures (why the single stopped selling). One tool comes from each: **parallel change** from the first, **polarity** from the second.
- ⚠️ **Your book prints both rule tables empty.** The parallel change table (p. 127) contains only the words _Parallel change_ and the line _Ask your teacher for help_; the _any / some_ table (p. 131) has four unfinished lines and **two blank cells** under _With your teacher try to complete_. Everything stated below comes from the **appendix** (p. 174 and p. 175), which does give both rules in full.
- **The words of the library** — a **civilisation** (how a society organises itself and passes on what it learns), an **era** (a long period of history), **lifeblood** (what something cannot live without), to **explore** a subject, to **enlighten** somebody (explain to them what they did not understand), **depth**, an **expansion** (a growing bigger), **crafts** (the skills of the hand), **literacy** (being able to read and write; its absence is **illiteracy**), to **wonder** (ask yourself, or admire), to **pay tribute to** somebody (thank and praise them publicly - the _to_ belongs to the expression) and a **shortage** (not having enough of something).
- **The words of the market that fell** — a **decline** (a slow, steady fall over years) against a **plunge** (sudden and steep) and a **drop** (either); a **casualty** (whoever suffers because of an event); a **performance** (what an artist does on stage, or how well something sells); a **track** (one recorded piece); to **release** a record (put it on sale, no preposition); **supremacy** (being above everybody else); a **passion**; **doubt**; to **shun** (keep away on purpose); to **blame**. ⚠️ You blame **somebody for something**, or you blame **something on somebody** - never "blame on somebody for something".
- **Parallel change: the + comparative … , the + comparative …** — _The larger the hall, the more chairs we carry._ Four conditions: **two comparatives**, never two superlatives ("The most…, the most…" ✗); **both halves keep _the_** ("Harder you work, faster you finish" ✗); each comparative is built normally, so a short adjective takes **-er** ("the more high" ✗, _the higher_ ✓); and the second half **may fall while the first rises** - _The higher the price, the **fewer** copies a shop sells_ - with **fewer** before what you count and **less** before what you cannot.
- **The short form** — when the halves would hold only _be_ and a subject, both drop out: _The more, the merrier._ · _The sooner, the better._ · _The less said about it, the better._
- ⚠️ **Not the same as gradual increase** (_more and more expensive_, Theme X): parallel change has **two** things moving and needs **two** halves; gradual increase has **one** that keeps going the same way.
- **some or any? Polarity decides, not quantity** — both mean _a certain quantity or number_ and both go before a plural or an uncountable noun.
  - **some**: in an **affirmative** statement (_I can lend you some maps_); in a question that is really an **offer** or **invitation** (_Will you have some tea?_); in a **request** (_Will you carry some of these boxes?_); when the answer **yes** is expected (_Is there someone at the door?_).
  - **any**: after a **negative** verb (_He hasn't any change_); after the near-negatives **hardly / barely / scarcely** (_hardly any petrol left_); in a **genuine question** (_Have you got any photos?_); after **if** (_If you find any old letters…_); after an expression of **doubt** (_I doubt whether there is any bread left_).
  - **any** also sits in an **affirmative** sentence to mean _practically every_ - it does not matter which one: _**Any** pupil will show you the way._
- **no and none** — **no** is only an **adjective** and stands before a noun (_I have **no** apples_); **none** is only a **pronoun** and stands alone (_He has **none**_). Each replaces a whole _negative verb + any_: _I haven't any apples_ = _I have no apples_.
- **⭐ One negative per clause, and one only** — _I didn't see **anybody**_ ✓ (negative in the verb) · _I saw **nobody**_ ✓ (negative in the word) · "I didn't see nobody" ✗. The same choice every time: _haven't got **anything**_ / _have **nothing**_; _couldn't find it **anywhere**_ / _could find it **nowhere**_. Your own language allows the double negative; English does not. Check every clause holding **not**, **never**, **without** or **hardly**: what follows must begin with **any-**.
- **The compounds** — three series, same rule as the word they are built on: **someone · somebody · something · somewhere** (affirmative) / **anyone · anybody · anything · anywhere** (negative and question) / **no one · nobody · nothing · nowhere** (the negative itself). A compound of the third row is **singular** (_Nothing **has** changed_) and makes the clause negative on its own, so its verb stays affirmative (_Nobody came_ ✓, "Nobody didn't come" ✗). ⚠️ Your book prints **no one** in two words and never prints **nobody** on these pages; both are correct and both are asked for.
- **Reading a short cultural press article** — separate the **claim** from the **evidence** and ask which sentence really supports which (a figure about ticket sales proves nothing about posters); a comparison needs **both** its terms (_fell by half_ - from what, over what time?); and conclude **only** what the text says, not the cause you find likeliest.
- **Functions** — lesson 23, **expressing an opinion** and **sensitizing** (_In my opinion… · What worries me is… · We ought to remember that…_), and an opinion that sensitizes ends in something to do. Lesson 24, **expressing feelings and likings** (_I am fond of… · I can't stand… · What I like best about it is…_), and a liking becomes interesting the moment you say **why**.
- **Career research project, step 2 - the interview** — find out about the field and the person **before** you meet them; **write your questions in advance**, open ones rather than yes/no ones; **be ready to take notes**; **show interest and thank them**. Step 3 is a formal letter, and that thanks is where its tone is learnt.
