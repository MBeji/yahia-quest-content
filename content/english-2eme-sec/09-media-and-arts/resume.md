# 📜 Summary: Media and Arts

- **Inside a library** — **monographs** (books on one subject by one author), **dictionary**, **catalogue** (the list of what the library holds), **newspapers**, **journals** (specialist or academic periodicals), **magazine** (general, with pictures), **videotape**, **atlas** (a book of maps), **encyclopedia**. ⚠️ *journal* and *magazine* are not synonyms in English, and a French *journal* is a **newspaper** — which is why the book lists all three.
- **The argument for libraries (Lois E. Hole)** — librarians are the **heroes of civilization**, keeping the gathering of human experience; without libraries that progress would be impossible, since we can **explore** the thoughts of others; films have their place but understanding **in depth** means books, and since books are expensive, libraries stop reading becoming a hobby for the rich; books are the **lifeblood** of the library, they **enlighten** and encourage the **expansion** of culture, and a country's future needs a **literate** population; her gift would be a good education with no **shortage** of teachers, and a call to **pay tribute** to them.
- **How she argues** — the exercise says "tick the correct answer**(s)**", plural: she uses **reasoning** (cause to consequence) **and quotation** (a saying between quotation marks). ⚠️ **No statistics** — not one figure in the whole text — and nothing imagined.
- **The words of the argument** — **civilization**, **an era**, **the lifeblood**, **to explore**, **to enlighten**, **expansion**, **crafts** (skilled handwork), **literacy**, **a shortage**, **to pay tribute to**, **to wonder**.
- **Parallel change — the formula** — **The + comparative + subject + verb , the + comparative + subject + verb**. *The **more** you read, the **more** you know* — and it also goes down: *The **more** I forget, the **less** I know*. Short word → *the **faster***; long word → *the **more** interesting*; countable → *the **more** / the **fewer** books*; uncountable → *the **more** / the **less** money*; irregular → *the **better**, the **worse***. ⭐ The two halves are **not** two sentences: both need **the**, both need a **verb**, and neither stands alone. ⚠️ **Call it parallel change, not "parallel increase"** — the book prints *parallel change* on p.127, and its own last item goes **down**. ⚠️ Do not confuse it with `more and more` (next chapter): **`the more… the more…`** links **two** changes; **`more and more`** describes **one** thing changing steadily.
- **Death of the single — an analysis of 2002** — the single was the passion of teenagers and the first thing pocket money bought; it was killed by **the internet, MTV and mobile phones**. In the UK, third week of January 2002: **400,000** singles, the worst week in **35 years**, half the figure of four years earlier; only **10** singles passed 10,000 copies. In the USA sales fell nearly **9 %**, the only exception being **Eminem**, the sole artist of 2002 to pass five million; industry-wide **8.7 %**, the second yearly **decline** in a row. **Dr Fox** explains that there was once nowhere else to hear a song, and **blames** the record companies, whose compilations carry almost every single the same week it is released — forty tracks for a little more than the price of one. And radio plays songs up to **two months** before release, tiring the buyer before the shops have them.
- **The words of the article** — **a casualty** (a victim), **a passion**, **to blame**, **to shun** (reject, avoid deliberately), **a decline**, **to plunge** (fall suddenly and steeply), **a performance** (how well something sells), **to release**, **to surpass**, **a track** (one song), **supremacy**. ⭐ Three carry the whole argument: sales **plunge**, the single is the first **casualty**, the companies are to **blame**.
- **`some` and `any`** — **some** in affirmatives (*They found **some** letters*), in offers and requests (*Would you like **some** coffee?* · *Will you carry **some** of these bottles?*) and in questions expecting **yes** (*Did you see **some** tall men…?*). **any** in negatives (*The children don't want **any** rice*), in ordinary questions, **after if** (*If you want to get **any** coffee, buy it now*), after **hardly / scarcely / barely** and after expressions of **doubt** (*I doubt that there is **any** coffee*). Same rules for **someone / somebody / something / somewhere** against **anyone / anybody / anything / anywhere**.
- ⭐⭐ **The sentence printed twice** — *Would you like **some** coffee?* and *Would you like **any** coffee?* are **both** in the book, on purpose: *some* makes it an **offer** (the speaker expects yes), *any* makes it a **neutral question**. Learn the meanings, not the columns — the table cannot be filled without contradicting itself, since the book's own *Did you see some tall men…?* is an interrogative with *some*.
- ⚠️ **Two traps of form** — "I haven't got **no** money" ✗ → *haven't got **any***; "There's hardly **no** paper" ✗ → *hardly **any***, because *hardly* is already negative.
- **A formal letter of enquiry (project, step 3)** — your name and address · the organisation's name and address · the **date** in full · **To Whom It May Concern:** (capitals on every word, followed by a **colon**) · who you are and what you want · where to send the answer · *Thank you in advance for your attention to this request.* · **Sincerely,** and your name. ⭐ Use *To Whom It May Concern* only when you truly do not know the reader; with a name, write *Dear Mr…,* and close *Yours sincerely*.
- **The limerick** — five lines, rhyme scheme **A A B B A**: lines 1, 2 and 5 rhyme together and are longer; lines 3 and 4 rhyme together and are short. The first line usually names a person and a place, the last delivers the joke. ⭐ You are asked to **write** one, not to learn one.
