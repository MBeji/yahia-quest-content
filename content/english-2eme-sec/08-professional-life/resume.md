# 📜 Summary: Professional Life

- **The words of a working life** — a **clerk** (office employee), the **management** (those who run the company), a **profit** against a **deficit**, to **earn** money (you win a match, you earn a salary); a **degree** (the university qualification), **training** (being taught the job), an **internship** (a period inside a company to learn the work), a **scholarship** (money given to a student); a **physician** (doctor of medicine) and his **assistant**, a **diplomat**, a **clinic**, **surgery** (the operation), **stitches**, a **check-up**; **competitive** (wanted by many, obtained by few), **self-sufficient** (needing nobody's help), **hooked** on (caught by), **fed up with** (tired and bored of), **worthwhile** (worth the trouble).
- **The verbs of a life story** — to **pick somebody up** (collect them), **look after** (take care of), **settle down** (begin a stable life), **set up** a company (found it), **drop out** (leave a course before the end), **light up** (become bright). _pick up_ splits round a pronoun: _pick **him** up_ ✓, "pick up him" ✗.
- **The word that betrays you** — the two months inside a company are an **internship**, never "a stage": in English a **stage** is a step in a process, or a platform.
- **used to + the base verb** — the same three words for every subject. Two values: a **habit that has stopped** (_There **used to be** a cinema in our street._ — there is none now) and a **past routine not necessarily stopped**. It is the mirror of the present perfect: that tense says _still true now_, this form says _not true any more_.
- **used to — negative and question** — built with **did**, and after _did_ the **-d** goes: _She **didn't use to** work at night._ · _**Did** he **use to** live in Gafsa?_ · without _did_: _He **never used to** complain._ "didn't used to" ✗
- **used to against the others** — one dated event takes the **past simple** ("She used to sign the contract in 2019" ✗ → _signed_) · **would** also tells a repeated past **action** (_Every August my uncle **would drive** us to Tabarka._) but never a past **state** ("He would be a fisherman" ✗ → _used to be_) · a habit you still have takes **usually**, not _used to_.
- **be used to + a noun or an -ing form** — a different structure: _this is no longer strange to me_. _I **used to work** in a hotel_ (I no longer do) against _I **am used to working** in a hotel_ (it does not tire me). It agrees (_am / is / are / was_) and takes **-ing**.
- **Expressing purpose — the three forms** — **to** + base verb (the shortest) · **in order to** + base verb (the same, more formal, for a report or a notice) · **so that** + a clause. _Wassim gets up at five **to finish** his revision._ · _The centre printed the leaflet in three languages **in order to reach** every family._
- **What decides between them** — ask **who is meant to do the second action**. The subject already there? _to_ or _in order to_. Somebody else, named? then an infinitive cannot carry that person and only **so that** can: _She turned the radio down **so that** her brother could revise._ ✓ · "…to her brother revise" ✗ Exception: if the main verb has a personal object, the infinitive attaches to it (_He sent two pupils **to collect** the certificates._ — the pupils collect).
- **The modal inside so that** — **can / will** after a present, present perfect or future; **could / would** after a past. _I am writing it down so that you **can** find the office._ · _They wrote it in large letters so that everybody **could** read it._ Never an infinitive after _so that_: "so that to help" ✗
- **A purpose is not a consequence** — a purpose is what you wanted **before** and may have failed; a consequence is what happened **after**. _He left at six **so that** he would not miss the train_ (aim) against _He left at six, **so** he did not miss the train_ (result).
- **should have + the past participle** — a duty of the past that was not carried out: **regret** about yourself (_I **should have applied** in March._), **reproach** about somebody else (_You **should have told** the manager._). "should have wrote" ✗ · "should had written" ✗
- **The negative reverses the facts** — **should have** = it was **not** done and ought to have been · **shouldn't have** = it **was** done and ought not to have been. _I should have kept the receipt_ (I threw it away) against _I shouldn't have kept it_ (I kept it). And _You **should send** it_ is advice with time left, _You **should have sent** it_ a verdict with the deadline past. Second value, far from regret: an **assumption** — _The van left at six; it **should have arrived** by now._
- **Enquiring about an intention** — _What do you **want to become**?_ · _What are you **going to do** after the bac?_ · _What do you **intend to** study?_ · _Are you **planning to** apply?_ then _Why that job in particular?_
- **Reading a job profile** — what the person does all day (the tasks, not the title), how long the training takes, where the work is done, and the conditions read one by one: two conditions joined by **and** reject a candidate who meets only one; joined by **or**, one is enough.
- **The final -s, three sounds** — /ɪz/ after a hiss (_-s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -ge_): _stitches, wishes_ · /s/ after _p, t, k, f_: _check-ups, streets_ · /z/ after every vowel and _b, d, g, v, m, n, l, r_: _schools, gives, goes, affairs_.
- **The career research project** — the book prints **step one only**: describe your ideal job as a narrative report (the daily work, indoors or outdoors, with people or alone, hands or head, what you could earn, the qualities it needs, how you know you could do it). No length, no grid, no next step is announced — but keep the corrected page in a file.
