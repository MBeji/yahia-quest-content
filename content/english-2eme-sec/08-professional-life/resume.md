# 📜 Summary: Professional Life

- **The words of working life** — **a manager**, **a deficit** (spending more than you earn) against **a profit**, **a childminder**, **to drop somebody** (leave them on your way) and **to pick somebody up** (collect them) — the two ends of the same day — **to look after**, **to be fond of**, **self-sufficient** (able to manage without help), **relieved** (no longer worried because the feared thing did not happen), **guilty**, **to settle down**.
- **`should have` + past participle = regret** — *I felt she was having precious hours with him which **should have been** mine.* Something ought to have been the case, and was not; a past that cannot be changed. Same form as chapter 07, but met here **inside a text**, which is where it really appears.
- **`would` + base verb = a past habit that is over** — *He **would** make himself beans on toast and settle down to do his homework.* Repeated, over a stretch of past time now closed. ⚠️ Not a conditional and not a future: **the test is the presence of an *if***. *He would make himself beans on toast **if** he were hungry* is a different sentence. And *He **made** himself beans on toast* is one occasion, not a habit.
- **Purpose — the three structures, and the box on page 117** — **to** + verb (*They went into the fields **to pick up** mushrooms*) · **in order to** + verb (*She is having evening classes **in order to have** better qualifications*) · **so that** + a **whole clause** with a modal from the eight the book lists — can, could, shall, should, may, might, will, would (*She turned down the radio **so that she could** concentrate*). ⭐ **The rule that decides**: `to` and `in order to` take a **verb**, `so that` takes a **subject and a verb**. Same person in both halves → *to*; different people → *so that* (*The assistant does these little things **so that the doctor does not have** to do them*). ⚠️ **"for to learn"** ✗ and **"so that to learn"** ✗ — the first never exists, the second forgets the subject.
- **The final -s and its three sounds** — **/ɪz/** after a hiss (*stitches*, *wishes*) · **/s/** after a voiceless consonant (*check-ups*, *streets*) · **/z/** after a vowel or a voiced consonant (*affairs*, *gives*, *schools*, *goes*). ⭐ **Find it without a list**: say the word without the *-s*; if your throat vibrates, add /z/; if not, /s/; if the last sound is already a hiss, English must insert a vowel, and that is /ɪz/. ⚠️ *stitches* and *streets* both carry a **t**, but the **ch** of *stitches* forces /ɪz/.
- **Two dream jobs (listening)** — **Heidrun** wants to be a **physician's assistant**: check-ups and taking out **stitches**, *so that the doctor does not have to do them*; **four to six years** of college, **two** in a hospital as an **intern**; then her own small **clinic**. **Thorson** wants to be a **diplomat** in Japan: a master's in **International Relations**, then the diplomat school of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs — a **three-year** programme — then the Norwegian **Embassy** in Tokyo. Very **competitive**, but a rare language gives an edge: only 300 Norwegians live in Japan. As a child he wanted to be a **baseball player**, *so that he could* be famous the world over — **not** a fireman, which the recording names and denies.
- **The words of qualifications** — **a degree**, **a physician** (a doctor — ⚠️ *not* a physicist), **surgery**, **a check-up**, **stitches**, **an internship**, **competitive**, **to earn**, **training**.
- **Emma Hutchinson** — 36, teaches music, **partially deaf** from birth, a **hearing aid** in each ear. Her mother, **fed up** with the noise, booked her into piano lessons; she was **hooked** at once despite the difficulty of finding the **pitch**, and refused to use her deafness as an excuse. At **11**, a **scholarship** to music school. She plays French horn, violin, piano and trumpet. At **30**, a children's concert decided her: she **set up** *The Music House for Children*. At **36**, thirty teachers and nearly three hundred children across London, plus deaf children in a special school — which is what makes it **worthwhile**. *(Woman's Own, adapted.)* ⚠️ **Partially** deaf, never profoundly.
- **`used to` + base verb** — says two things at once: it was true **repeatedly or for a long time**, and it is **not true now**. *She **used to play** the triangle* (and does not any more) against *She **played** it at the concert* (one occasion) and *She **plays** it* (still). Forms: affirmative **used to** · negative **didn't use to** · question **did … use to**. ⚠️⚠️ **The d disappears after *did*** — *didn't **use** to*, *did you **use** to* — the commonest written mistake of the chapter. ⚠️ And do not put `used to` everywhere: *Traders sometimes **put** together camel trains* and *Each animal often **carried** 400 pounds* are ordinary past simples, since *sometimes* and *often* already carry the repetition; after a modal, no tense at all — *it could **travel** twenty miles a day*.
- ⚠️⚠️ **`used to` and `be used to` are two different structures** — **used to** + **base verb** = a past habit that has **stopped** (*I **used to work** at night*, and I no longer do); **be used to** + **noun or -ing** = being **accustomed** to something, now (*I **am used to working** at night*, and it no longer troubles me). The first has no *be* and no *-ing*; the second always has both. Lesson 22 uses only the first — but the second is one syllable away, and it is the commonest confusion of the year.
- **The words of the story** — **partially**, **deafness**, **a hearing aid**, **fed up** (tired and bored), **hooked** (unable to stop), **pitch** (how high or low a note is), **a scholarship**, **drama**, **to set up** (to found), **worthwhile**, **to light up**.
- **Career Research Project, Step 1 — your ideal job** — a narrative report covering: what you would do all day · indoors, outdoors or both · with people or alone · with your hands, your head or both · earnings potential · the **qualities** needed to succeed · how you **know** you could do it. ⭐ The last two make the difference: describing a job is easy, arguing that you have what it needs is the report.
- ⚠️ **Two printed errors, not to be learnt** — the son of lesson 20 is **Donavan** in the first paragraph and **Donovan** everywhere else (never answer on that name); and the language exercise of lesson 22 misquotes its own text as "that little **boy**" where the text says "that little deaf **girl**" — the person is Emma.
