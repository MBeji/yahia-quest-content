# 📜 Summary: Communicating with Others

- **The words of friendship** — **keep in touch** (go on being in contact) ↔ **lose touch** (stop being in contact), **renew** (start again after a break), **intimate** (very close), a **website**, a **password**, a **waitress** (a man is a **waiter**), **incredible** (hard to believe).
- **Judging a friend** — **honest** (tells the truth), **faithful / loyal** (never betrays you), **sincere** (means what they say), **open-minded** (accepts what is not like them); and on the cold side **negligent** (careless), **indifferent** (does not care), **narrow-minded** (refuses every idea that is not their own). The clean pair to remember: **open-minded** ↔ **narrow-minded**.
- **Telling how it started: the past simple and its markers** — **ago** comes **after** the length of time and counts back from today (_two months ago_, never "ago two months"), **at that time** points back to the moment just named and is the opposite of _now_, **then** means _at that moment_ or _after that_. **ago** only ever goes with the past simple ("I have met her two years ago" ✗).
- **be going to: the plan already made** — **am / is / are + going to + base verb** (_I'm going to open an account tonight._): the decision was taken **before** you spoke. It also predicts what you can already see coming (_Look at that sky — it's going to rain._). Never drop **be**: "I go to call him" ✗.
- **will: the decision born as you speak** — **will + base verb**, same for every subject, short form **'ll**, negative **won't**. Four jobs: a **decision** taken on the spot (_It's heavy — I'll carry it._), a **promise** (_Don't worry, I'll be there._), an **offer** (_I'll lend you my notes._), a **prediction** (_I think he will pass._). Never "will to", never two future forms at once ("I'll going to" ✗).
- **The four futures** — decision taken **before** → **be going to**; decision taken **now**, promise, offer → **will**; arrangement **fixed with somebody**, with a time and a place → **present continuous** (_I'm meeting Dorra at six._); **timetable** fixed by an organisation → **present simple** (_The train leaves at 7.15._).
- **Attitudes and feelings** — **to be weary** (worn out), **hopeless**, **depressed**, a **tear**, **to feel small** (ashamed, unimportant), **to comfort somebody**, **to take somebody's part** (defend them), **to ease somebody's mind** (calm it), a **bridge** (the image of the friend who carries you across), **to sail**, **to shine** (said of a person, to be at your best). When you comfort, English reaches for **will**: the promise is the comfort.
- **Naming the cause** — **because** + subject + verb, **since** and **as** + subject + verb (the reason is already known), **because of** + **noun**. "because of it was raining" ✗ → _because it was raining_ ✓ or _because of the rain_ ✓.
- **Naming the result** — **so** joins two clauses with a comma before it (_It was raining, so he stayed in._); **therefore** and **as a result** open a new sentence and take a comma after them (_It was raining. Therefore, he stayed in._); **that's why** opens the second half.
- **The two long forms** — **so + adjective alone + that** (_The queue was so long that he gave up._) and **such + a + adjective + noun + that** (_It was such a long queue that he gave up._). "so a long queue" ✗, "such long" ✗.
- **One linker per link** — "Because he was late, **so** he missed the bus" ✗ → _Because he was late, he missed the bus._ or _He was late, so he missed the bus._
- **Describing behaviour** — an **inbox**, a **smiley**, **concise** (much in few words), **discreet**, **anonymity**, **appeal**, **hooked** (addicted), **needy**, **desperate**, **shy**, **to confess**, a **conflict**, **to handle** (deal with without losing control).
- **Register: text or e-mail?** — to a friend, contractions, shortcuts and smileys are normal; to a teacher, a company or an office, an e-mail keeps **Dear Sir or Madam**, full forms (_I am_, _cannot_, _will not_), complete sentences, a short precise subject line, **Yours sincerely**, and never a smiley. Same news, same writer, two languages.
- **Reading a shortcut** — a letter stands for the word it sounds like (**U** = you, **R** = are, **C** = see); a figure for the sound it makes (**2** = to, **4** = for, **8** = _-ate_, so **L8R** = later, **GR8** = great); the vowels are dropped (**PLS**, **THX**, **MSG**). _CU 2MORO B4 8_ = See you tomorrow before eight.
