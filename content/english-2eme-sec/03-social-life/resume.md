# 📜 Summary: Social Life

- **The kinds of journey** — **a flight** (by plane), **a voyage** (long, by sea), **an excursion** (short, for pleasure, in a group), **a trip** (short, there and back), **a journey** (the movement itself), and **travel**, the general idea, which is **uncountable** ("a travel" ✗ → _a trip_). You **catch** a flight, **go on** an excursion, **make** a journey.
- **The words of the leaflet** — **a brochure** (a booklet with photos and prices), **a leaflet** (a single folded sheet), **galleries** (the rooms of a museum), **jewellery** (rings, bracelets, necklaces), **to broaden** (to make wider — _travel broadens the mind_), **to amaze** (to surprise so much that you cannot react).
- **Saying what is possible** — **can / could / may / might** + base verb, _It is possible to…_ **Giving advice** — _You **should** book early._, _**Why not** take the night train?_ (no _to_), _**How about** visiting the souk?_ (+ _-ing_), _If I were you, I'd…_, _It's worth …-ing._
- **either** — the negative twin of **too**, at the **end** of the sentence: _Hedi enjoys flying and Emna does **too**._ / _Hedi doesn't enjoy flying and Emna doesn't **either**._ Rewriting: _Neither Salma nor Amine phoned the hostel._ → _Salma didn't phone the hostel, and Amine didn't phone it **either**._ ; _The little station has no cafe and no waiting room._ → _It hasn't got a cafe, and it hasn't got a waiting room **either**._
- **Short replies** — affirmative: _**So do I.** / **So have I.**_ ; negative: _**Neither do I.** / **Neither have I.**_ — after _neither_ the auxiliary comes **before** the subject. Traps: "I don't like it too" ✗, "I don't like it neither" ✗, "Neither I do" ✗.
- **Satisfaction** — asking: _How do you feel **about**…? Are you **happy with**…? Are you **satisfied with**…? What do you **think of**…?_ ; answering: _I'm **delighted with** it. I'm **pleased with** it. I'm **grateful for** their welcome._ ; dissatisfaction: _I'm **not satisfied with** it. I'm **disappointed with** the result. I'm **fed up with** the travelling._ And **to look forward to** + noun or **-ing** ("to meet" ✗).
- **The words of the interview** — **a league**, **teammates**, **a coach**, **a career**, **to cheer**, **to miss somebody**, **to look forward to**, **grateful**, **eager**.
- **The present perfect progressive: form** — **have / has + been + verb-ing**: _I have been training_, _he has been training_, _I haven't been training_, _Have you been training?_ _been_ never disappears ("I have playing" ✗) and the _-ing_ never disappears ("I have been play" ✗).
- **What it expresses** — an activity that **started** in the past, has gone on **without stopping** and is **still going on**: _Wassim has been revising since seven o'clock._ Markers: **since** + a starting point (_since 2019_, _since he signed the contract_), **for** + a length (_for twelve years_). Question: **How long have you been…?** ("How long time" ✗, "since seven years" ✗).
- **Progressive or simple?** — the progressive points at the **activity and its length** (_She has been writing letters all afternoon._ → **How long?**), the simple points at the **result and its number** (_She has written four letters._ → **How many?**). A count forbids the progressive; a duration forbids nothing (_I have lived here for ten years_ ✓ = _I have been living here for ten years_ ✓). Often together: _Sami has been driving the coach for nine years, and he has carried thousands of passengers._ **State verbs** (be, have, know, like, want, believe, belong) refuse the progressive: _I have known him since 2018._ ✓ And no present perfect ever takes a date: _I moved here in 2020._ / _I have been living here since 2020._
- **Word stress** — one strong syllable per word: ca-**REER**, ex-**CUR**-sion, mu-**SE**-um, **TEAM**-mates, **GRATE**-ful, **EA**-ger, op-por-**TU**-ni-ty, ex-pec-**TA**-tions. Two-syllable nouns and adjectives → first syllable (_career_ is the exception); **-tion / -sion** → nearly always the syllable just before (_**TE**-le-vi-sion_ is the famous escape); **-ity** → two before; compound nouns → the first part; **-ful, -ly, -ness, -er** never move the beat.
- **Progress Check 1 reflexes** — **word formation** (broad → to broaden / breadth / broadly; secure → security; person → personal; know → knowledge): a base verb after a modal, a noun after _the_ or _my_, an adjective before a noun. **Punctuation and capitals**: capital at the start, for proper nouns and for _I_; comma after an opening clause and before the words somebody actually said (_Mouna asked, "Where…?"_); a question mark that belongs to quoted words stays inside the quotation marks. **Linkers**: _and / moreover / in addition_ (same direction), _but / however / although_ (opposition), _so / therefore_ (consequence), _because_ + a whole clause and _because of_ + a noun (reason) — and do not read the _since_ of this chapter as a reason: next to a present perfect it marks a starting point.
