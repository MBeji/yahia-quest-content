# 📜 Summary: Education

- **The words of the uniform debate** — **a uniform** (the clothes everybody in one school must wear), **to distinguish** (recognise the difference), **smart** (tidy and well dressed, *not* clever), **individuality** (what makes you yourself), **to suppress** (put an end to), **to conform to** (follow a rule), **a community**, **competition**, **an identity**. ⚠️ **smart** is about clothes here, never about intelligence.
- **Five pupils, three positions** — **Rachel** *for* (no decision to make each morning, no competition between the girls) · **Anita** *for* (it distinguishes the school, makes a community, and people say they look smart) · **Sue** *for* (you do not lose your individuality by dressing like everyone else) · **Katie** *against* (the same uniform suppresses identities) · **Sarah** **both** (proud of hers, and still sees faults in it). ⭐ Sarah is the one to remember: admitting the other side is what makes the rest believable. The text is **argumentative** — every speaker defends a position, and you can tell from the openings alone. *(Source: Linguapress.)*
- **Saying what you think** — *I think that…* · *I believe that…* · *In my opinion,…* · *I agree that…* · *I disagree that…* · *I don't think so.* ⚠️ *I think so* / *I don't think so* stand alone as a whole answer; add a clause and you drop *so*.
- **Reflexive pronouns — the nine forms** — I → **myself** · You *(one)* → **yourself** · He → **himself** · She → **herself** · It → **itself** · One → **oneself** · We → **ourselves** · You *(several)* → **yourselves** · They → **themselves**. Used when the doer and the receiver are the same person (*I cut myself*), and placed straight after the verb, where the object would go. ⭐ **`You` appears twice** because English has one *you* but **two** reflexives — the only place where singular and plural *you* are visibly different. ⚠️ Singular ends in **-self**, plural in **-selves** (never "ourself", never "themself"); and it is **himself** (not "hisself") and **themselves** (not "theirselves"). ⚠️ The reflexive agrees with **its own subject**: — *Did you enjoy **yourselves**?* — *Yes, we enjoyed **ourselves**.* ⚠️ **themselves** = each looked at his own reflection; **each other** = they exchanged looks.
- **The examination room — seven situations** — **Paul** has no time left to check → *always leave enough time to check* · **Jane** revised what she already knew → *use your revision plan, spend the time on what you are unsure of* · **Mary** finds it easy and won't bother checking → *use all the time available, go back and check everything* · **Beth** is ten minutes into one question → *don't waste time on one question, come back later* · **Andy**'s mind has gone blank → *keep calm, there's no need to panic* · **Barbara** started last week and could not sleep → *don't revise at the last minute, get a good night's sleep* · **Steve** has a whole section unread → *plan your time, look through the whole paper first*. ⭐ **The trap**: Paul and Mary both talk about checking — Paul has **no time left**, Mary has **time to spare** and wastes it.
- **The words of examinations** — **to sit / to take an exam** (to do it) against **to pass** (to succeed) and **to fail** · **to cope with** (manage something difficult) · **to check** · **blank** (*my mind's gone blank*) · **to revise** / **revision** (uncountable). ⚠️ **to pass an exam** means **to succeed**, never merely *passer un examen* — for that, say *sit* or *take*.
- **Saying that you are afraid — the second function of Lesson 19** — the guide gives the lesson **two** functions, advising **and expressing fear and worry**: **I'm afraid of** + noun or **-ing** · **I'm afraid (that)** + clause · **I'm worried about** / **I'm anxious about** + noun or -ing · **What if…?** + clause · **I can't stop thinking about…**. ⚠️ **`afraid of` and `afraid that` are not interchangeable**: *of* takes a noun or an -ing (*afraid of failing*), *that* takes a subject and a verb (*afraid that I will fail*). ⚠️ And **`I'm afraid`** alone is not fear but a polite way of announcing bad news (*I'm afraid the library is closed*). To reassure: *There's **no need to** panic* · *Don't **worry*** · *You'll **be fine*** · ***Keep calm***.
- **Advice: `had better`, `ought to`, `should`** — all followed by the **base verb**; only *ought* keeps **to**. *You **had better** revise the last chapter* (urgent — there is a bad consequence if you don't) · *You **ought to** read the whole test once first* (it is the right thing) · *You **should** plan your time* (ordinary advice). ⚠️ **had better** is written with **had** for every person, usually shortened to **'d better** — "you better" ✗. Negative: **had better not** + base verb, never "hadn't better".
- **Lack of necessity: `needn't`** — *You **needn't** turn off the computer; it switches off automatically.* ⚠️⚠️ **`needn't` is not the negative of `must`.** *You **mustn't** open the door* = it is **forbidden**; *You **needn't** open the door* = it is **not necessary**. Opposites, not neighbours.
- **Regret and reproach: `should have` / `shouldn't have`** + past participle — *I know I **should've** studied last night, but I watched TV instead* (**regret** — blaming myself) · *You **shouldn't have** left it so late* (**reproach** — blaming somebody else). The only difference is **who is blamed**; both describe something that can no longer be repaired.
- **Conclusion: `must`** + base verb — *Mr Bean looks anxious. He **must** be worried about his sick mother.* Nobody obliges him: *must* here measures **how sure the speaker is**. Beside it, *could* = it is possible, *couldn't* = I am sure it is not.
- **Offering: `will`** — *I **will** be glad to help you any time you ask.* A decision taken **as you speak**: an offer, a promise, an instant reaction. ⚠️ The teacher's key files it under "future", but the table has no such column — it belongs under **offering help**, and then the six columns are all used exactly once each except *advice*, which is used twice.
- **The one-line test** — telling someone what to do now → *should / ought to / had better* · saying it is pointless → *needn't* · saying it is forbidden → *mustn't* · sorry about **my own** past → *should have* · blaming **somebody else's** past → *shouldn't have* · concluding from evidence → *must* · offering → *will*.
- **Writing as a process — the last two steps** — gather · organise · **first draft** (Lesson 18), then **exchange it with a classmate** for proofreading · **final draft** (Lesson 19), reread and hand in. ⭐ *Exchange*, not *show*: a reader who must write comments sees what the writer cannot, because the writer already knows what he meant. Keep the corrected copy in your file.
- ⚠️ **One activity has no written key anywhere** — the pronunciation exercise of page 102 turns entirely on which sounds are **underlined**. Do it aloud with your teacher; no book, not even the teacher's guide, answers it.
