# 📜 Summary: Education

- **The words of the theme** — **a uniform** · **to conform to** a rule (to follow it) · **to distinguish** (to see or to show the difference; *this badge distinguishes us from the others* = sets us apart) · **smart** = **tidy and well dressed**, not "clever" · **to suppress** (to put an end to) · **individuality** (what makes one person different from every other) · **to cope with** (to deal with successfully) · **to check** (to look at your work again) · **to fail** (not to pass) · **blank** (*my mind went blank* = for a moment I remembered nothing) · **to revise / revision** · **to panic**. Four fixed collocations: _cope **with**_ · _conform **to**_ · _**fail** an exam_ (no preposition before an exam; *fail in* exists, but only before an attempt or a duty) · _**check** your answers_.
- **The nine reflexive pronouns** — myself · yourself · himself · herself · itself · oneself · ourselves · yourselves · themselves. Singular in **-self**, plural in **-selves**; **you** alone has two forms, *yourself* for one person and *yourselves* for a group. No such words as "theirselves", "hisself", "himselfs".
- **The obligatory reflexive** — the doer and the receiver are the same person, and the reflexive fills the **object** place: _Hedi burnt **himself**_ · _Emna blamed **herself**_ · _the boys behaved **themselves**_. Test: *who receives the action?* If the answer is the subject, the reflexive is compulsory. _Emna made **her** a cup of tea_ = for somebody else; _Emna made **herself** a cup of tea_ = for her own use.
- **The emphatic reflexive** — it says *this person and nobody else*, and it can be **crossed out**: _The club members built the shelves **themselves**._ → *The club members built the shelves.* Still a full, true sentence — but the insistence goes out with the pronoun, so the two versions do **not** say exactly the same thing. That removal test tells the two uses apart: if what is left has a hole in it, the reflexive was obligatory.
- **by + reflexive** — _she revised **by herself**_ = with nobody's help. Not the same as the emphatic reflexive, which insists on *who*, not on *alone*.
- **each other against themselves** — _they congratulated **each other**_ = the first congratulated the second and the second the first, the action crosses. _They congratulated **themselves**_ = each one was pleased with his own result, two separate actions.
- **No reflexive at all in English** — _I **feel** tired_ · _she **concentrated** on the question_ · _**hurry up**_ · _we **relaxed**_ · _they **met** at the library_ · _I **woke up** at five_. Adding a pronoun to these is a calque from your own language.
- **The four modals of advice** — **should** + base verb (ordinary advice) · **ought to** + **to** + base verb (advice as a duty) · **had better** + base verb (urgent advice, with a bad result hanging over it) · **needn't** + base verb (not necessary at all). Contracted: _you'd better_. Negatives: **had better not** + base verb, **ought not to**.
- **Forms that cost marks** — "had better to revise" ✗ · "hadn't better waste" ✗ → _had better **not** waste_ · "ought check" ✗ → _ought **to** check_ · "needn't to come" ✗ → _needn't **come**_.
- **needn't is not mustn't** — _you **needn't** turn it off_ (no need, do as you like) against _you **mustn't** turn it off_ (forbidden). And **had better** warns, **ought to** states a duty. (The grammar summary at the back of the book files all three under plain **Advice**: that is short, not wrong — only *had better* names what goes wrong if you ignore it.)
- **The two other values the page 107 table asks for** — **must** for a **deduction**, where nobody is being ordered and the speaker is concluding from what can be seen: _her chair has been empty all week; she **must be** ill_ · past form **must have** + past participle: _there is chalk on his sleeve; he **must have been** at the board_. Careful: _the form **must be** signed by a parent_ is an order, with the same two words. And **will** / **can** with *I* or *we* for **offering help**: _I **will** carry those files down for you_ · _I **can** show you where the past papers are kept_ — neither a promise about the future nor a boast about your abilities.
- **should have / shouldn't have + past participle** — the duty that was not done. Same form, two values decided by the subject: _**You** should have started in April_ = **reproach**; _**I** should have started in April_ = **regret**. _You **shouldn't have** stayed up all night_ blames a night that is over, while _you **shouldn't** stay up all night_ advises about the next one. A **third** value hides in the same three words: _Rania posted the form ten days ago, so the club **should have** written back already_ blames nobody — it says what you **expect** to be true, counting the days. Never "should of", never "should have revise" — after *have* comes the **past participle**.
- **Opinion, and fear** — _I think that…_ · _In my opinion,…_ · _As far as I am concerned,…_ · _I agree / I disagree that…_ · _I don't think so._ · _I see what you mean, but…_ — and _I am **worried about**…_ · _I am **afraid (that)**…_ · _I am **anxious about**…_ · _What **if** I forget everything?_ · _Take it one question at a time._ ("worried for" ✗, "afraid from" ✗.)
- **Reading a school notice** — three forces, one paragraph. **Forbidden**: _must not_, _are not allowed_. **Advised**: _are advised to_, _should_, _ought to_, _had better_. **Not necessary**: _need not_, _do not have to_, _there is no need to_. Read the verb, not the tone.
- **For and against** — an argument attacks the **rule**, never the person. A **concession** admits one good point to the other side before continuing: _It is true that a uniform saves time, **but**…_ Its openings: _It is true that…_ · _Nobody denies that…_ · _Of course,…_ · _Admittedly,…_ · _I agree that…_ ; the return to the writer's own side comes with **but**, **yet**, **still** or **however**, so the linker marks the return and never the concession itself. A concession is the sentence that does **not** follow the writer's conclusion, which is how you find out where that writer really stands.
