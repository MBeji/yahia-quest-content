# 📜 Summary: Science and Technology

- **The words of the net** — **online** (connected to the net), **to surf the net** (to move from page to page with no fixed aim), **cyberspace**, **to click** / **a click**, **an addict** / **an addiction**, **preoccupied** (unable to stop thinking about one thing), **to conceal** (to hide on purpose), **to relieve** (to take away a pain or a worry), **irrational** (supported by no reason), **aggressive**, **to break up**, **to survive**, **an escape key**. **break up** changes sense with what stands round it: two people **break up** (their relationship ends), a meeting **breaks up** (it ends and everybody leaves), a signal **breaks up** (it becomes unclear), a crate is **broken up** (smashed into pieces).
- **Direct and reported** — direct speech gives the exact words between inverted commas; reported speech gives the sense, in your own sentence, with no inverted commas. **that** may stay or go. Four families change: the **verb**, the **pronouns**, the **demonstratives**, the **words of time and place**.
- **The first question: what tense is the reporting verb in?** — **present, present perfect or future → nothing changes** (_He **says** the bus **will** be late._). **Past → the verb steps back one tense.**
- **The step-back table** — simple present → simple past · present continuous → past continuous · present perfect → past perfect · present perfect continuous → past perfect continuous · simple past → past perfect · **will** → **would** · **can** → **could** · **may** → **might**. _"I never eat meat," he explained._ → _He explained that he **never ate** meat._ · _"I bought the parts in Sfax."_ → _He said he **had bought** the parts in Sfax._ The **past perfect** is a tool of the report here, not a tense to study for itself.
- **What does not move** — the modals **would, should, ought to, had better, might, used to, could, must** (_She said she **might** come back._) · a past tense describing something **still true** (_because it **is** near the station_) · a verb inside a clause opening with **when** or **while** · the verbs of **if I were**, **I wish**, **I'd rather**.
- **Pronouns and possessives** — first and second person move to the **third** (_He said **he** had lost **his** card._ · _He told Emna that **she** had forgotten the key._). **One exception: report your own words and they stay in the first person** — _I said that **I** liked **my** new bike_, never _she liked her new bike_.
- **this and these** — **this** in a time expression becomes **that** (_that week_); **this** and **these** before a noun become **the** (_He said he had bought **the** cables._).
- **Time and place** — now → then · today → that day · tonight → that night · tomorrow → the next day · yesterday → the day before · last week → the week before · next week → the following week · ago → before · **here → there**. **Not automatic**: they change only because the report is made later or somewhere else. Ask the one question — would the word still point at the right moment and the right place? If yes, keep it.
- **say or tell** — **tell** takes the person straight after it (_He **told me** that…_); **say** never does, and needs **to** if you name the person (_He **said** that…_ / _He **said to me** that…_). ✗ _He said me…_ · ✗ _He told to me…_ · ✗ _He told that…_
- **Reporting a question** — it stops being a question: no question mark, **no inversion**, no **do / does / did**. A **wh-** question keeps its question word (_She asked where **I worked**._); a **yes/no** question takes **if** or **whether** (_He asked **if I was** ready._). **ask** takes the person without **to**. ✗ _She asked where did I work._ · ✗ _He asked was I ready._ · ✗ _He asked that I was ready._
- **A change that goes on** — two comparatives joined by **and** = gradual increase or decrease. **Long adjective**: _more and more useful_, _less and less expensive_. **Short adjective**: the **-er** comparative repeated — _shorter and shorter_, _longer and longer_. ✗ _more expensive and expensive_ · ✗ _longer and long_ · ✗ _lesser and lesser_. Do not confuse it with the plain comparative: **more and more expensive** = the price keeps climbing; **much more expensive** = it is high today, and nothing is said about a change.
- **The words of the future human being** — **futuristic** (looking as if it belonged to the future), **to assume** (to take as true without proof), **obvious** (so clear nobody needs telling), **substantial** (large in size or amount), **brains**, **emotions**, **bald**. **assume** is what you do without evidence; **substantial** always means large.
- **Opinion, agreement, disagreement** — _In my opinion…_ · _As far as I am concerned…_ · _I quite agree._ · _You have a point there._ · _I am afraid I disagree._ · _I see what you mean, but…_ · _That is going a little too far._ The strongest move is the **partial** one: keep what is true in the other person's sentence and refuse only what is excessive.
- **Reading a self-test** — a questionnaire that sets its own line at five _yes_ answers says **nothing** below that line, and no questionnaire replaces a doctor. Saying what a document does **not** allow you to conclude is part of reading it.
- **Step 4 of the career project** — a presentation plus a written report: description of the work · wage at entry and later · qualities the job calls for · education needed · where it is given · advantages and disadvantages · career ladder · outlook. No marking scheme is printed; those eight points are the whole of it.
