# 📜 Summary: Science and Technology

- **The words of a life online** — **online addiction**, **to surf the net**, **cyberspace**, **to chat** (online: to exchange typed messages in real time), **a chat room**, **the escape key**, **to survive**, **depression**, **irrational**, **to break up**, **to conceal** (to hide), **to acknowledge** (to admit something is true). ⚠️ **to chat** has two live meanings — talking lightly face to face, and typing in real time; this lesson uses the second.
- **Four readers** — **Ian** *(against)*: he can live without it, his **wife** cannot — five hours from the moment she gets home, three chat rooms at once, and a marriage suffering for it; his real question is how to make somebody **acknowledge** a problem when raising it makes them defensive. **Sam** *(for)*: seven hours at a screen is no worse than seven at a television, and better, since television offers **no interactivity**. **Janet** *(for)*: hours on the telephone make her sociable, the same hours online make her a geek — she calls that an **irrational prejudice** and asks that communication be valued in all its forms. **Manel Ali** ⚠️ *(not decidable)*: she met her fiancé online, her excessive love of the internet cost her the engagement, and she still defends the need that caused it. ⭐ Her message **tells a harm** and **defends a need** at once; a question that forces her into one column has not read her.
- ⚠️ **The self-test is not a diagnosis.** Seven questions ending in "if you answered yes to at least five, you are an addict" is a classroom prompt for discussion, never a medical instrument. Learn the **vocabulary** and the **argument**, not the verdict.
- **Reported speech — the three shifts** — **(1) the person**: I → he/she, my → his/her, we → they. **(2) the tense steps back** when the reporting verb is past: present simple → past simple · present progressive → past progressive · past simple → **past perfect** · present perfect → past perfect · will → would · can → could · must → had to. **(3) time and place**: now → then · today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · yesterday → the day before · here → there · this → that.
- ⭐ **Two things never move** — the **past perfect** stays as it is, and **would, could, should, might** stay as they are: once you are as far back as the language goes, there is nowhere left to step.
- ⚠️ **`say` and `tell`** — you **say** something, you **tell somebody** something. *She **said** that she was tired* / *She **told me** that she was tired*. "She said me" ✗ · "She told that…" ✗.
- ⚠️ **The backshift is not automatic** — if what was said is still true (a permanent fact, a rule), English keeps the present: *He said that water **boils** at a hundred degrees.* Backshift reports a moment; the present reports a truth.
- ⚠️ **The book's model sentence is faulty in one respect** — *one of those who met **her** fiancé*: the relative depends on *those*, which is plural, so correct English is *those who met **their** fiancés*. Use the sentence for the **tense change**, which is exactly right, and not for the agreement.
- **What will man be like (listening)** — **Dr Franklin Gruber**, of the department of futuristic studies at Durban University: man **changes slowly all the time**. **Taller** — men are about **three inches** taller than five hundred years ago, and five hundred years is a relatively short period. **A larger forehead** — we use only about **20 %** of the brain's capacity and shall use it **more and more**, so it becomes **more and more substantial**. **Stronger eyes** over a very long period, though today constant use weakens them into glasses. **Weaker arms and legs**, used **less and less**. **More sensitive fingers**, used a great deal. **No hair** — it serves no purpose, and both men and women are likely to be **bald**. ⭐ **And what does not change**: he will still be a human being, with **thoughts and emotions** similar to our own — a listening always asks about the exception.
- **The words of the future** — **futuristic**, **obvious**, **to assume** (to accept as true without proof, in order to reason from it), **substantial**, **bald**, **emotions**, **capacity**.
- **Gradual change — one thing moving steadily** — long adjective → **more and more** + adjective (increase) or **less and less** + adjective (decrease); short adjective → **-er and -er** (*the days are getting **longer and longer***); irregular → **better and better**, **worse and worse**. ⚠️ **The box on page 144 stops halfway**: it prints the line for *-er and -er* and leaves the meaning empty with no example. The meaning is **gradual increase**, exactly like *more and more* — only the length of the adjective differs. ⚠️ **"more and more taller"** ✗ — the ending already does the work.
- ⭐⭐ **The distinction that decides half the marks** — **`more and more`** describes **one** thing changing steadily (*the library is getting more and more crowded*); **`the more… the more…`** ties **two** changes to each other (*the closer the exams get, the more crowded the library becomes*). Your book puts *parallel change* — the name it gave the second structure in Lesson 23 — among the options as the trap. **Count the changes**: one → *more and more*; two → *the more… the more…*.
- **Word stress** — **1st syllable**: INternet, HEALTHier, MEDicine, NEWSpaper. **2nd**: adDICtion, reSEARCH, rePEATedly, supPORTed, scienTIFic. **3rd**: eduCAtion, technoloGICally, matheMATics. **4th**: environMENtal, communiCAtions, availaBILity, impossiBILity. ⭐ Endings in **-tion, -sion, -ic, -ity** put the stress on the syllable **immediately before** them; and adding a suffix often **moves** the stress (TECHnology → technoLOGical → technoloGICally). ⚠️ *research* is stressed on the second syllable in British English, often on the first in American — never build a question on it.
