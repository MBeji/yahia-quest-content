# ⚔️ Self Evaluation — Look Back to Level Up

> 💡 "A hero who never checks the map walks very fast in the wrong direction."

Five lessons are behind you. Before the next gate opens, you stop and ask two questions: what am I good at, and what is still hard? This chapter gives you the language to answer both — and a rarer power, the language to **help somebody else** with a suggestion.

## 🔁 The question builder — one formula, three helpers

You met the WH-words in the first lesson. Here they are **systematised**: one line per helper, and every question you need is built from it.

| You ask about                   | Formula                                     | Example                                                                |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| a state, a quality, an identity | **WH word + am/is/are/was/were + subject**  | _What **is** your strong point?_ · _Which lesson **was** the hardest?_ |
| a finished action               | **WH word + did + subject + base verb**     | _How **did** you learn that word?_                                     |
| a habit, a taste, an opinion    | **WH word + do/does + subject + base verb** | _Which activity **do** you enjoy most?_                                |

Your partner replies **yes** or **no** with a short answer. One rule: repeat the **helper of the question**, never the main verb.

| The question begins with | Positive              | Negative                |
| ------------------------ | --------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Do / Does…?              | _Yes, I **do**._      | _No, I **don't**._      |
| Did…?                    | _Yes, I **did**._     | _No, I **didn't**._     |
| Is / Are…?               | _Yes, they **are**._  | _No, they **aren't**._  |
| Was / Were…?             | _Yes, she **was**._   | _No, she **wasn't**._   |
| Have / Has…?             | _Yes, they **have**._ | _No, they **haven't**._ |

> ⚠️ A positive short answer is **never contracted**: _Yes, I **am**._ ✓ — "Yes, I'm." ✗ And the helper must match the question: _Were the texts long? — Yes, they **were**._ ✓ — "Yes, they did." ✗ When the question asks about **you**, the answer speaks for **I**, and _be_ follows the new subject: _**Were you** ready? — No, **I wasn't**._ · _**Are you** ready? — No, **I'm not**._ Only _be_ changes shape this way; _do_, _did_, _have_ and _has_ keep the form the question used.

## 🗣️ Saying what is easy and what is hard

A self-evaluation questionnaire asks you to tick boxes; talking about it asks you for phrases. Here are the six you need.

| To say…                 | Use                                                | Example                                       |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| how difficult it was    | **I found + the lesson + easy / fairly difficult** | _I **found** lesson 3 fairly difficult._      |
| how long a text was     | **too long / the right length / too short**        | _The second text was **too long** for me._    |
| how interesting it was  | **interesting / quite interesting / boring**       | _I found the topic **very interesting**._     |
| how much you understood | **everything / most of it / only a little**        | _I understood **most of it**._                |
| a strong point          | **I'm good at + noun or -ing verb**                | _I'm **good at** reading._                    |
| a difficulty            | **I'm not very good at + noun or -ing verb**       | _I'm **not very good at** speaking in class._ |

**too** is not _very_: it means _more than I want_ — _the text was very long_ states a fact, _the text was **too long**_ is a complaint. Then you and your partner agree on **one** common strong point and **one** common difficulty, and you report them to the class with **we** and **our**: _We **agree that** our strong point is grammar, and that our main difficulty is new vocabulary._

## 💡 Five ways to make a suggestion

Here is the real new power of the chapter. Your partner names a difficulty; you offer an idea. Five structures — and each one is followed by a precise form.

| Structure         | What follows it                             | Example                                                                        |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Why not…?**     | the **base verb**, without _to_             | _**Why not** read an English story every week?_                                |
| **What about…?**  | a **noun** or a verb in **-ing**            | _**What about** a vocabulary notebook?_ · _**What about** listening to songs?_ |
| **You should…**   | the **base verb**, without _to_             | _You **should** revise the words of every lesson._                             |
| **You could…**    | the **base verb**, without _to_             | _You **could** ask your partner to test you._                                  |
| **If you…, you…** | present tense after _if_, **will** after it | _**If you** read more, **you will** learn faster._                             |

They do not all carry the same force. **Why not…?** and **What about…?** are friendly offers: they are questions, they end with a **question mark**, and your partner may say no. **You should…** is the strongest — what you believe is right. **You could…** is softer, one possibility among several. **If you…, you…** convinces best, because it gives the idea **and** its result. Watch the five of them work on one problem: your partner has just said _My main difficulty is new vocabulary._

- _**Why not** write every new word in a notebook?_ (base verb after _why not_)
- _**What about** re-reading the sentence where the word appears?_ (-ing after _what about_)
- _You **should** revise the vocabulary in every lesson._ (base verb, no _to_)
- _You **could** listen to English songs and try to understand them._ (a possibility, not an order)
- _**If you** read more texts in English, **you will** meet the same words again._ (present, then _will_)

> 🗡️ The same idea only changes shape: _What about **listening** to songs?_ and _Why not **listen** to songs?_ say exactly the same thing. Choose the structure first, then give it the form it demands.

## 🎯 Resolutions with **will**

A resolution is a decision you take **now**, for the future. Its verb is **will + base verb**.

- Positive: _I **will** speak more English in class._ — in speech and in notes: _**I'll** be more active in group work._
- Negative: **will not** = **won't** + base verb: _I **won't** translate every single word._
- Nothing comes between **will** and the base verb: _She **will read** longer texts._ ✓ — "She will reads" ✗, "She will to read" ✗

Pick yours from the class list: _I'll review the vocabulary in every lesson. I'll re-read the sentences where new words appear. I'll listen to English songs and try to understand them. I'll review last year's vocabulary and grammar. I'll be more active in pair and group work. I'll speak more English in class. I'll read longer texts. I'll read more texts in English. I'll look up one English word a day on the Internet._ You may add resolutions of your own.

> 🗡️ _I **will** revise every evening_ and _I'm **going to** revise every evening_ are both correct. **will** fits a decision taken at this very moment, **going to** a plan you had already made. Do not lose sleep over the difference.

## 🎭 Two students, one review

- **Hedi:** Which lesson did you find the hardest?
- **Salma:** Lesson 2. The text was too long for me. Did you understand it?
- **Hedi:** Yes, I did — but I read it twice. What is your strong point?
- **Salma:** Grammar. My main difficulty is vocabulary. Have you got the same problem?
- **Hedi:** No, I haven't. Why not keep a notebook for new words?
- **Salma:** Good idea. What about testing each other every Friday?
- **Hedi:** If you do that, you will remember them much longer. I'll bring the first list.
- **Salma:** Then we agree: our strong point is grammar, and our main difficulty is vocabulary.

## 🔭 Planning ahead — your research mission

Lesson 10 will bring you a passage by **Ibn Khaldun**. Come prepared: ask your Arabic teacher, go to the school or public library, or search a website, and find the answers to these six questions — **this course gives you the questions, not the answers**.

_When **was** he born?_ · _What kind of education **did** he get?_ · _Where **did** he live mainly?_ · _What **did** he write about?_ · _What positions **did** he hold?_ · _What **is** his best known book?_

Read them again: every one of them comes out of the builder — **was/is + subject** for the first and the last, **did + subject + base verb** for the four in between. The question builder is not a school exercise; it is how you interrogate the world.

## ⚠️ The trap wall

| Wrong                                    | Right                                       | Why                                               |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| "What about to go to the library?"       | _What about **going** to the library?_      | after **what about**: a noun or an **-ing** verb  |
| "Why not to try a new method?"           | _**Why not try** a new method?_             | after **why not**: the base verb, never _to_      |
| "You should to revise more."             | _You **should revise** more._               | **should** and **could** take the base verb alone |
| "I'm good at to speak English."          | _I'm good at **speaking** English._         | after **at**: a noun or an **-ing** verb          |
| "If you will read more, you will learn." | _**If you read** more, you **will** learn._ | present after **if**, **will** in the other half  |

> 🏆 Check-point cleared. You can measure your own progress, say it out loud, and hand somebody else an idea that actually helps. Keep your resolutions close — the next gates are longer, and they will ask for them.
