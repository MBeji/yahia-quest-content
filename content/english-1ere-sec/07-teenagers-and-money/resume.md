# 📜 Summary: Teenagers and Money

- **The pocket-money questionnaire** — five questions: how often you get it, whether you would like more, what you spend it on, whether you share it with your brothers and sisters, and whether it is a **right** or a **favour**. Two judgements come out of it: an answer is **selfish** when it keeps everything for one person, and pocket money is a **right** if parents owe it to you, a **favour** if they give it when they can.
- **The interview** — Carol Chapel, 17, a singer still at school, said by the papers to have made **£20 million**: she calls herself "a mixture of both" (a girl and a naïve but ambitious businesswoman), calls show business **ruthless**, says Max is a childhood friend and not a boyfriend, does not mind not being skinny, gets **£80 a month**, can touch her fortune at **21** and is "not as rich as the press say", likes her all-girls school for the studies but not for mixing with boys, names her mother as the closest person to her, and her own house as her biggest extravagance.
- **Matching an answer to its question** — eleven questions, ten answers, so **one question is always left over**. Name the topic of the answer in one word, look for the question on that topic, then check the shape: a sum answers _How much…?_, a name answers _Who…?_, a date answers _When…?_
- **The words of the lesson** — **naïve** (inexperienced, simple minded), **ruthless** (cruel, pitiless), **close** (near, intimate), **looks** (physical appearance), **to matter** (to be of importance), **self-confident** (sure of yourself), **obsessed with** (thinking of one thing all the time), **an extravagance** (an expensive thing you allow yourself), **publicity** (what the press and the public say and write about somebody).
- **The trap wall** — an **extravagance** is a costly purchase, not a strange way of behaving; **publicity** is public attention, not the advertisements on television; **obsessed** keeps the preposition **with**; a question needs its auxiliary (_**How do you** like the album?_); and _feel_ stays in the present simple ("I am feeling that…" ✗).
- **Asking for an opinion, giving one** — ask with _What do you think of…?_ or **_How do you like…?_** (both need the auxiliary **do**, and the object comes straight after: _How do you like the interview?_); answer with _I **think** / **feel** / **believe** that…_ + a whole sentence, always in the present simple ("I am feeling that…" ✗).
- **The group report** — five or six pupils agree on three things they like and three they do not like, then write a report of **opinions with their reasons** (_We think that she has kept her feet on the ground, because…_), not a list of facts; once the group has agreed, it speaks with **we** and **our**.
- **IT'S YOUR TURN TO ASK THE QUESTIONS** — the new closing box: you write the oral test of the next lesson yourself, on the topic, on your classmates' ideas, on the vocabulary and on the grammar.
