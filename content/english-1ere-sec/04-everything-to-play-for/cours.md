# ⚔️ Everything to Play For — Your Own Life, in Your Own Words

> 💡 "Tell me the words you use every day, and I will tell you who you are."

For three lessons you have been asking other people questions. Now the spotlight turns round: **you** talk about yourself and your family. There is no new tense here and no new rule — this gate is won with **words**. English stores them in **families**, and a word learnt inside its family, and inside a sentence, is a word that comes back when you need it. When a match is still open and anybody can win, the British say there is **everything to play for**: that is exactly where you stand.

## 🏠 The first two fields — FAMILY and FOOD

A word field is a **family of words about one subject**. Never learn a word alone: learn the sentence around it, and when you meet a new word, ask which of the four families it joins.

| Field  | Word             | The word inside a sentence                                                         |
| ------ | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| FAMILY | **grandparents** | _My **grandparents** live in a small village near Sfax._                           |
| FAMILY | **grandfather**  | _My **grandfather** grows his own vegetables behind the house._                    |
| FAMILY | **mum**          | _My **mum** works in a chemist's shop._ (British, informal — _mother_ in a letter) |
| FAMILY | **dad**          | _My **dad** drives me to school every morning._ (informal for _father_)            |
| FAMILY | **cousin**       | _My **cousin** Amine is exactly my age._ (a boy or a girl — the same word)         |
| FAMILY | **relatives**    | _All our **relatives** meet at my grandparents' house on Sundays._                 |
| FOOD   | **vegetables**   | _I eat **vegetables** every day, but I hate cabbage._                              |
| FOOD   | **carrots**      | _My grandmother puts **carrots** in almost every dish._                            |
| FOOD   | **recipe**       | _Her cakes come from her mother's secret **recipe**._                              |
| FOOD   | **fruit**        | _I love **fruit**, especially in summer._                                          |
| FOOD   | **grapes**       | _We pick **grapes** in September._                                                 |
| FOOD   | **cooking**      | _**Cooking** with my mum is my favourite moment of the week._                      |

## 🎭 Character adjectives — never without their proof

Saying what somebody is like takes two moves: the **adjective**, then the **fact** that proves it.

| Adjective    | What it says about a person                             | The proof that makes it true                                             |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **sociable** | enjoys being with other people                          | _She is **sociable**: she joins every group at break time._              |
| **kind**     | thinks of other people and helps them                   | _He is **kind**: he carries his neighbour's bags every Saturday._        |
| **bright**   | clever, quick to understand                             | _She is **bright**: she understands a new rule after one example._       |
| **dull**     | boring, with nothing lively to say                      | _He is **dull**: he answers every question with one word._               |
| **childish** | behaves like a small child at an age when he should not | _He is **childish**: he sulks for an hour when he loses a game._         |
| **naughty**  | badly behaved — said of a small child                   | _The little boy is **naughty**: he hides his sister's shoes and laughs._ |

Answer in two moves, **yes or no** and then the **fact**: _Is your friend kind? — **Yes, she is kind: she cooks for her grandparents.**_ · _Is she dull? — **No, she is not dull: she plays the violin, she reads, she looks after two kittens.**_

Then compare her with yourself: _We are **alike**: we are both sociable._ · _I am **like** her: I read every evening too._ · _I am **different from** her: she loves cooking, and I never go into the kitchen._

> ⚠️ A second adjective is not a proof: "She is kind because she is nice" proves nothing. Only a **fact** proves an adjective. And **bright** and **dull** are opposites, but only **dull** keeps this meaning for a thing (_a dull book_ = a boring book). And an opinion is not a fact either: "everybody says so" proves nothing.

## 📖 A model paragraph — the four fields in one Sunday

Leila is **invented for this lesson**. Read her paragraph: it has no title yet.

> _My grandparents live in a small village near Sfax, and on Sundays all our relatives meet there. My grandfather grows vegetables in his garden, and my mum cooks his mother's old recipe with carrots and chicken. My dad brings fruit — grapes, always grapes. After lunch my cousin and I play games in the yard, or I take out my violin and play a little music for my grandmother. Our dog sits under the table and waits, and the cat watches the birds in the lemon tree._

Every single sentence carries at least one word from the four fields. That density is what makes a paragraph feel full — and it is what yours will have to reach.

## 🏷️ Giving a paragraph its title

You already know that a title carries the **main idea**, never one detail. What is new is the **closed list**: your book gives eight titles and you pick the one that fits.

_Cooking · Learning and playing music · Leisure activities · My favourite music · Pets · What I think of some jobs · The food I like · My grandparents._

- Leila's paragraph is **My grandparents**: their house is the place, and every other fact — the recipe, the games, the cat — happens during that visit.
- **Cooking** and **The food I like** cover one sentence each, and **Pets** only the last one. A detail is not a main idea.
- **Leisure activities** is the opposite mistake: so wide that it would fit almost any paragraph.

> 🗡️ Separate the near-twins before you choose. **Cooking** (you are in the kitchen) ≠ **The food I like** (you are at the table) · **Learning and playing music** (you are the musician) ≠ **My favourite music** (you are the listener) · **Pets** (the animals of the house) ≠ **Leisure activities** (everything you do in your free time).

When every paragraph has its title, the class agrees on **one title for the whole text**. It must cover all the paragraphs at once, so it is broader than any of the eight: _A page from my life_, _All about me_.

## ✍️ Your turn — a similar paragraph about you

Choose one of the eight titles and build your paragraph in four moves — Leila's four moves. Same shape, **your** facts: a similar paragraph is not a copied one.

1. **Name the topic in the first sentence**: _My grandparents live in a small village near Sfax._
2. **Add two or three facts** using the words of the field: _grows vegetables_, _cooks a recipe_, _brings grapes_.
3. **Give one detail nobody else can give**: a first name, a street, a Sunday habit, the name of your cat.
4. **Close with what you feel or want**: _I hope we go there every Sunday next year too._

## ⚠️ The trap wall — when French speaks first

| Wrong                                       | Right                                                | Why                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "All my parents come on Sunday."            | _All my **relatives** come on Sunday._               | **parents** = your mother and your father, nobody else.                    |
| "She is sensible: she cries at every film." | _She is **sensitive**._                              | English **sensible** means _reasonable_ (_a sensible decision_).           |
| "My cousin is very sympathetic."            | _My cousin is very **nice** / **friendly**._         | **sympathetic** = sorry for someone who suffers, not _likeable_.           |
| "Actually my cousin lives in Tunis."        | _**At present** my cousin lives in Tunis._           | **actually** means _in fact_: _Actually, I hate cabbage._                  |
| "I play violin and I play the football."    | _I play **the** violin and I play football._         | **play + the +** instrument · **play +** sport, with **no** article.       |
| "I eat many fruits and the foods are good." | _I eat a lot of **fruit** and the **food** is good._ | **fruit**, **food** and **music** are uncountable: no _-s_, singular verb. |

> 🏆 Gate cleared, hero. You can sort a word into its field, praise or criticise somebody and **prove** it with a fact, give a paragraph its exact title and write your own page. The next texts will hand you no word list — you carry your own now.
