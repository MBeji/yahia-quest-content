# 📜 Summary: Everything to Play For

- **FAMILY and FOOD** — a word field is a family of words about one subject, learnt inside a sentence: **grandparents**, **grandfather**, **mum** and **dad** (British, informal), **cousin** (a boy or a girl), **relatives** (the wide family: uncles, aunts, cousins, grandparents) · **vegetables**, **carrots**, **recipe**, **fruit**, **grapes**, **cooking**.
- **LEISURE and ANIMALS** — **play** (_play the violin_, _play football_), **games**, **reading**, **instrument**, **music**, **cards** · **pet**, **dog**, **cat**, **bird**, **elephant**, **mouse** (plural **mice**).
- **Character adjectives, never without their proof** — **sociable** (enjoys other people), **kind** (helps others), **bright** (quick to understand), **dull** (boring, nothing lively to say), **childish** (behaves like a small child when he should not), **naughty** (a badly behaved small child). Answer in two moves, yes or no then the fact: _Yes, she is kind: she cooks for her grandparents._ — a second adjective is never a proof. Then compare: _We are alike_ · _I am like her_ · _I am different from her_.
- **The model paragraph** — Leila's Sunday at her grandparents' puts all four fields to work, and every sentence carries at least one word of a field: that density is the target for your own paragraph.
- **Giving a paragraph its title** — the title carries the main idea, never one detail, and here it comes from a closed list of eight (Cooking · Learning and playing music · Leisure activities · My favourite music · Pets · What I think of some jobs · The food I like · My grandparents). Leila's paragraph is **My grandparents**; _Cooking_, _The food I like_ and _Pets_ are true of one sentence only, _Leisure activities_ is too wide. Separate the near-twins: Cooking ≠ The food I like, Learning and playing music ≠ My favourite music, Pets ≠ Leisure activities. The whole text then gets one broader title, wider than any of the eight (_A page from my life_, _All about me_).
- **Writing your own paragraph** — four moves: name the topic in the first sentence, add two or three facts with the words of the field, give one detail nobody else can give, close with what you feel or want. Same shape, your facts.
- **The trap wall** — _relatives_ (not "all my parents") · _sensitive_ for feelings, **sensible** means _reasonable_ · **sympathetic** = sorry for someone who suffers, so say _nice_ or _friendly_ · **actually** = _in fact_, use _at present_ for the time · _play **the** violin_ but _play football_ · **fruit**, **food** and **music** are uncountable: no _-s_, singular verb.
