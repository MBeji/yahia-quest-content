# 📜 Summary: Queen of Soul

- **The portrait** — a biographical text mixes the three tenses in one paragraph, and each one does a different job: who she is today, what happened at a past date, what she has done so far.
- **Choosing the tense** — three questions, in this order: still true today → **present simple** (_she lives_, _she is_); finished event with a past time → **simple past** (_she was born in 1994_, _she recorded her first album in 2015_); result or total up to now with no date → **present perfect** (_she has released_, _she has toured_). A finished past date kills the present perfect ("has released… in 2015" ✗).
- **The present perfect as a career report** — an unfinished career takes the present perfect: a total (_she has released four albums_), an experience she never had (_she has never given an interview_), a result that still counts (_she has already said everything_, _she hasn't written her memoirs yet_), a period that continues (**since** + starting point, **for** + length of time; never "since ten years").
- **Opinion phrases** — ask: _What do you think of…?_, _How do you feel about…?_ (and _Would you like…?_ for a **wish**, not an opinion); give: _I think / believe (that)…_, _In my opinion,…_, _I'm (not) sure / certain that…_; agree: _I agree with you_, _That's true_, _You're right_; disagree politely: _I don't agree_, _I don't think so_, _I'm afraid I can't agree_. **agree** is a verb ("I am agree" ✗), _that_ is optional after _think_, and _I don't think she is right_ beats "I think she is not right".
- **The identity card** — scan a portrait for name, real name, place of birth, place of residence, marital status, occupation and major records, and answer with a short phrase; residence hides behind the verb _live_, marital status behind _husband / wife / single_.
- **Prove it, then name it** — a true/false answer carries a justification **copied from the text**, never a personal reason; a good title gives the main idea of the whole text — not a detail ("Nour's Grandmother" ✗), not a vague word ("Music" ✗), not a full sentence — and you read it again once the other questions are answered.
- **Read between the lines** — a pronoun never names a person the same sentence already names, and what a character **risks or refuses** says more than what they claim about themselves; both readings must still rest on a line of the text.
- **Word power** — a successful career, a large fortune, becoming famous, achieving an ambition, starting a business, earning a lot of money, university studies, a brilliant future; real name, place of residence, marital status, occupation, major records, fame, publicity, the press. You **earn** money by working but **win** a prize or a match, and _fame_ and _publicity_ are uncountable.
