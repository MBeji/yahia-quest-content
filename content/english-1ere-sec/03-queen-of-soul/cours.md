# ⚔️ Queen of Soul — The Story Behind the Fame

> 💡 "Anyone can list the facts of a life. A hero knows which tense to tell them in — and dares to say what they think of it."

You already know how to **build** the three tenses of this level: the present simple, the simple past and the present perfect. That was the forge. This gate is harder, because in a **real text** nobody tells you which one to use — you choose, verb by verb. Then comes the second weapon: saying **what you think of the story**, and answering when somebody asks you.

## 📖 The portrait — one life, three tenses

Read this short portrait. The singer is **invented for this lesson**: every fact below belongs to her and to nobody else.

> **The Voice from the Old Town**
>
> Everybody calls her Nour, but her real name is Nour Belhaj Amara. She was born in Bizerte in 1994, and she now lives in Sousse with her husband and their two sons. She is a singer and a songwriter. She sang her first songs with her grandmother, and she recorded her first album, _Long Nights_, in a friend's garage in 2015. Since then she has released three more records and has toured in six countries. She has never given a television interview. "I have already said everything in my songs," she says.

## 🧭 Choosing the tense in a life story

Before every verb, ask yourself these **three questions, in this order**:

1. Is it **still true today** (home, family, job, opinion, habit)? → **present simple**.
2. Is it a **finished event with a past time** (_in 1994_, _in 2015_, _last year_, _two years ago_)? → **simple past**.
3. Is it a **result or a total up to now**, with **no past date** given? → **present perfect**.

| Verbs in the portrait                           | Tense           | Why this one                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| _calls_, _is_, _lives_, _says_                  | present simple  | still true at the moment we read the text    |
| _was born_, _sang_, _recorded_                  | simple past     | over and done, tied to _in 1994_ / _in 2015_ |
| _has released_, _has toured_, _has never given_ | present perfect | the score so far, with no past date given    |

> 🗡️ The same fact can take two tenses — it depends on what you show. _She **recorded** her first album in 2015._ (the dated **event**) — _She **has recorded** four albums._ (the **total**, today).

> ⚠️ A finished past date **kills** the present perfect: "She has released her first album **in 2015**." ✗ → _She **released** her first album in 2015._ ✓ Keep the present perfect for _since 2015_, _so far_, _already_, _never_, _yet_.

> ⚠️ Only these three tenses answer the three questions. A **-ing** form (_is releasing_, _was touring_) is never one of the three answers here — if you meet one in a list of choices, it is there to be left behind.

## 🏅 The present perfect as a career report

To sum up a career you use the **present perfect**, because the career is **not finished**: the count is still open.

- **A total up to now**: _She **has released** four albums._ — _She **has sold** more records than any other singer in her town._
- **An experience she never had**: _She **has never given** a television interview._ — _She **has never won** a big prize._
- **A result that still counts**: _She **has already said** everything in her songs._ — _She **hasn't written** her memoirs **yet**._
- **A period that continues**: _She **has lived** in Sousse **since** 2018._ — _She **has been** a singer **for** more than ten years._

> 🗡️ **since** + the **starting point** (_since 2018_, _since she was a child_) · **for** + the **length of time** (_for ten years_, _for six months_). Never "since ten years".

## 💬 Opinions — asking, giving, agreeing, disagreeing

| Function              | Phrases to use                                                                     | Example                                                     |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Asking for an opinion | _What do you think of…?_ · _How do you feel about…?_                               | _What do you think of her latest record?_                   |
| Asking about a wish   | _Would you like…?_ · _Would you like to…?_                                         | _Would you like to be famous one day?_                      |
| Giving your opinion   | _I think / believe (that)…_ · _In my opinion,…_ · _I'm (not) sure / certain that…_ | _In my opinion, the press asks too many private questions._ |
| Agreeing              | _I agree (with you)._ · _I agree on…_ · _That's true._ · _You're right._           | _I agree with you._ · _They agree on the price._            |
| Disagreeing politely  | _I don't agree._ · _I don't think so._ · _I'm afraid I can't agree._               | _I'm afraid I can't agree — publicity is part of the job._  |

Careful: _Would you like…?_ does **not** ask for an opinion — it asks about a **wish**. Here are the tools in action:

> — **What do you think of** her silence with the press?
>
> — **I believe that** she is right. **In my opinion**, a singer owes us songs, not a private life.
>
> — **I'm afraid I can't agree.** **I'm sure that** publicity is part of her work.

> ⚠️ Two traps. **agree** is a **verb**, never an adjective: _I **agree** with you._ ✓ — "I am agree with you." ✗ · _I **don't agree**._ ✓ — "I am not agree." ✗ (you agree **with** a person and **on** a subject). And _What do you think **of** it?_ asks for a **judgement**, while _think **about**_ usually means to **consider**: _I'm thinking about my future._ Both _What do you think **of** it?_ and _What do you think **about** it?_ are correct; your textbook uses **of**, so use **of**.

> ⚠️ _think_, _believe_ and _be sure_ never take a **-ing** form when they carry an opinion: _I **don't think** she is right._ ✓ — "I am not thinking she is right." ✗ The **-ing** is only for _think **about**_, the job of turning something over: _I'm still thinking about it._

> 🗡️ _that_ is **optional** after _think_, _believe_ and _be sure_: _I think **(that)** she is right._ — both forms are correct. And English prefers to negate the first verb: _I **don't think** she is right._ sounds more natural than "I think she is not right."

## 🔎 Reading skill — build the identity card

A biographical text hides an **identity card**. Scan for it and answer with a **short phrase**, never a full sentence.

| Item               | From the portrait                      | The words that give it away           |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Name               | Nour                                   | "Everybody calls her Nour"            |
| Real name          | Nour Belhaj Amara                      | "her real name is…"                   |
| Place of birth     | Bizerte                                | "She was born in Bizerte"             |
| Place of residence | Sousse                                 | "she now lives in Sousse"             |
| Marital status     | married                                | "with her husband"                    |
| Occupation         | singer and songwriter                  | "She is a singer and a songwriter"    |
| Major records      | _Long Nights_, plus three later albums | "her first album… three more records" |

> 🗡️ Two items are never written in words: **place of residence** hides behind the verb _live_, and **marital status** hides behind _husband_, _wife_ or _single_. Read the clue, then name the fact.

## ✅ Reading skill — prove it, then name it

**True or false + justification.** One word is never an answer. Write **True** or **False**, then **copy the words of the text** that prove it.

- "Nour has always lived in Bizerte." → **False**: _"She was born in Bizerte … she now lives in Sousse."_
- "Nour keeps away from television." → **True**: _"She has never given a television interview."_
- "Nour recorded her first album in a professional studio." → **False**: _"she recorded her first album … in a friend's garage."_

> ⚠️ A justification comes **from the text**, never from your own head. "False, because she prefers other towns" proves nothing.

**Choosing a title.** A good title carries the **main idea of the whole text** in a few words — not one detail, and not a full sentence.

- _A Voice That Keeps Its Distance_ ✓ — it covers her career **and** her silence with the media.
- "Nour's Grandmother" ✗ — one detail from one sentence.
- "Music" ✗ — too vague, it would fit any text; "Nour Belhaj Amara Was Born in Bizerte in 1994" ✗ — a full sentence carrying one single fact.

> 🗡️ Choose a title, answer the other questions, then **read your title again**. If the text turned out to be about something else, change it.

## 🧠 Reading skill — read between the lines

Two questions never have their answer written down in the text.

- **Who does this pronoun name?** Cross out first: a person the sentence already names cannot be the pronoun of that same sentence. _"Sonia showed Anis the photographs, and he chose four of them."_ — Sonia is named, so **he** is Anis.
- **What does this tell us about the person?** Read what somebody **does**, and above all what they **risk** or **refuse**, not what they say about themselves. _"She turned down the biggest studio in the country and went back to her old teacher."_ — nobody writes the word, but she is **faithful** to the person who trained her.

> ⚠️ Your reading must still rest on a sentence of the text. If no line supports it, it is your idea, not the writer's.

## 💰 Word power — success, fame and the press

| Phrase                     | Meaning                                   |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **a successful career**    | a working life that goes well             |
| **a large fortune**        | a very great amount of money              |
| **becoming famous**        | starting to be known by everybody         |
| **achieving an ambition**  | reaching a goal you had dreamed of        |
| **starting a business**    | opening your own company                  |
| **earning a lot of money** | being paid a lot for your work            |
| **university studies**     | the years of study after secondary school |
| **a brilliant future**     | a future full of success                  |

The portrait words: **real name** = the name on your papers · **place of residence** = the town where you live now · **marital status** = single, married, divorced or widowed · **occupation** = your job · **major records** = a singer's most important albums · **fame** = being known by many people · **publicity** = the attention of the media · **the press** = newspapers, magazines and their journalists.

> ⚠️ You **earn** money by working; you **win** a match, a prize or a game. _She earns a lot of money._ ✓ — "She wins a lot of money." ✗ And **fame** and **publicity** are uncountable: _Fame is not always easy._ ✓ — "a fame" ✗.

> 🏆 Gate cleared, hero. You can read a life story, decide every tense yourself, defend an opinion and answer politely to someone who disagrees. Keep the three questions in your pocket — the next texts will not warn you either.
