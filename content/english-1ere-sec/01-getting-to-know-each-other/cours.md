# ⚔️ Getting to Know Each Other — The First Quest Is Always a Question

> 💡 "A hero who knows how to ask never stays a stranger for long."

First day, new classroom, thirty faces you have never seen. You already know how to say _Hello_ and give your name — that is your starting equipment. This lesson hands you the weapon you will use all year: the **WH-question words**, the small words that unlock any piece of information about anybody.

## 🗺️ The WH-words — your map of questions

Each WH-word opens a different door. Learn exactly what each one asks for.

| WH-word      | It asks about | Example question                   |
| ------------ | ------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **what**     | a thing       | _What is your favourite subject?_  |
| **how old**  | an age        | _How old are you?_                 |
| **how many** | a number      | _How many brothers have you got?_  |
| **how much** | a quantity    | _How much do you weigh?_           |
| **where**    | a place       | _Where do you live?_               |
| **who**      | a person      | _Who is your English teacher?_     |
| **when**     | a time        | _When does the school year start?_ |
| **why**      | a cause       | _Why do you like English?_         |
| **why**      | a purpose     | _Why do you go to the library?_    |

**why** is in the table twice on purpose: the same word asks for a **cause** (_for what reason?_) and for a **purpose** (_for what aim?_). The **answer** separates them:

- Cause → **because** + a sentence: _Why do you like English? — **Because** it opens doors._
- Purpose → **to** + base verb: _Why do you go to the library? — **To** borrow books._

> 🗡️ **what + noun** makes the door narrower: _What **colour** is your hair?_, _What **sport** do you play?_ Same word, much sharper question.

## 🏰 Questions with **be** — WH-word + am/is/are + subject

When the information is a **quality or an identity**, the verb is **be**. The order is fixed: **WH-word + am/is/are + subject**.

- _**What is** your name?_ — _My name is Nadia._ (in speech: _**What's** your name?_)
- _**How old are** you?_ — _I'm fifteen._
- _**How tall are** you?_ — _I'm 1.68 metres tall._
- _**What colour are** your eyes?_ — _They're brown._ · _**What colour is** your hair?_ — _It's black._
- _**Who is** your English teacher?_ — _My English teacher is Mrs Trabelsi._

**how old** and **how tall** belong to the same family: **how + adjective** asks you to measure a quality.

> ⚠️ _eyes_ is plural, so it takes **are**; _hair_ is uncountable, so it takes **is**. And never add _do_ to a **be** question: _Where **are** you from?_ ✓ — "Where do you are from?" ✗

## ⚡ Questions with **do** and **does** — WH-word + do/does + subject + base verb

When the information is an **action or a habit**, you need the helper **do** (I, you, we, they) or **does** (he, she, it). The main verb goes back to its **base form**.

- _**Where do** you **live**?_ — _I live in Bizerte._
- _**How much do** you **weigh**?_ — _I weigh 55 kilos._
- _**What sport do** you **play**?_ — _I play volleyball._
- _**What do** your parents **do**?_ — _My father is a farmer and my mother is a teacher._ · _**What does** your father **do**?_ — _He's an engineer._
- _**When does** the school year **start**?_ — _It starts in September._

> ⚠️ Two classic errors. First, **does** already carries the -s, so the verb loses it: _What does your mother **do**?_ ✓ — "What does your mother does?" ✗ Second, a question is **never** built like a statement: _Where **do you live**?_ ✓ — "Where you live?" ✗

## 🛡️ Questions with **have got** — and _how many_ against _how much_

Your textbook uses **have got** to talk about what somebody possesses. The order is **WH-word + have/has + subject + got** (_has got_ for he, she, it).

- _**How many** brothers and sisters **have** you **got**?_ — _I've got one brother and two sisters._
- _**How many** English lessons **have** you **got** a week?_ — _We've got three lessons a week._
- _**What colour** eyes **has** your sister **got**?_ — _She's got green eyes._

Both _Have you got a bike?_ and _Do you have a bike?_ are correct English; simply keep the form your book uses.

**how many** counts, **how much** measures:

- **how many** + a **plural** noun you can count: _How many books have you got?_ — _About thirty._
- **how much** + an **uncountable** noun: _How much time do you spend on your homework?_ — _Two hours a day._
- After _weigh_ and _cost_, **how much** stands alone: _How much do you weigh?_, _How much does it cost?_

> ⚠️ "How much brothers…" ✗ (brothers are countable → **how many**) · "How many money…" ✗ (money is uncountable → **how much**).

## 📋 Ask and answer — the personal-details file

This is the information you can now collect about a classmate, with the question that gets it.

| Information         | Question                                    | Example answer                                   |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| name                | _What's your name?_                         | _My name is Ines._                               |
| age                 | _How old are you?_                          | _I'm fifteen._                                   |
| height              | _How tall are you?_                         | _I'm 1.62 metres tall._                          |
| weight              | _How much do you weigh?_                    | _I weigh 50 kilos._                              |
| eye colour          | _What colour are your eyes?_                | _They're brown._                                 |
| hair colour         | _What colour is your hair?_                 | _It's black._                                    |
| town or village     | _Where do you live?_                        | _I live in Menzel Bourguiba._                    |
| hobby               | _What are your hobbies?_                    | _I like reading and swimming._                   |
| attitude to English | _Why do you like English?_                  | _Because it opens doors._                        |
| parents' occupation | _What do your parents do?_                  | _My mother is a nurse and my father is a baker._ |
| favourite subject   | _What is your favourite subject?_           | _My favourite subject is English._               |
| favourite sport     | _What sport do you play?_                   | _I play handball._                               |
| plans for the year  | _What are your plans for this school year?_ | _I want to improve my English._                  |

Answer in two ways. The **full answer** repeats the verb — _Where do you live? — I **live** in Nabeul._ The **quick answer** gives the information alone — _In Nabeul._ Both are correct; the full answer is safer in an exam.

> ⚠️ Age is expressed with **be**, never with _have_: _I **am** fifteen._ ✓ / _I'm fifteen **years old**._ ✓ — "I have fifteen years." ✗

## ⏳ The same pattern in the past — a previous school, a famous person

The pattern never changes; only the helper travels in time. **is/are** becomes **was/were**, and **do/does** becomes **did** + base verb.

- _**What was** the name of your previous school?_ — _It was Ibn Khaldoun preparatory school._
- _**Who was** your English teacher last year?_ — _My English teacher was Mr Karray._
- _**When** and **where was** he **born**?_ — _He was born in Tunis in 1932._
- _**Why did** she **become** famous?_ — _Because she won an Olympic medal._
- _**What sort of** education **did** he **get**?_ — _He went to a village school, then to university._

> ⚠️ After **did**, the verb goes back to its base form: _What did he **get**?_ ✓ — "What did he got?" ✗

## 🎭 Model dialogue, and your two missions

- **Yassine:** Hello! I'm Yassine. What's your name?
- **Amira:** My name's Amira. Nice to meet you. Where do you live?
- **Yassine:** In Msaken. How many brothers and sisters have you got?
- **Amira:** I've got one brother. What's your favourite subject?
- **Yassine:** English — because I want to work with tourists. What are your plans for this year?
- **Amira:** I want to read one English book a month. Please help me choose the first one!

Three rules while you work in pairs: **stay on task**, **speak English**, and **be polite** (_please_, _thank you_).

**Mission 1 — introduce your classmate.** Choose five pieces of information and report them to the class in the **third person**: verbs take **-s**, _have got_ becomes _has got_, and _your_ becomes _his_ or _her_.

_This is Amira. She **is** fifteen. She **lives** in Hammam Sousse. She **has got** one brother. **Her** favourite subject is English._

**Mission 2 — project work.** Collect information about a famous person (a scientist, an artist, a writer, a leader, a sportsperson) and introduce him or her to the class. Answer these five questions: _Who is the person? When and where was he/she born? What sort of education did he/she get? Why did he/she become famous? Did success or fame affect his/her life?_

## ⚠️ The trap wall

| Wrong               | Right              | Why                                                                  |
| ------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "How old have you?" | _How old are you?_ | Age uses **be**, not _have_ — and so does the answer: _I'm fifteen._ |
| "For borrow books." | _To borrow books._ | Purpose = **to** + base verb                                         |

_What is your age?_ is correct English, but it sounds like a form to fill in — people really say _How old are you?_

> 🏆 First gate cleared, hero. You can now walk up to any stranger and come back with a full file on them. Next quest: **We've made it to the top** — new people, and new stories to tell about them.
