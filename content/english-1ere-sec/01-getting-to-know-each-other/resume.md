# 📜 Summary: Getting to Know Each Other

- **The WH-words** — _what_ (thing), _how old_ (age), _how many_ (number), _how much_ (quantity), _where_ (place), _who_ (person), _when_ (time), _why_ (cause **and** purpose); a cause is answered with **because** + sentence (_Because it opens doors._), a purpose with **to** + base verb (_To borrow books._).
- **Questions with be** — **WH-word + am/is/are + subject**: _What is your name?_, _How old are you?_, _How tall are you?_, _What colour **are** your eyes?_ (plural) but _What colour **is** your hair?_ (uncountable); never add _do_ to a _be_ question.
- **Questions with do/does** — **WH-word + do/does + subject + base verb**: _Where do you live?_, _How much do you weigh?_, _What does your father do?_ (occupation); after **does** the verb keeps its base form (_does she do_, not "does she does").
- **Questions with have got** — **WH-word + have/has + subject + got**: _How many brothers have you got?_, _What colour eyes has your sister got?_ Use **how many** with countable plurals and **how much** with uncountables (_How much time…_); after _weigh_ and _cost_, _how much_ stands alone.
- **The personal-details file** — name, age, height, weight, eye colour, hair colour, town or village, hobbies, parents' occupations, favourite subject, favourite sport, plans for the school year, each with its question; answer in full (_I live in Nabeul._) or quickly (_In Nabeul._).
- **The same pattern in the past** — only the helper changes: _is/are_ → **was/were** (_What was the name of your previous school?_, _When and where was he born?_), _do/does_ → **did** + base verb (_Why did she become famous?_, _What sort of education did he get?_).
- **Introducing someone** — after the pair-work dialogue (stay on task, speak English, be polite), report five details in the **third person**: _She **is** fifteen. She **lives** in Hammam Sousse. She **has got** one brother. **Her** favourite subject is English._ The project work applies the same questions to a famous person.
- **The trap wall** — _How old are you?_ / _I'm fifteen._ (never "I have fifteen years"), _Where do you live?_ (never "Where you live?"), _Where are you from?_ (never "Where do you are from?"), **how many** friends, **to** borrow books; _What is your age?_ is correct but _How old are you?_ is what people say.
