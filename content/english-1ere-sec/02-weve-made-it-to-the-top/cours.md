# ⚔️ We've Made It to the Top — The Three Tenses of a Climb

> 💡 "Anyone can have a dream. Only the right tense can tell how you reached it."

Every success story is a story of **time**: what is always true, what happened and finished, and what has changed by today. You already master the **present simple** for habits and facts, and the **simple past** for finished actions. This lesson hands you the weapon that states a result **without saying when**: the **present perfect**. Hold all three, and you can narrate any past — a match, a journey, a company that started from nothing.

## 🏰 Three jobs, three tenses

This table is the map of the whole lesson. Read the **job** of your sentence first, then pick the tense.

| The job of the sentence                                           | Tense               | Example                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| A **general truth** — true yesterday, today and tomorrow          | **present simple**  | _Hard work **brings** success._             |
| A **finished past action** — the time is known or stated          | **simple past**     | _They **opened** their first shop in 1998._ |
| A **past action whose time is unknown** — the result counts today | **present perfect** | _They **have opened** three factories._     |

The third line is the new power. _They have opened three factories_ gives you the score **now**; it never says **when**. The moment you name the moment, the sentence must change tense: _They **opened** the third factory last May._

> 🗡️ Ask one question before you write: **"Is the moment of the action fixed?"** Yes — named in the sentence, or already set by the story around it → simple past. No, and the result is what counts today → present perfect.

## ⚡ The simple past — the tense of finished time

Use the simple past for an action **finished at a definite past time**.

- **Regular verbs** add **-ed**: _work → **worked**_, _start → **started**_, _arrive → **arrived**_ (silent -e → + d), _carry → **carried**_ (consonant + y → ied), _plan → **planned**_ (short vowel + one consonant → double it).
- **Irregular verbs** have their own form, learned by heart: _sell → **sold**_, _leave → **left**_, _go → **went**_.

| Form          | Rule                                     | Example                                     |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Affirmative   | the past form, the same for every person | _She **left** school at sixteen._           |
| Negative      | **didn't** + **base verb**               | _She **didn't finish** university._         |
| Question      | **Did** + subject + **base verb**        | _**Did** they **buy** the factory?_         |
| Short answers | with **did**                             | _Yes, they **did**. / No, they **didn't**._ |

> ⚠️ After **did / didn't** the verb goes **back to its base form**: _She **didn't leave**_ ✓ — "didn't left" ✗ · _**Did** he **know**?_ ✓ — "Did he knew?" ✗

## 🔮 The present perfect — have/has + past participle

The present perfect ties a past action to **now**. Its form never changes: **have / has + past participle**.

| Form          | Rule                                                 | Example                                   |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Affirmative   | I/you/we/they **have** + PP · he/she/it **has** + PP | _We **have made** it to the top._         |
| Contracted    | **'ve** / **'s**                                     | _**They've begun** a new project._        |
| Negative      | **haven't / hasn't** + PP                            | _He **hasn't sold** the company._         |
| Question      | **Have / Has** + subject + PP                        | _**Has** she **found** a partner?_        |
| Short answers | with **have / has**                                  | _Yes, she **has**. / No, she **hasn't**._ |

The **past participle** is the third form of the verb. For regular verbs it is identical to the simple past (_work → worked → worked_); for irregular verbs it is often **different** — and that difference is where marks are lost.

## 🗡️ The fourteen irregular verbs of this lesson

Learn the three columns together, out loud, always in this order.

| Infinitive | Simple past      | Past participle  |
| ---------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| be         | was / were       | been             |
| begin      | began            | begun            |
| buy        | bought           | bought           |
| come       | came             | come             |
| dream      | dreamt (dreamed) | dreamt (dreamed) |
| find       | found            | found            |
| get        | got              | got              |
| go         | went             | gone             |
| have       | had              | had              |
| know       | knew             | known            |
| leave      | left             | left             |
| lose       | lost             | lost             |
| make       | made             | made             |
| sell       | sold             | sold             |

> 🗡️ Three families make the table easy. **Past = participle**: _buy, bought, bought_ · _dream, dreamt, dreamt_ · _find, found, found_ · _get, got, got_ · _have, had, had_ · _leave, left, left_ · _lose, lost, lost_ · _make, made, made_ · _sell, sold, sold_. **Participle = infinitive**: _come, came, come_. **Three different forms**: _be, was/were, been_ · _begin, began, begun_ · _go, went, gone_ · _know, knew, known_.

> ⚠️ Your textbook is **British English**: the past participle of _get_ is **got** (_The market **has got** tougher._). _Dream_ is the flexible one — _dreamt_ and _dreamed_ are both correct, in the past and in the participle.

## ⏳ The time markers that choose the tense for you

Small words betray the tense. Learn them in two columns.

| Simple past — a finished moment is named | Present perfect — no finished moment is named |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **yesterday**, **last year / last week** | **ever**, **never**                           |
| **… ago** (_two years ago_)              | **already**, **yet**                          |
| **in 1998**, **when I was young**        | **just**, **since**, **for**                  |

- **ever, never, already, just** stand **between have/has and the participle**: _**Have** you **ever** worked abroad?_ · _She **has just** signed the contract._ · _They **have already** signed._ · _I **have never** lost a client._
- **yet** goes at the **end** of the sentence, and only in negatives and questions: _He **hasn't** answered **yet**._ · _**Have** they arrived **yet**?_
- **since** + a **starting point** (_since 2015_, _since Monday_, _since I left school_); **for** + a **length of time** (_for ten years_, _for six months_). The matching question: _**How long have** you **had** this shop?_

> ⚠️ **since 2018** names a year and still takes the present perfect: it gives the **starting point** of something that is **still true** — _He **has managed** the club **since** 2018._ **in 2018** closes the action inside the past and takes the simple past — _He **managed** the club **in** 2018._ The year is not the test; the **finished moment** is.

## 📖 Narrating a climb — the tenses at work

Watch the three tenses share one short story:

> _Good ideas **come** from real needs_ (general truth → present simple). _In 1996 Nadia **left** her job and **bought** a small van_ (dated and finished → simple past). _She **began** with four customers and **lost** money for two years_ (still dated → simple past). _Since then her company **has sold** millions of bottles, and she **has never** borrowed a dinar from a bank_ (no date, the result counts today → present perfect). _She **has just made** a new deal; she **hasn't found** a manager for the second warehouse **yet**._

> 🗡️ In a real story the two past tenses take turns: the **present perfect announces the score** (_we **have made** it_), the **simple past supplies the details and the dates** (_we **started** in 1996_).

## ⚠️ Three traps that cost marks

1. **A date kills the present perfect.** _"I have come back yesterday"_ ✗ → _I **came** back yesterday_ ✓ · _"She has left the company last year"_ ✗ → _She **left** the company last year_ ✓. **Yesterday, last year, in 1998** and **… ago** force the simple past, however natural the other tense feels in your first language.
2. **Never the past form after have/has.** _"I have went"_ ✗ → _I **have gone**_ ✓ · _"She has knew"_ ✗ → _She **has known**_ ✓ · _"They have began"_ ✗ → _They **have begun**_ ✓. Column three, never column two.
3. **since ≠ for.** _since_ marks the **beginning**, _for_ marks the **duration**: _We **have been** partners **since** 2010_ ✓ · _We **have been** partners **for** many years_ ✓ — "since many years" ✗.

> 🏆 Gate two is cleared, hero. You can open a story with a result, fill it with dates, and never let a _yesterday_ sit beside a _have_. Keep the three tenses close: the next lesson puts all three to work on the life of a legend of soul music.
