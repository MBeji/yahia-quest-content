# 📜 Summary: We've Made It to the Top

- **Three jobs, three tenses** — a **general truth** takes the **present simple** (_Hard work brings success._); a **finished past action with a stated time** takes the **simple past** (_They opened their first shop in 1998._); a **past action whose time is unknown** takes the **present perfect** (_They have opened three factories._). The test: is the moment of the action fixed — named here, or already set by the story?
- **Simple past** — regular verbs add **-ed** (_worked, started, arrived, carried, planned_), irregular verbs use their own form; negative = **didn't + base verb**, question = **Did + subject + base verb**, short answers _Yes, they did. / No, they didn't._ Never keep the past form after did (_didn't leave_ ✓, "didn't left" ✗).
- **Present perfect** — **have / has + past participle**: _We have made it to the top._ Contracted **'ve / 's**; negative **haven't / hasn't** + participle; question **Have / Has** + subject + participle; short answers _Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't._ The participle is the verb's **third form**, often different from the simple past.
- **The fourteen irregular verbs** (infinitive · simple past · past participle) — be · was/were · been | begin · began · begun | buy · bought · bought | come · came · come | dream · dreamt (dreamed) · dreamt (dreamed) | find · found · found | get · got · got | go · went · gone | have · had · had | know · knew · known | leave · left · left | lose · lost · lost | make · made · made | sell · sold · sold. British English keeps **got** as the participle of _get_.
- **Time markers** — a **finished moment** (**yesterday, last year / last week, … ago, in 1998, when I was young**) → simple past; **ever, never, already, yet, just** and the pair **since** (starting point) / **for** (duration) → present perfect — _since 2018_ names a year but opens a situation that is still true, unlike _in 2018_. _ever, never, already, just_ sit **between have/has and the participle**; **yet** goes at the **end**, in negatives and questions only.
- **Narrating past events** — the two past tenses take turns: the **present perfect announces the result** (_her company has sold millions of bottles_), the **simple past supplies the dates and details** (_in 1996 she left her job and bought a small van_).
- **The three traps** — a stated date kills the present perfect ("I have come back yesterday" ✗ → _I came back yesterday_ ✓); never put the simple past after have/has ("I have went" ✗ → _I have gone_ ✓, "They have began" ✗ → _They have begun_ ✓); **since** marks the starting point, **for** marks the duration ("since many years" ✗ → _for many years_ ✓).
