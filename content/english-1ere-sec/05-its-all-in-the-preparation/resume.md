# 📜 Summary: It's All in the Preparation

- **The three columns** — subject / object / possessive adjective: **I·me·my**, **we·us·our**, **you·you·your**, **he·him·his**, **she·her·her**, **it·it·its**, **they·them·their**; _you_ and _it_ keep one form for subject and object, and _her_ is both the object form and the possessive one.
- **Where each column stands** — subject pronoun **before the verb** (_**She** works in a kitchen._), object pronoun **after the verb or after any preposition** (_The chef trained **her**. / talk to **her**_), possessive adjective **immediately before a noun**, never alone (_**Her** kitchen is famous._); the subject is never dropped, and the possessive follows the **owner** (_Sarah phoned **her** father._).
- **What a pronoun points at** — look backwards for the nearest noun that agrees in number and gender, check the column (subject or object = the person or thing itself, possessive = its owner), then put the noun back: _her sister_ → Hela, _pay **it** back_ → the loan, _promised **them**_ → the parents, _**its** bread_ → the bakery.
- **The prepositions** — **at** (exact point, clock time), **in** (inside something; a month, a season, a year), **on** (a surface; a day or a date), **to / from** (direction and origin), **for** (who it is meant for, how long), **with** (company or tool), **about** (the subject you speak of); time zooms out **at** seven → **on** Monday → **in** July, and _at the weekend_ (British) and _on the weekend_ (American) are both correct.
- **Verbs that keep their preposition** — **work in** a place, **get on well with** somebody, **think of / about** something (both correct), **listen to** somebody, **depend on** somebody or something.
- **Getting the information, and giving it back** — fill the file (name, age, occupation, family size, father's job, mother's job) by inferring it from what the speaker says, then say the same content with other words: _"I can't see myself sitting in an office…"_ → _She cannot imagine an office job_; _I'm a chef._ = _I work as a chef._
- **The words of the lesson** — **freedom**, **a struggle**, **a chef**, **a nurse**, **cookery** (a **cookery school**), **a mobile** (a mobile phone, British English), **a wedding**, **a loan**, and **to get on well with** somebody.
- **The trap wall** — **it's** = _it is_ but **its** is the possessive with no apostrophe; _I go **home**_ (no _to_); _depend **on**_, never "depend of"; _listen **to**_, never "listen the"; after a preposition the object form (_for **him** and **me**_); _Karim washed **his** hands._
