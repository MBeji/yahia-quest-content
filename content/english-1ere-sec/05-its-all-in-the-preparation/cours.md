# ⚔️ It's All in the Preparation — Every Small Word Points at Somebody

> 💡 "Nothing in a kitchen is ready by accident. Prepare your small words, and the sentence cooks itself."

This lesson tells the story of a young woman who left school, said no to a safe job and walked into a hot kitchen full of men. To follow a story like that you need two families of small words: the **pronouns**, which name people again without repeating their names, and the **prepositions**, which say where, when, with whom and about what. They are tiny. Miss one, and the whole sentence collapses.

## 🏰 The three columns — subject, object, possessive

English gives every person **three** forms. Learn them as three columns, always in this order — and watch two rows: **you** and **it** keep one single form for subject and object, and **her** is the object form **and** the possessive one.

| Subject pronoun | Object pronoun | Possessive adjective | Example                                                 |
| --------------- | -------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **I**           | **me**         | **my**               | _I keep my knives with me._                             |
| **we**          | **us**         | **our**              | _We finished our shift and the chef thanked us._        |
| **you**         | **you**        | **your**             | _You know your recipes, so the chef trusts you._        |
| **he**          | **him**        | **his**              | _He washed his hands, then I called him._               |
| **she**         | **her**        | **her**              | _She gave her order and the waiter repeated it to her._ |
| **it**          | **it**         | **its**              | _It is a small restaurant, but its kitchen is famous._  |
| **they**        | **them**       | **their**            | _They packed their tools before we helped them._        |

## ⚡ Where each column stands in the sentence

The column is chosen by the **place** the word occupies in the sentence, never by the person you are talking about.

| Column                   | Its place                                    | Example                                       |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Subject pronoun**      | before the verb — it does the action         | _**She** works in a kitchen._                 |
| **Object pronoun**       | after the verb, or after any preposition     | _The chef trained **her**. / talk to **her**_ |
| **Possessive adjective** | immediately before a noun — never on its own | _**Her** kitchen is famous._                  |

Transform one sentence and the three columns appear at once: _Leila trained Karim in Leila's kitchen._ → _**She** trained **him** in **her** kitchen._ English never drops the subject ("Is a nurse." ✗ — _**She** is a nurse._ ✓), and a possessive follows the **owner**, not the thing owned: Sarah phoned **her** father — _her_ because Sarah is a woman, even though _father_ is a man.

> ⚠️ After a preposition, the object form is compulsory: _This dish is for **him** and **me**._ ✓ — "for he and I" ✗

## 🔮 What does the pronoun point at?

A pronoun is a promise: the noun it replaces has already been said. Finding that noun is a reading skill, and you will be asked for it. Follow the pronouns through the short passage below.

Look **backwards** for the nearest noun that agrees in number (one or several) and in gender, then check the column: a subject or object pronoun replaces the person or thing itself, a possessive names its **owner**. Last step, put that noun back into the sentence — if it still makes sense, you have found the right one.

> Hela left school at seventeen and opened a small bakery with her sister. Their parents were worried, because the loan was large and the two girls were very young. "We will pay it back in three years," Hela promised them. Today the bakery employs six people, and its bread is sold in every shop in the town.

| Pronoun           | Its column           | It points at |
| ----------------- | -------------------- | ------------ |
| **her** sister    | possessive adjective | Hela         |
| pay **it** back   | object pronoun       | the loan     |
| promised **them** | object pronoun       | the parents  |
| **its** bread     | possessive adjective | the bakery   |

## 🧭 The prepositions, use by use

| Preposition   | Use                                               | Example                                                           |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **at**        | an exact point in space; a clock time             | _Service starts **at** seven._                                    |
| **in**        | inside something; a month, a season, a year       | _She works **in** a kitchen. / She started **in** June._          |
| **on**        | on a surface; a day or a date                     | _The wedding is **on** Saturday._                                 |
| **to / from** | the direction you go to; the point you start from | _She went **to** cookery school. / She comes **from** a village._ |
| **for**       | who something is meant for; how long it lasts     | _She cooked **for** two hundred guests._                          |
| **with**      | in the company of somebody; the tool you use      | _She works **with** ten men._                                     |
| **about**     | the subject of what you say, think or read        | _They talked **about** her future._                               |

> 🗡️ Time zooms out from left to right: **at** seven o'clock → **on** Monday → **in** July. Both _at the weekend_ (British) and _on the weekend_ (American) are correct English; your book is British, so keep _at the weekend_.

## 🔗 Verbs that keep their own preposition

Some verbs travel everywhere with a preposition attached. Learn the pair, never the verb alone.

| Verb + preposition                | Meaning                           | Example                                             |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **work in** a place               | to be employed inside it          | _She **works in** a busy hotel kitchen._            |
| **get on well with** somebody     | to have a good relationship       | _She **gets on well with** the other cooks._        |
| **think of / about** something    | to have it in mind (both correct) | _She often **thinks about** her first day at work._ |
| **listen to** somebody            | to pay attention to               | _A young cook **listens to** the head chef._        |
| **depend on** somebody, something | to be decided by                  | _The menu **depends on** the season._               |

## 📋 Getting the information — and giving it back in your own words

Reading a testimony means filling a file: **name, age, occupation, family size, father's job, mother's job**. The speaker almost never uses those labels, so you infer each line from what is said.

| What the speaker says                                 | What you write in the file                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| _"I'm nineteen and I've just left cookery school."_   | Age: 19 · Occupation: cook                      |
| _"My mother is a nurse and my father drives a taxi."_ | Mother's job: nurse · Father's job: taxi driver |

Then comes the harder move: **say the same thing with other words**. Keep the meaning, change the wording. It works about yourself too, where one content takes two forms: _I'm a chef._ = _I work as a chef._ · _I've got two sisters._ = _There are three children in my family._

- _"I can't see myself sitting in an office working with a computer."_ → **She cannot imagine an office job; that life is not for her.** (_I can't see myself + -ing_ means _I can't imagine myself doing it_.)
- _"I want to work in all the top places."_ → **She hopes to cook in the very best restaurants.** It tells us she is ambitious.

## 📖 The words of the lesson

- **freedom** — being able to choose for yourself: _At sixteen she wanted **freedom**, not orders._ · **a struggle** — a hard fight or effort: _The first year was a **struggle**._
- **a chef** — the cook in charge of a kitchen · **a nurse** — the person who looks after patients in a hospital · **cookery** — the art of cooking, hence **a cookery school**.
- **a mobile** — a mobile phone in British English (as an adjective, able to move around) · **a wedding** — the ceremony where two people marry · **a loan** — money you borrow and must pay back: _She took out a small **loan** to buy her first oven._

## ⚠️ The trap wall

| Wrong                             | Right                                 | Why                                                     |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Its a hard job."                 | _**It's** a hard job._                | **it's** = _it is_; **its** = possessive, no apostrophe |
| "I go to home after work."        | _I go **home** after work._           | _go home_ takes no preposition at all                   |
| "The menu depends of the season." | _The menu **depends on** the season._ | _depend_ always keeps **on**                            |
| "She listens the head chef."      | _She **listens to** the head chef._   | _listen_ always keeps **to**                            |
| "Karim washed her hands."         | _Karim washed **his** hands._         | the possessive follows the owner: Karim is a man        |

> 🏆 Preparation done, hero. You can now follow any pronoun back to its owner and pin the right preposition to any verb — the two skills that keep a long story readable. Sharpen them: the next lesson will ask for every word you have just prepared.
