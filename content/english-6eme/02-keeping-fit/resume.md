# 📜 Summary: Keeping Fit

- **Healthy and unhealthy food** — **healthy** (fruits, vegetables, meat, fish, eggs, pasta, bread, milk, yoghurt) is good for your body and you eat **lots of** it; **unhealthy** (sweets, doughnuts, crisps, cakes, too much oil) is **bad for** it.
- **Giving advice — should / shouldn't** — **should** + bare verb for a good idea (_You should eat vegetables_), **shouldn't** + bare verb for a bad one (_You shouldn't eat lots of sweets_); never _to_, never an **-s**.
- **The strong rule — must / mustn't** — **must** + bare verb = it is necessary (_You must brush your teeth_), **mustn't** + bare verb = it is forbidden; same shape as should, stronger force.
- **Ability — can / can't** — **can** + bare verb says the body is able (_She can swim_), **can't** says it is not; ask _Can you swim?_ and answer _Yes, I can._ / _No, I can't._ A **fit** person plays and runs without getting **tired**.
- **Ailments — have a + ailment** — what hurts is a noun in **-ache**: _I have a headache / a toothache / a stomachache_ (with he, she, it: _has_).
- **States — to be + adjective** — how you feel is an adjective: _I am sick._ · _I am tired._ · _He is sad._ · _They are excited._
- **Quantity — How many / How much** — **How many** + a countable noun in **-s** (eggs, biscuits, tomatoes); **How much** + an uncountable noun (cheese, milk, oil, rice). The test: _two eggs_ works, _two cheese_ does not.
- **Pronunciation** — the **short** vowel stops at once (fit, hit, drink, milk, rabbit, sit) and the **long** one can be held (cheese, meat, teeth, eat, feet, read).
