# 📜 Summary: Going Shopping

- **The shops and their goods** — each shop is named after what it sells: the **bakery** (bread), the **butcher's** (meat), the **toy shop**, the **clothes shop**, the **jewellery shop** (a ring, a necklace, gold, silver), the **music shop** (an instrument), the **market** and the **mall**. The **customer buys**, the **shopkeeper sells**.
- **Asking politely** — **I would like** + a thing (_I'd like a kilo of meat_), **I would like to** + a verb (_I'd like to see the rings_), **Would you like…?** to ask; answer _Yes, please._ / _No, thank you._ Not the same as **I like**, which tells your taste.
- **The price** — **How much is it?** → **It's** 12 dinars · **How much are they?** → **They're** 30 dinars. Something **cheap** costs little money; a shop **on sale** puts money **off** its prices.
- **Numbers up to the hundreds** — 100 = a hundred, 200 = two hundred … 900 = nine hundred; **hundred takes no -s** after a number, and **and** joins the two parts: 468 = four hundred and sixty-eight.
- **The simple past** — before now: regular verbs take **-ed** (visited, shopped, studied, closed), irregular verbs change shape (**bought**, sold, found, chose, won, spent, paid, went); the same form for everybody. Time words: yesterday, last week, last month, five weeks ago.
- **At the clothes shop** — a blazer, a vest, a sweater, a tie; you **try on** the clothes in the **fitting room**. It **fits** you = it is your size; it **suits** you = it looks good on you.
- **in and on the corner** — **in the corner** = inside, where two walls meet (the fitting room of the shop); **on the corner** = outside, where two streets meet (the bakery of Market Street).
- **Pronunciation** — the sound of **jeans** (jacket, jewellery, fridge, jump, enjoy) starts with a tiny **d**; the sound of **garage** (usually) is the buzz alone.
