# 📜 Summary: The First Conditional

- **Condition and result** — the **if clause** states the condition (what may happen), the **main clause** states the result (what will follow); the condition is always the clause that starts with **if**, wherever it stands.
- **The form** — **If + simple present, will + base verb**: _If I want a city tour, I'll take the bus._ Never _will_ in the if clause; with he / she / it the present takes **-s** (_If the train goes faster, I'll be on time_).
- **The negative** — if clause: **don't / doesn't + base verb**; main clause: **won't (= will not) + base verb**: _If you don't hurry, you won't catch the bus._ Never _doesn't understands_, never _won't to be_.
- **Order and comma** — the clauses can change places without changing the meaning: **if clause first → comma after it** (_If the bus is late, I'll call you._); **main clause first → no comma** (_I'll call you if the bus is late._).
- **Real possibilities** — the first conditional predicts the likely result of something that may really happen; in a chain, each result can become the next condition, back in the simple present (_If I ask a policeman, he will show me the way._).
- **Going further (beyond the 9th-grade book, not tested)** — zero conditional (_If you heat ice, it melts_), second conditional (_If I had…, I would…_), **unless** = _if … not_.
