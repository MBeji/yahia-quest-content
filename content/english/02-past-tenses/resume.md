# 📜 Summary: Past Tenses

- **Simple past** — a **completed** action in the past, often with an indicator: _yesterday_, _last week_, _two years ago_, _in 2019_.
- **Regular and irregular verbs** — regular: **verb + ed** (_walked_), only **-d** after an **e** (_changed_); irregular verbs have their own form to learn: _go → went_, _eat → ate_, _take → took_, _have → had_; **be** → _was / were_.
- **Questions and negatives** — **Did** + subject + **base verb**? / subject + **didn't** + **base verb**: _Did you meet him? — I didn't meet him._ The past is marked once, in _did_ (never _didn't prepared_).
- **Present perfect** — **have / has + past participle** (_visited_, _eaten_, _gone_, _written_), for an **experience** (_Have you ever…?_), a **change** or a piece of **news**, with no finished time.
- **Just, already, recently, lately** — _just_ and _already_ between have/has and the participle (_has just sent_); _lately_ at the end; _recently_ at either place.
- **Present perfect or simple past?** — a **finished time** (yesterday, last…, … ago, in 1956) → simple past (_I visited Tabarka last year_); **no finished time** → present perfect (_I have visited Tabarka_).
- **Past progressive** — **was / were + verb + ing** for an action **in progress** in the past: _I / he / she / it **was** reading_, _we / you / they **were** drawing_.
- **Going further** — _when_ / _while_ joining the past progressive and the simple past (_I was reading when the phone rang_) is beyond the 9th-grade book.
