# 🏆 Relative Clauses & Comparatives — Defining and Comparing

> 💡 "A hero who knows the right word can point at anyone in the crowd, and say who is the strongest of them all."

## 🏭 Who, which or that? — the relative pronouns

Your class is making a poster for a greener school. On the draft, a classmate has written two short sentences: _We don't like people. They throw rubbish in the playground._

Read them again: we do not dislike **all** people, only some of them. So how do you say, in one sentence, **which** people you mean?

::: definition
**who**, **which** and **that** are **relative pronouns**. Each one opens a group of words that **defines** the noun just before it: it tells us **which** person or **which** thing we are talking about.

:::

| Pronoun   | It refers to…          | Example                                      |
| --------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **who**   | a person               | the people **who** throw rubbish             |
| **which** | a thing (or an animal) | the fumes **which** come from the factory    |
| **that**  | a person or a thing    | the people **that** … · the fumes **that** … |

The noun on the left of the blank chooses the word: a **person** asks for **who**, a **thing** asks for **which**, and **that** fits both of them.

::: exemple
Finish two sentences of the poster, step by step:

1. _We don't like people ___ throw rubbish._ The noun before the blank is _people_, persons → **who**.
2. _We want trees ___ give shade._ The noun before the blank is _trees_, things → **which**.
3. Both blanks could also take **that**, because **that** works for persons and for things.

:::

> ⚠️ **Common mistake**: _the fumes **who** come from the factory_ ✗. It tempts you because in Arabic the same relative word serves for people and for things alike. English does not: fumes are things, so the pronoun is **which** — _the fumes **which** come from the factory_ ✓ — or **that**.

::: propriete
In these groups that define a noun, you can always replace **who** or **which** by **that**. The reverse is not free: **which** never refers to a person, and **who** never refers to a thing.

Edge case: **what** and **when** are not the relative pronouns of this lesson — they never replace **who** for a person or **which** for a thing. After a noun, never write _the boy **what** won_ ✗: write _the boy **who** won_ ✓.

:::

::: verifie
Complete: "Mr Salah is a farmer ___ grows olives. He has a machine ___ makes olive oil."

---

_a farmer **who** grows olives_ — a farmer is a person, so **who**; _a machine **which** makes olive oil_ — a machine is a thing, so **which**. **that** would also fit both blanks.

:::

::: retenir
**who** → people · **which** → things · **that** → people or things.

:::

## 🔗 Joining two sentences with who, which or that

A news flash on the radio: _The police stopped a lorry. The lorry was throwing waste into the river._ Two sentences, and the second one starts by repeating _the lorry_.

The listener already knows which lorry we mean. So how do you make **one** sentence, without saying _lorry_ twice?

| Two sentences                                                                | One sentence                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The police stopped a lorry. **The lorry** was throwing waste into the river. | The police stopped a lorry **which** was throwing waste into the river. |

The bold words are the only change: the repeated noun _the lorry_ becomes the pronoun **which**, and the second sentence follows the noun directly.

::: exemple
Join the two sentences of the news flash, step by step:

1. Find the noun that appears in both sentences → _a lorry_ / _the lorry_.
2. In the second sentence, _the lorry_ is the **subject**: it does the action (_was throwing_).
3. A lorry is a thing → the pronoun is **which** (or **that**).
4. Replace _the lorry_ by **which** and put the second sentence right after the noun → _The police stopped a lorry **which** was throwing waste into the river._

:::

> ⚠️ **Common mistake**: _a lorry which **it** was throwing waste_ ✗. It tempts you because the second sentence had a subject, and your ear wants one before the verb. But **which** has already taken the place of the subject: a second subject is one too many. Say _a lorry **which** was throwing waste_ ✓.

::: methode

1. Find the noun shared by the two sentences.
2. Choose **who** (person), **which** (thing) or **that** (both).
3. Replace the repeated subject of the second sentence by that pronoun — and add no _he, she, it, they_.
4. Put the new group **right after** its noun.

Edge case: the shared noun can be the subject of the first sentence. _The boy is in my class. The boy threw a bottle into the sea._ → _The boy **who** threw a bottle into the sea is in my class._ The relative group sits in the middle, glued to _the boy_.

:::

::: verifie
Join: "I met a girl. The girl collects waste paper for recycling."

---

_I met a girl **who** collects waste paper for recycling._ (or _**that** collects_) — _the girl_ is the subject of _collects_ and a person, so **who** replaces it, and no _she_ is added after it.

:::

::: retenir
To join two sentences: the repeated subject becomes **who / which / that**, placed right after its noun — never followed by _he, she, it_.

:::

## ⚖️ Comparing two: taller than, more intelligent than

Your cousin shows you a family photo. Her father is 1.80 m tall, her mother 1.65 m. Her little brother, Karim, solves maths problems that she cannot solve.

You want to say two things: the father beats the mother in height, and Karim beats his sister in intelligence. Why does English say one sentence with **-er**, and the other with **more**?

::: definition
The **comparative** compares **two** people or things. It has two forms:

- a **short adjective** (_tall, old, young, strong_) takes **-er** + **than**;
- a **long adjective** (_intelligent, interesting, courageous_) takes **more** + adjective + **than**.

:::

| Adjective   | Type  | Comparative               |
| ----------- | ----- | ------------------------- |
| tall        | short | **taller** than           |
| young       | short | **younger** than          |
| intelligent | long  | **more intelligent** than |
| interesting | long  | **more interesting** than |

**than** introduces the second person or thing, the one we compare with: _taller **than** the mother_.

::: exemple
Write the two sentences about the photo, step by step:

1. 1.80 m is more than 1.65 m → the father wins: he is the subject.
2. _tall_ is a short adjective → **taller**, and **than** introduces the mother → _The father is **taller than** the mother._
3. _intelligent_ is a long adjective → it stays whole, **more** goes before it → _Karim is **more intelligent than** his sister._

:::

> ⚠️ **Common mistake**: _The father is **more taller** than the mother._ ✗ It tempts you because you want to add strength to the comparison, and in Arabic the word for _more_ can come before an adjective. But **-er** already means _more_: one mark only. _The father is **taller** than the mother._ ✓

::: propriete
Short adjective → **-er than**. Long adjective → **more** … **than**. Never both at once: _more taller_ ✗, _intelligenter_ ✗.

Edge case: after **than** we find a noun or an object pronoun — _He is taller **than me**._ — and the word is always **than**, never _that_ or _as_.

:::

::: verifie
Compare a plane and a bus, first with _fast_, then with _expensive_.

---

_A plane is **faster than** a bus._ — _fast_ is short, so it takes **-er**. _A plane is **more expensive than** a bus._ — _expensive_ is long, so **more** goes before it. **than** introduces the bus in both.

:::

::: retenir
Comparing two: short adjective + **-er than** (_taller than_) · **more** + long adjective + **than** (_more intelligent than_).

:::

## ✏️ Spelling the -er and -est forms

In July, Kairouan is very hot, and Tabarka is cooler. You write in your notebook: _Kairouan is hoter than Tabarka._ Your teacher circles one word in red.

The grammar is right: _hot_ is short, so it takes **-er**. So what went wrong with the spelling?

| Adjective | What happens              | Comparative | Superlative      |
| --------- | ------------------------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| small     | just add -er / -est       | smaller     | the smallest     |
| hot       | double the last consonant | **hotter**  | the **hottest**  |
| slim      | double the last consonant | **slimmer** | the **slimmest** |
| silly     | **y** becomes **i**       | **sillier** | the **silliest** |

Look at the right-hand columns: the change made for **-er** is exactly the one made for **-est**.

::: exemple
Spell the comparative of _hot_, step by step:

1. Look at the end of the word: _h-**o-t**_ — one vowel (**o**), then one consonant (**t**).
2. That ending doubles its consonant before -er → _hot_ + _t_ + _er_ = **hotter**.
3. The superlative takes the same double letter → **the hottest**.

So: _Kairouan is **hotter** than Tabarka._

:::

> ⚠️ **Common mistake**: _hoter_ ✗ and _sillyer_ ✗. They tempt you because you simply glue **-er** to the word, as with _small_. But one vowel + one consonant at the end doubles the consonant (**hotter** ✓), and a consonant + **y** changes the **y** into **i** (**sillier** ✓).

::: methode

1. Most short adjectives: add **-er** / **-est** (_hard → harder_, _tough → tougher_).
2. One vowel + one consonant at the end: double the consonant (_fat → fatter_, _slim → slimmer_).
3. Consonant + **y** at the end: **y → i** (_silly → sillier → the silliest_).

Edge case: two consonants at the end never double — _warm → warmer_, not _warmmer_ — and a final **w** never doubles: _new → newer_. And an adjective ending in **-e** only adds **-r**: _wise → wiser_.

:::

::: verifie
Write the comparative of _fat_ and the superlative of _silly_.

---

**fatter** — _f-a-t_ ends with one vowel + one consonant, so the **t** doubles. **the silliest** — _silly_ ends with a consonant + **y**, so the **y** becomes **i** before **-est**.

:::

::: retenir
**hot → hotter**, **slim → slimmer** (double the consonant) · **silly → sillier** (y → i) · otherwise just add **-er**.

:::

## 🏅 The top of the group: the tallest, the most beautiful

Sports day at school. Fethi, Sami and all the other boys try to lift the same bag of sand, and Fethi lifts it higher than all of them.

_Stronger than_ compares Fethi with **one** boy. But Fethi beats **every** boy in the school: how do you say that he is number one?

::: definition
The **superlative** puts one person or thing at the top of a group of **three or more**:

- short adjective: **the** + adjective + **-est** → _the strongest_;
- long adjective: **the most** + adjective → _the most beautiful_.

:::

| Adjective | Comparative         | Superlative            |
| --------- | ------------------- | ---------------------- |
| tall      | taller than         | **the tallest**        |
| strong    | stronger than       | **the strongest**      |
| beautiful | more beautiful than | **the most beautiful** |

Read the table from left to right: **-er** becomes **-est**, **more** becomes **the most**, and **the** comes in front of every superlative.

::: exemple
Say that Fethi is number one, step by step:

1. We compare Fethi with the whole school, not with one boy → superlative.
2. _strong_ is short → **-est** → _strongest_.
3. A superlative always takes **the** → _the strongest_.
4. Name the group with **in** → _Fethi is **the strongest** boy **in** the school._

:::

> ⚠️ **Common mistake**: _Fethi is **strongest** boy in the school._ ✗ It tempts you because in Arabic this top form needs no article. English needs **the**: _Fethi is **the strongest** boy in the school._ ✓

::: propriete
Short adjective → **the -est**; long adjective → **the most** + adjective. **the** is never missing.

Edge case: **the most** and **-est** never go together — _the most beautiful_ ✓, _the most beautifulest_ ✗, _the most strong_ ✗.

:::

::: verifie
Complete: "He bought ___ house in the street." (beautiful)

---

_He bought **the most beautiful** house in the street._ — we compare it with all the houses of the street, so it is a superlative; _beautiful_ is long, so it takes **the most**, not **-est**.

:::

::: retenir
Number one of a group: **the** + short adjective + **-est** (_the tallest_) · **the most** + long adjective (_the most beautiful_).

:::

## 🟰 Equal: as old as

Ahmed was born in March 2011, and his friend Omar in May 2011. Today both of them are 15 years old.

_Older than_ is false, and _younger than_ is false too. So how do you say that they are **the same**?

::: definition
**as** + adjective + **as** expresses **equality**: the two people or things are the same.

:::

| Ahmed | Omar | Sentence                      |
| ----- | ---- | ----------------------------- |
| 15    | 14   | Ahmed is **older than** Omar. |
| 15    | 15   | Ahmed is **as old as** Omar.  |

When the two numbers differ, we use the comparative; when they are the same, **as … as** takes its place.

::: exemple
Write the sentence about Ahmed and Omar, step by step:

1. 15 = 15 → the two ages are equal → **as … as**, not the comparative.
2. Between the two **as**, the adjective stays in its plain form: no **-er**, no **more** → _as **old** as_.
3. _Ahmed is **as old as** Omar._

:::

> ⚠️ **Common mistake**: _Ahmed is as **older** as Omar._ ✗ — or _as old **than** Omar_ ✗. It tempts you because you have just learnt _older than_ and you mix the two structures. Equality keeps the plain adjective and two **as**: _Ahmed is **as old as** Omar._ ✓

::: propriete
**as** + plain adjective + **as**, whatever the length of the adjective: _as tall as_, _as generous as_ (never _as more generous as_).

Edge case: after the second **as** we can find an object pronoun, as after _than_ — _He is **as old as me**._

:::

::: verifie
"Her father is generous. Her mother is generous, too." Make one sentence of equality.

---

_Her father is **as generous as** her mother._ — they are equally generous, so we use **as … as**; _generous_ is long, but it stays plain between the two **as**, without **more**.

:::

::: retenir
Equality: **as** + adjective + **as** (_as old as_, _as generous as_) — plain adjective, never **-er** or **more** inside.

:::

## 💰 The same amount: as much money as, as many friends as

Rim gets 10 dinars of pocket money every week. Her brother gets 10 dinars every week, too.

This time we do not compare an adjective: we compare an **amount** of something — of money. So where do _money_ and _as_ go?

::: definition
**as much** / **as many** + noun + **as** also expresses **equality**: the two people have the same amount of something.

:::

| Start       | Noun    | End    | Example                                   |
| ----------- | ------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| **as much** | money   | **as** | I spend **as much money as** her.         |
| **as much** | food    | **as** | He has **as much food as** his sister.    |
| **as many** | friends | **as** | I have **as many friends as** my brother. |

The noun is sandwiched between **as much / as many** and the second **as**.

::: exemple
Write the sentence about Rim, step by step:

1. 10 dinars = 10 dinars → equality.
2. We compare an amount of a noun (_pocket money_) → **as much** + noun + **as**.
3. The noun comes right after _as much_ → _as much **pocket money** as_.
4. _Rim gets **as much pocket money as** her brother._

:::

> ⚠️ **Common mistake**: _Rim gets as much pocket money **than** her brother._ ✗ It tempts you because **than** follows every comparison you learnt before. Equality always closes with **as**: _as much pocket money **as**_ ✓.

::: propriete
Order: **as much / as many** → noun → **as** → the second person.

Edge case: the second person can be an object pronoun — _I spend as much money a week **as her**._

:::

::: verifie
"I have 12 friends. My brother has 12 friends, too." Make one sentence of equality.

---

_I have **as many friends as** my brother._ — we compare an amount of a noun (_friends_), so the noun sits between **as many** and **as**, and the sentence closes with **as**, not _than_.

:::

::: retenir
Same amount: **as much / as many** + noun + **as** (_as much money as_, _as many friends as_).

:::

## 🚀 Going further (beyond the 9th-grade book)

This section is **not** in the 9th-grade programme and it is never tested. It is here for the curious reader who meets these words in a text.

- **whose** shows possession: _the girl **whose** bag is red_ (= the girl's bag is red).
- **where** refers to a place: _the school **where** I study_.
- Two adjectives have irregular forms: **good → better → the best**, **bad → worse → the worst**.

The linkers (_because, so, therefore, as a result…_) are taught in chapter 07.

> 🏆 Combo mastered, hero! You can now point at the right person with **who**, **which** and **that**, rank anyone with **-er**, **more** and **the most**, and declare a draw with **as … as**.
