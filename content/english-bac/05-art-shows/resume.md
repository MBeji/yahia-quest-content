# 📜 Summary: Art Shows

- **Reading a leaflet** — a leaflet answers five questions in order: **What? When? Where? How much? How do I get in?** Read **across** a line (one line = one event, with its own time, room and price), find **your own category** in a price list (_adults £7.90 · children up to 14 £4.95 · under 3 free_ costs you what your age says it costs), treat a condition (_ages 12–19_, _no need to book_) as seriously as a price, and remember that what a line does not repeat is printed at the top. Large programmes come in three columns — **DRAMA · MUSIC · CINEMA** — and comparing across them means reading two lines far apart. **programme** is British, **program** American; a **venue** is the place where an event is held.
- **The words of a programme** — **foyer** = the entrance hall, the large open space just inside · **refreshments** = drinks and the light food with them · **interactive** = you take part and communicate, you do not only watch · **performance** = a show in front of an audience · **to book** = to reserve before the day · **free** = at no cost. Also **guided tour**, **exhibition**, **box office**. Careful: **free** is about money only — a free event can still be fully booked.
- **am and pm** — from the Latin _meridiem_, midday: **am** = _ante meridiem_, before midday; **pm** = _post meridiem_, after midday. So **12.30 pm** is half an hour **after** midday and comes **after** 11.30 am.
- **Compound adjectives** — two or three words welded with a **hyphen** in front of a noun, doing the work of a whole clause: _a talk that lasts ten minutes_ → **a ten-minute talk**. The patterns: noun + past participle (**science-inspired** gift, **stone-walled** streets — the official pattern of the lesson), adverb + past participle (**naturally-produced** silk), adjective + noun + -ed (**bright-coloured** umbrellas), noun + -ing (an **elephant-training** centre), number + noun (a **200-page** book), number + noun + adjective (a **one million-year-old** tree).
- **The three rules of the compound** — (1) the **hyphen** joins the parts; (2) the noun inside **never takes -s**: _a six-**hour** flight_, _a 30-**dinar** bank note_; (3) the compound stands **before** its noun, and after _be_ you unpack it: _The flight lasts six hours_ ✓ / "The flight is six-hour" ✗. Play it both ways — compress (_a park ornamented with flowers_ → _a **flower-ornamented** park_) and unpack (_a one-hundred-year-old tree_ → _The tree **is** one hundred years **old**_). Age takes **be**, never _have_. And **-ing** ≠ **-ed**: an _elephant-training_ centre trains elephants, an _elephant-trained_ keeper would be trained by them.
- **Expressing an opinion** — a **fact** can be checked (_the play starts at eight_), an **opinion** can be disagreed with (_the play is far too long_). Give it with _In my opinion… · I think… · I find it… · To me…_, **support it with a reason** (_I'd choose the drum show — you play the instruments yourself_), agree with _So do I · That's true_, disagree softly with _I don't quite agree · That's true, but…_, and ask for one with _What do you think?_ An opinion with no reason is half a turn.
- **Judging a film** — **wit** (clever, quick humour), **shot** (one scene as the camera filmed it), **adept at** (skilful at), **thought-provoking** (it makes the audience think), **to tackle a subject** (to deal with a difficult one), **film director**, **script writer**.
- **Word stress** — one syllable louder, longer and clearer. **-ion**, **-ic** and **-ity** pull the stress onto the syllable just **before** them (ex-hi-**BI**-tion, dra-**MA**-tic, ac-**TIV**-i-ty); a **compound noun** stresses its **first** word (**FILM** director, **BOX** office, **TICKET** office) whereas an ordinary adjective + noun keeps it on the noun (a free per**FOR**mance); and two-syllable noun/verb pairs move it (a **RE**-cord / to re-**CORD**). This lesson: per-**FOR**-mance, re-**FRESH**-ments, in-ter-**AC**-tive, **FOY**-er, **CIN**-e-ma.
- **Strings** — **James Joyce** (1882–1941), born in Dublin, an Irish writer who lived by journalism and teaching before _Finnegans Wake_. His poem **"Strings in the earth and air"** (three stanzas) holds one scene — a riverbank, an instrument, the figure of Love: music and love are not two subjects but one and the same presence, and the tone to hear is quiet, tender and unhurried.
- **The word _string_** — thin cord for tying · a chain or series of shops · a queue or line of people · a strand of beads · the wire of an instrument. The **sentence around the word** decides which sense applies, and then ask whether the use is **literal** (a real wire) or **figurative** (a string of shops is tied to nothing). In the poem it is the wire of an instrument.
- **Rhyme scheme** — a **quatrain** is a four-line stanza; same letter = same rhyme sound. **a b a b** alternate (1 with 3, 2 with 4), **a a b b** couplets, **a b b a** enclosed. A **new sound gets a new letter**, so a second quatrain with new rhymes is **c d c d**; and a word never rhymes with itself.
- **Ray Charles's word list** — **inspiration** (stimulation, motivation, encouragement) · **single-handedly** (without anyone's help) · **posthumous** (after a person's death) · **pioneering** (introducing ideas never used before) · **encompassed** (included) · **stature** (importance and respect earned by achievement) · **spell** (fascination, irresistible influence) · **indisputably** (undeniably).
