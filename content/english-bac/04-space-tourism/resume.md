# 📜 Summary: Space Tourism

- **The words of space travel** — a **spaceship** is the craft, a **shuttle** the craft that goes up and comes back again and again, a **spaceport** the **place** it takes off from and returns to; **space travel** is the activity in general, a **space trip** one journey there and back, a **space flight** one crossing. A **suborbital** flight touches the edge of space and comes down; an **orbital** trip goes round the Earth, costs far more and lasts days. New words: a **billionaire** (more than a thousand million dollars), **well-to-do** (rich), a **face-off** (a fight, an open argument), a **foothold** (a first strong position from which you can make progress), a **deal** (an agreement), **emerging** (beginning to be known), **a couple of** (two).
- **Reading a definition** — a definition names the **family first**, then what makes the word different: _billionaire (noun) — **a person who** owns more than a thousand million dollars_. So the first two or three words already say what kind of thing it is (_a place where…_, _a person who…_, _the act of…_, _a position…_). Use it in both directions: from the definition, keep only the word that can be that kind of thing; from the definition, judge a sentence — a face-off is a **fight**, so _the face-off between the two companies lasted a year_ ✓ but "he put the face-off in his pocket" ✗. And when a word has several senses, the surrounding sentence chooses: _they signed a deal_ (agreement) against _a great deal of money_ (quantity).
- **Paraphrasing** — say the same thing with other words: keep the meaning, drop the shape. An **idiom** cannot be built from its words — _to see the world_ = to travel widely, _to do the sights_ = to visit the famous places of a city. A **proverb** hides its idea in an image, and paraphrasing means letting the image go: _The world is a book and those who do not travel read only a page_ = one page tells you almost nothing about a book, and one country almost nothing about the Earth. The classic failure is to keep the image (books, pages) and lose the idea (travel).
- **Verb + -ing** — some verbs are followed straight by a verb in the **-ing** form, with no _to_: **stop, enjoy, dislike, admit, consider, miss, finish, mind, imagine, deny, involve, postpone, delay, suggest, regret, avoid, practise, risk**. _Would you mind closing the door?_ · _He denied knowing the price._ · _The trip involves crossing two deserts._ The trap is **suggest**: "suggested to visit" ✗, **suggested visiting** ✓. A **preposition** does the same — _interested in flying_, _instead of paying_.
- **Verbs that accept both forms** — **begin, start, continue, like, love, hate, prefer, can't stand, can't bear, cease, neglect, propose** take the -ing form **or** _to_ + infinitive with **no change of meaning**: _The countdown continued running_ = _The countdown continued to run._
- **The trap: `stop + -ing` against `stop + to`** — _He **stopped talking**_ names the activity that **ends**: he was talking, he is talking no more. _He **stopped to talk**_ gives the **reason** for stopping — _to_ means **in order to**: he was doing something else and broke off so that he could talk. Three checks: what was he doing before, what is he doing now, and how many actions are named — **one** with the -ing form, **two** with _to_.
- **Negative prefixes** — **un-** (uncertain, unpaid), **in-** (inaccurate), **im-** before **m/p/b** (impatient, immature), **il-** before **l** (illegal, illiterate), **ir-** before **r** (irregular, irresistible), **dis-** often on verbs (dishonest, disobey, discontinue), **ab-** (abnormal), **non-** for an absence rather than an opposite (non-smoker, non-violent). The letter rule covers only il-, im- and ir-, and nothing is dropped when prefix and word meet, hence the **double letter**. Between **un-** and **in-** there is **no rule**: unclear but inexpensive, unpleasant but inconvenient. And a prefix is not only for adjectives — _obey_ → **disobey**, _balance_ → **imbalance**, _paid_ → **unpaid**.
- **Reading the figures** — the **dot** is the decimal point (1.5 = one point five, so a **1.5-hour visit** lasts one hour and a half), the **comma** separates thousands (3,000,000 = three million). French and Arabic swap the two marks.
- **The sound [ei]** — the sound of the letter **A** in the alphabet, written **a…e** (space, take), **-ai-** (paid, aim), **-ay-** (day, delay), **-ei-** (eight, weight), **-ea-** in _great_ and _break_ only, and **a** before **-tion** / **-ency** (station, agency) — plus **they**. Traps: the same **-ea-** gives [i:] in _reach_ and [e] in _head_, and **-i…e** gives [ai] in _price_ and _flight_. Listen for a vowel that ends like the **y** of _say_.
