# 📜 Summary: Online Learning

- **A quotation is a sentence handed to you with its owner attached.** The line printed under the three remarks of this lesson names e-learners writing to a magazine for learners of English, in June 2003 — it settles **who** spoke, **where** and **when**, and nothing else. ⚠️ **A quotation is evidence of an opinion, never proof of a fact**: quote an e-learner and you have proved that an e-learner believed something. That is why the book asks, after each remark, whether **you** agree.
- **The three remarks** — the first: an online course asks for as much of the learner's time as a classroom course, **if not more**. The second: some students expect e-learning to be **easier**, because there is no journey to make. The third: online learning, like every other kind, **demands a commitment of time**, and a learner short of time can make more of it. ⇒ **two of the three say the time is not saved.**
- ⚠️ **The mistake the second remark reports.** The **fact** is that a learner at home does not travel (eighty minutes of bus come free). The **conclusion** — therefore the course is easier — does not follow: those minutes were spent **getting to** the course, never **on** it. **A saving outside a thing tells you nothing about the thing.**
- **Read the hedges.** *If not more* is not decoration: it means *as much, quite possibly more, certainly not less*. Drop the three words and you have a claim of exact equality the writer never made.
- **a time commitment** = hours of the week promised to one thing, and therefore to nothing else. Not money; not a promise to finish; a block of the week spoken for in advance.
- **Making more time** — *not adding hours to a day, but taking hours that are already going somewhere and sending them somewhere else.* That is why the printed example (**sleeping less**) works: sleep is not spare, it is one of the places the hours already go. ⇒ giving up the evening telephone hour **makes time**; hoping the days will feel longer, counting the hours the course will need, or waiting for a quieter term make none. A good suggestion names **two** things: the hours, and the place they used to go.
- **Flexibility** (John Bears, author of a guide to earning degrees by distance learning) = distance study offers a freedom a traditional programme cannot, because the learner may study **whenever** (the **hour**) and **wherever** (the **place**) he wishes.
- ⚠️ **The sharpest point of the lesson.** Flexibility and the time commitment do **not** contradict each other: **flexibility is about *when* and *where* the hours are spent, the commitment about *how many* there are.** A learner may choose every hour freely and still owe the course exactly as many as a classroom would have taken — so answering *you are wrong, it takes just as much time* counters nothing that was said.
- **Benefits / drawbacks** — benefits: the hour is the learner's to choose, the place is his to choose, the journey disappears. Drawbacks: the hours must still be found somewhere in the week; no timetable and no room put the learner at his desk; **the freedom that lets the work happen also lets it stop.**
- **The comma has exactly two jobs here.** **1)** before **and after** a phrase **in apposition**, giving additional information about the noun it follows; **2)** to separate items on a list.
- **An apposition** = a phrase added to another **as an explanation**. _John Bears, author of Bears' Guide to Earning Degrees by Distance Learning, argues that…_ **1)** it stands **immediately after the noun it explains**. **2)** **two commas, not one** (~~Mr Bears, the author of a guide says…~~ opened and never closed; ~~Mr Bears the author of a guide, says…~~ closed and never opened). **3)** **additional** means the sentence **stands without it** — lift the phrase out and what remains must be a whole sentence. That is the test for a doubtful comma.
- ⚠️ **Additional never means useless.** The grammar survives the removal; the **claim** does not. The three words *like all learning*, fenced by two commas, say that online learning is **no exception** — it is put in the same class as every other kind. Take them out and the sentence stops answering anybody who thought this kind was special.
- **List commas** keep the items of a list apart: _She gave up the cinema, the football and the long telephone calls._ And one sentence may need **both** jobs at once: _Habib, the caretaker of the block, has given up the radio, the newspaper and the long lunches._ — a **pair** of commas fencing the explanation, a **single** one keeping list items apart.
- **The formal argumentative letter** — the page prints the frame: the greeting *Dear Mr Bears,*, about a dozen lines of body, and a rule at the foot for the signature. Four instructions, four marks:
  **1) Formal English — because you do not know the man.** That single fact settles the register: a stranger is *Mr Bears*, never *John*.
  **2) State a view.** *To what extent do you agree?* is a question; agree, disagree or agree in part, but land somewhere.
  **3) Justify it with convincing arguments.** Insisting (*I am quite sure of this*), restating (*it is, in my opinion, no easier at all*) and counting heads (*many would agree with me*) are **not** arguments. An argument gives the reader something he can check: _the lessons are the same lessons and the hours they claim are the same hours; only the journey has gone._
  **4) Be tactful.** **tactful** = choosing words so as not to wound the person you disagree with. It does **not** mean dropping the disagreement, and it does **not** mean changing the subject. The move: **grant the point, then set something beside it — without taking the grant back** (_That freedom is real, and it is precisely what makes the hours so easy to let slip._).
- **The words** — **an on-line learner / an e-learner** = somebody following a course through a screen · **a cyber student** = the same person, named for the way he studies · **e-learning** = learning carried by electronic means · **distance learning** = studying in which learner and school are not in the same place · **a time commitment** · **flexibility** = the freedom of hour and place · **benefits / drawbacks** = what is good / what is bad about a thing · **tactful** · **an apposition** · **a comma**.
