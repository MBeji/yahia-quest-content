# 📜 Summary: First Impressions

- **Four books, four questions** — a **dictionary** gives the **meaning** of a word; a **dictionary of synonyms and antonyms** gives similar words **and their opposites**; a **thesaurus** gives an **exhaustive list of equivalents**, grouped by sense; an **encyclopedia** gives information about **subjects**, in alphabetical order. Beside them: grammar book, workbook, pronunciation dictionary, monolingual and bilingual dictionaries.
- **The labels** — **formal** English (essays, lectures, reports) against **colloquial** English (everyday conversations); **synonym** = similar meaning, **antonym** = opposite meaning; **collocation** = words that go together; **phrasal verb** = verb + adverb or preposition, new meaning; **idiom** = a group of words meaning something different from its words; **proofreading** = revising a piece of writing.
- **Collocations** — _time flies / goes by / passes_ ✓ but "time speeds" ✗; _dark hair_, _light meal_, _a new look_, _first aid_, _a first impression_; **make** a phone call / an effort / a noise / your bed, **do** your homework / the washing-up / your best, **cost** a fortune, **go** home. Traps: "do a mistake" ✗ → _make a mistake_; "make your homework" ✗ → _do your homework_; "a new someone" ✗ → _somebody new_.
- **Phrasal verbs — the particle rules** — _come about_ = happen, _make up_ = invent, _give up_ = stop, _call off_ = cancel; and one verb with four faces: **put off** = postpone, **put aside** = save (money), lay down (a newspaper) or leave behind (fears), **put away** = save or put back, **put forward** = suggest, propose. The words that follow decide which meaning.
- **Idioms** — _burn the candle at both ends_ = _burn the midnight oil_ = work very hard and stay up very late; _so far, so good_ = well up to now; _you could hear a pin drop_ = completely silent; _give somebody a hand_ = help; _follow in somebody's footsteps_ = do the same job; _the life and soul of the party_ = the one who makes a gathering fun; _make it to the top_ = reach the highest position; _all of a sudden_ = quickly and unexpectedly.
- **British against American** — three differences: **accent** (with regional accents inside one country), **spelling** (**-our → -or**: colour/color, neighbour/neighbor · **-re → -er**: theatre/theater, metre/meter) and **vocabulary** (ground floor/first floor, autumn/fall, luggage/baggage, pavement/sidewalk, queue/line, holidays/vacation, taxi/cab, rubber/eraser, flat/apartment, sweets/candy, rubbish/garbage, bill/check, lorry/truck, lift/elevator, petrol/gas, biscuits/cookies). Neither is more correct — stay inside one variety per text.
- **The ear against the eye** — the spelling **ch** covers four sounds: **/k/** (psychology, stomach, chorus, chemistry, ache), **/ʃ/** (machine, moustache), **/tʃ/** (achievement, chores), **/dʒ/** (spinach). **Homophones** = same sound, different spelling (one/won, flour/flower, some/sum, higher/hire, I/eye); **homographs** = same spelling, different meaning; **homonyms** = same spelling and same sound, different meanings.
- **Text types and their signals** — an amount and a refund policy → a **receipt**; author, date, title, publisher → a **bibliography** (and the four-figure number is the **date of publication**); praise and a slogan → an **advertisement**; warnings, expiry date, "consult your doctor" → **instructions for use**, field medicine; ingredients and what the item does → a **description of an item**.
- **Big figures and register** — each space or comma cuts a group of three digits: 200 000 = two hundred thousand, 200 000 000 = two hundred million; after a number, **million / billion / trillion** take **no -s** and a plural noun. _Cuppa_ is colloquial for _a cup of tea_: _Shall we have a cuppa?_ = suggestion, _I'm dying for a cuppa._ = desire, _Make us a cuppa, will you?_ = request. Four words to keep from the unit: an **alien** = a stranger (a **legal alien** holds the papers to be there), a **kettle** = the pot with a spout and a lid you boil water in, **staggering** = so big it causes surprise, a **cuppa** = a cup of tea.
- **Cultures and languages** — **mono-** = one (monolingual), **bi-** = two (bilingual, bicultural), **multi-** = many (multilingual, multicultural); **ethnic diversity** = people of many different races and nationalities; **lifestyles** = the way people live. And your **reading rate**, in words per minute, should reach **200** at this level.
