# 📜 Summary: Checking Year 3 Programme

- **Past simple** for a closed moment (_ago_, _yesterday_, _last Monday_, _in 2019_); **present perfect** (_have / has_ + past participle) for a past that still touches now (_since_ + a starting point, _for_ + a length, _never … before_, _so far_, _over the past three weeks_); **past perfect** (_had_ + past participle) for an earlier past. The rule that decides most exercises: **a closed time marker forbids the present perfect** — _The school closed last Monday._ ✓ / "has closed last Monday" ✗
- **Reported speech — three moves at once.** ① The tense steps back: _is_ → _was_, _started_ → _had started_, _has started_ → _had started_, _will_ → _would_, _can_ → _could_, _must / have to_ → _had to_ (a past perfect does not move). ② The people move: _I_ → _he / she_, _we_ → _they_, _my_ → _his / her_, _your_ → _his / her / my_. ③ The time and place words move away: _now_ → _then_, _today_ → _that day_, _yesterday_ → _the day before_, _tomorrow_ → _the next day_, _ago_ → _before_, _here_ → _there_, _this_ → _that_.
- **say or tell** — you **say** something (**to** somebody), you **tell** **somebody** something: _She told her father that…_ / _She said to her father that…_, never "said her father" nor "told to her father". A long quotation is shared out between introducing verbs: _explained_, _added_, _admitted_, _complained_, _ended by saying_.
- **A reported question is no longer a question** — no inversion, no _do_, no question mark: _"Where are the pupils now?"_ → _He asked **where the pupils were then**._ · _"Do you close on Fridays?"_ → _He asked **if / whether they closed on Fridays**._ An order or a piece of advice becomes **tell / ask / advise + somebody + to** + base verb (_told them **not to** come_ for a negative).
- **Building words** — nouns made from verbs and adjectives: _to advise_ → **advice** (c for the noun, s for the verb), _to break out_ → **an outbreak** (the particle jumps in front), _to enrol_ → **enrolment**, _ill_ → **illness**, _anxious_ → **anxiety**. Prefixes: **re-** (again), **over-** (too much), **mis-** (wrongly), **well-** (hyphenated), **un-** and **dis-** (not), **il-** / **im-** / **ir-** / **in-**, one single negative prefix whose form is chosen by the **first letter**: _il-_ before **l** (_illegal_), _im-_ before **m, p, b** (_impatient_), _ir-_ before **r** (_irregular_), _in-_ elsewhere.
- **Adjectives in -ed and in -ing** — **-ed** is how somebody **feels** (_the children were depressed_), **-ing** is what **causes** the feeling (_the situation was depressing_).
- **Words that look alike** — **rise** (no object) / **raise** (needs an object: _raise money_) / **arise** (come into existence) · **require** (need as a condition) / **acquire** (obtain) / **request** (ask politely) / **inquire** (ask for information) · _a lot of_ and _plenty of_ fit a positive sentence, where _any_ cannot go; _any_ belongs to a negative or a question.
- **Linkers** — contrast: _although / though_ + clause, _but_ from the middle, _However,_ in a new sentence, _in spite of_ + noun · consequence: _Therefore,_, _As a result,_, _so_ + clause · reason: _because / as / since_ + clause, _because of_ + noun · alternative: _Otherwise,_, _instead of_ + noun or -ing.
- **Lexical fields** — Leisure, Education, Family relationships, Science and Technology, Nature: grouping words by field is what lets you guess an unknown word from its neighbours; a word can change field with the text (_depression_).
- **Writing as a process — six steps**: ① generating ideas ② planning and organizing them ③ writing the first draft ④ sharing and responding (peer review) ⑤ revising and editing ⑥ writing the final draft. **Content before form**: you act on the peer's marks before you copy anything out neatly. Developing notes into sentences means putting back the articles, the prepositions, the linkers and above all a **finite verb**.
- **The correction code** — **V** vocabulary mistake or misused word · **G** grammar · **S** spelling · **P** punctuation · **C** capitalization · **W.L** wrong linker · **W.O.** word order · **√** something missing in the sentence. **V** is an ordinary word, **W.L** a link word; **S** is inside a word, **W.O.** between words.
