# 📜 Summary: At the Travel Agency

- **The words of the counter** — to **book** (verb) → a **booking** / a **reservation** (noun); a **deposit** is the first part of the price, paid to hold the booking, and **the balance** is the rest; a **refund** gives your money **back**, a **discount** only takes money **off a price you will still pay**; a **voucher** is exchanged for a service already paid for, a **receipt** proves you paid. A **package holiday** sells flight, hotel and **transfers** at one price; an **excursion** is a short organised trip; **facilities** are what the hotel puts at your disposal; **airport taxes**, drinks, excursions and insurance are the things most often **not included**. **scheduled** = the time planned on the timetable, **expected** = the time it will really go (_scheduled 09.30, expected 12.00_ means two and a half hours late); **superb** = excellent; an **oil refinery** is where crude oil becomes fuel.
- **Two rules of the small print** — the **deposit is payable on booking** (no deposit, no booking) and it is **not refundable** once the deadline of the small print has passed.
- **Dissatisfaction, by strength** — _It wasn't quite what we expected_ · _I was rather disappointed with…_ · _I was very disappointed with…_ · _It fell far short of your description_ · _It was far from satisfactory_. English **understates**: _The coach was **not exactly** punctual_ means it was always late. Problems are added up with **to make matters worse**. Grammar: **disappointed** (the person feels it) ≠ **disappointing** (the thing causes it), disappointed **with / by** a thing, **in** a person.
- **The four moves of a complaint** — **1)** open it (_I'd like to make a complaint about…_ ; in writing, _I am writing to complain about…_) · **2)** state the facts, with dates and figures · **3)** recall the promise, in reported speech · **4)** say what you want (_I'd like a refund of…_), and ask only for **what matches what you lost**. **Firm attacks the problem, rude attacks the person.**
- **Reporting the promise** — the tenses step back (_will_ → **would**, _has_ → **had**, _are_ → **were**, _can_ → **could**, _must_ → **had to**, and a past simple one step further: _booked_ → **had booked**), **the time words travel with them** (_today_ → **that day**, _yesterday_ → **the day before**, _last week_ → **the week before**, _tomorrow_ → **the next day**), and **the people change sides**: the advertisement's **you** (the reader) becomes **we / I**, and **you** now names the one who promised. Model: _"You'll love the food."_ → _"**You said that we would love the food**, but we didn't; it was awful."_
- **The pattern of move 3** — _You said (that) + the promise, backshifted + **but** + what really happened._ The reported half alone is only a report; the **but** turns it into a complaint.
- **Reporting verbs that carry the force** — **complain that**, **add that**, **point out that**, **promise that**. Four are enough, and they are the ones the lesson itself uses; _insist_, _admit_ and _deny_ belong to other lessons and to other constructions.
- **Two duties** — **report faithfully** (the manager has the brochure: if it said _near the beach_, do not report _on the beach_), and know how to **read a report backwards** to recover the exact words: _She said the taxes **were** included_ → _"The taxes **are** included."_ (a past _were_ would have become **had been**).
- **The agent's reply** — apologise (_I do apologise for…_) · explain · refuse (_I'm afraid we can't…_) · offer compensation (_a free upgrade on your next booking_). One answer often apologises **and** refuses: listen for the **but**. You accept (_That seems fair_) or refuse politely (_I'm afraid that isn't good enough_ · _I'd rather have a refund_).
- **Word stress** — **1st syllable**: PACKage, SCHEDule, TRAVel, INterested, BOOKing, AGency. **2nd**: dePOSit, reFINery, inCLUDed, exCURsion, suPERB, comPLAINT, hoTEL, faCILities. **3rd**: reserVAtion, recomMEND, disapPOINTed, compenSAtion. Two-syllable words tend to be stressed first as nouns, second as verbs (_hoTEL_ is the exception), and a few **move**: a **RE**fund / to re**FUND**, a **DIS**count / to dis**COUNT**.
