# 📜 Summary: School-Related Words

- **The ladder** — **primary school** (from about five) → **secondary school** → **O level** (**O = Ordinary**, about sixteen) → **A level** (**A = Advanced**, about two years later) → **university**. Ordinary comes first, Advanced comes after: whoever holds the advanced papers is nearer the exit. You **attend** a school (**no preposition**: _attend the lesson_ ✓, "attend at the lesson" ✗ — and *to assist* means *to help*, never *to be present*), and one day you **leave** it; the **school-leaving age** is the earliest age at which that is allowed.
- **The year** — cut into **three** → each piece is a **term**; cut into **two** → each piece is a **semester** (*semi* = half). The week is printed on the **timetable**. Opposite the terms: the **holidays** (*work* / *holiday*).
- **The headings above the subjects** — **the sciences** = physics + chemistry + biology; **languages** = English + French + German + … Both are **collective names**, so one subject alone never answers the question. **Geometry** belongs to maths, and **art** to neither heading.
- **The four operations** — **+** add (*put together*) · **−** **subtract** (*take away*, *take from*) · **×** **multiply** (*times*) · **÷** **divide** (*share among*, *split*). The order in which they are asked for changes the answer.
- **On the page**, from the widest to the smallest: **a book** → **a chapter** → **a paragraph** (one idea) → **a sentence** → **a full stop**. Down the side, left blank: **the margin**. A chapter divides a **book**, never an essay; a full stop closes a **sentence**, never an idea.
- **In the examination room** — the **candidate** sits the paper, the **examiner** sets and marks it. You **sit** a paper, then **pass** or **fail** it. Two pairs that are not the same pair: **written** (on paper) / **oral** (out loud, facing the examiner), and **practical** (done, with your hands) / **theoretical** (known, on the page).
- **The opposites of the field** — teach/learn · clever/stupid · good/bad · hard/easy · pass/fail · practical/theoretical · candidate/examiner · worse/better · attend/leave · last/first · work/holiday · written/oral.
- **Two rules of the opposites game** — **1)** an opposite answers a **meaning**, not a word: _a hard question_ → **easy**, but _a hard bench_ → **soft**. **2)** watch the **part of speech**: in _she works hard_, *hard* is an adverb and neither opposite fits.
- **Word building (the spidergram)** — provide → **provision** · deny → **denial** (y → i) · reduce → **reduction** · attend → **attendance** · expect → **expectation** / **expectant**. Endings: **-ion/-ation**, **-al**, **-ance/-ence**, **-ment**. ⚠️ **The ending cannot be guessed** — "providation", "providment", "deniation", "denyal" do not exist. And **attendance** (being there) ≠ **attention** (listening). Headlines run on these nouns: _the government provided meals_ → **MEALS PROVISION**.
- **The five new words** — **provide** = offer, give · **deny** = refuse to give (*and*, in its other sense, say that something is untrue) · **nutritious** = nourishing, feeding the body well (**not** *tasty*, **not** *plentiful*) · **priority** = what comes **first** when there is not enough for everything · **boost** = raise, push up (its opposite here: **reduce**, bring down).
- **Reading an ad** — **headline** (stops you) · **subtitle** · **body** (facts and figures) · **appeal** (asks you to act) · **contact details**. Predict from the headline, the subtitle and the photograph before reading the body. The last lines name the sender: **.org** = an organisation, and an address beginning with **fundraising** = somebody collecting gifts. **Persuasion** = stating facts **so that the reader acts**; a good appeal shows a **chain** (a meal → the child comes in → the child learns), and never runs it backwards.
- **too + adjective + to** — the quality has passed the point where the action is **possible**: _The fees were too high to pay_ = they were not paid. **1)** the negative is already inside (~~too high not to pay~~). **2)** **too ≠ very**: *very small* measures, *too small* blocks. **3)** with **for + somebody**, that person is the one who acts: _too high for the little ones to reach_ = **the little ones** cannot reach. **4)** it can **praise**: _too honest to copy_. **5)** same idea, other shapes: *so … that …* / *not … enough*.
- **An acrostic** spells a word downwards, one letter per line — and if the model asks for verbs, the letter alone is not enough: on an O line, *offer* and *organise* qualify, *often*, *ordinary* and *opinion* do not.
