# 📜 Summary: Walking Tour

- **Four names, four boxes** — the **British Isles** are a **group of islands**: Great Britain, Ireland and thousands of smaller ones. **Great Britain** is an **island**, and it holds England, Scotland and Wales. **Ireland** is the other main island, and it holds the Republic of Ireland plus, in its north-east corner, Northern Ireland. **The United Kingdom** is a **state** — England + Scotland + Wales + **Northern Ireland**, whose full name says it: _the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland_. So **Northern Ireland is in the UK but not on the island of Great Britain**, and **the Republic of Ireland is in the British Isles but not in the UK**. Capitals: London (England), Edinburgh (Scotland), Cardiff (Wales), Belfast (Northern Ireland) — and Dublin, capital of the independent Republic of Ireland. Around them: the **Atlantic Ocean** to the west, the **North Sea** to the east, the **English Channel** to the south.
- **Describing a place — the model in three moves** — (1) name the whole it belongs to: _one of the constituent countries of the United Kingdom_; (2) say where it sits inside it: _it occupies the northern third of the island of Great Britain_; (3) name its neighbours and on which side: _it shares a land border to the south with England_. Imitating a model means keeping the three moves **and** getting the map right. A **land border** is one you can walk across — two countries on two islands share none.
- **The words** — **border** (frontier) · **constituent** (forming a part of) · **cosmopolitan** (full of people from many countries) · **venue** (a place where a show is held) · **year round** (in all seasons) · **residence** (the house someone lives in) · **official** (belonging to the post, not the person) · **former** (the one before, not the one now) · **spectacular** (so impressive that people stop to watch). An **official residence** comes with the **job**; put **former** in front and the prince no longer lives there.
- **The words of praise, not interchangeable** — **captivating** (it holds you: ten minutes became two hours) · **breathtaking** (for a second you cannot speak) · **magnificent** (grand and impressive) · **splendid** (brilliant, warm general praise) · **to revive** (bring back to life what had gone quiet — so the town was quiet the other eleven months).
- **Reading an ad** — five lines, five answers: **what · when · where do I turn up · how long · how much**. Then read it again for what it does **not** print: an ad never prints what would put you off (the rain, the size of the group, where you will be standing at the end), and **what is missing is information too**. Its three habits: the highlight in capitals, the objections removed in advance (_daily_, _FREE!_), the praise put where you walk past it. And: **an ad proves only what it prints** — _on the way, the fish market_ proves the walk passes it, not that it is open.
- **Describe · persuade · give an opinion** — **describing** = checkable facts, no reader in sight (_it stands / lies / occupies… · it dates from… · it is known for…_); **persuading** = aimed at **you**, imperative + benefit (_don't miss… · come and see… · you will never forget…_); **opinion** = it has an **owner** (_I think… · to me… · in my opinion…_). A superlative is **not** persuasion by itself: _the coldest rooms in the building_ is plain description. A whole text has a purpose too — **to inform**, **to persuade** or **to narrate**.
- **The superlative** — the **length of the adjective** decides: short adjective (and two-syllable ones in _-y_) → **the** + adj + **-est** (the oldest, the warmest, the youngest, the largest, the busiest); long adjective → **the most** + adj (the most captivating, the most memorable, the most spectacular, the most cosmopolitan). Spelling: _-e_ → add **-st** (largest) · consonant + _-y_ → **y→i** (busiest) · one vowel + one consonant → **double** it (hottest). Irregulars: **good → the best · bad → the worst · far → the furthest · little → the least**.
- **What a superlative always needs** — (1) a **range**, named with _in_, _of_ or a relative clause: _the most famous lake **in Britain**_, never _the most famous_ alone; (2) a group of at least **three** (with two, compare: _the older of the two_); (3) an honest reading of its **strength**: _the largest_ = the top; _the **second** largest_ = exactly one is larger (an ordinal says how many stand above); _**one of the** most spectacular ceremonies_ = the **top group**, so finding a better one elsewhere disproves nothing. Never mark it twice: _the most oldest_ ✗, _more older_ ✗ — and keep **the**.
- **Long and short vowels** — the two dots mean *hold it*. **/ɔː/** long, as in **walk** (spellings _al, aw, au, or, war_: talk, north, more, warm) · **/ɜː/** long, as in **first** (_ir, er, ur, ear, wor_: church, world, turn, learn) · **/ɑː/** long, as in **guard** (_ar_: heart, park, star, half) · **/ɒ/** short, as in **odd** (a single _o_ in a closed syllable: novel, stop, watch, want). Your book prints them `/ɔ:/ /ə:/ /a:/ /ɔ/`. **Length changes the word**: cart/cat, port/pot, sheep/ship — and above all **walk** /wɔːk/ against **work** /wɜːk/.
- **The _th_ of north** — **north** and **south** end in the voiceless _th_ of **three**; add **-ern** and **northern** and **southern** take the voiced _th_ of **the** (and _southern_ changes its vowel too: /ˈsʌðən/).
- **Planning a guided tour** — about **two hours on foot**. Start where a stranger cannot get lost and give time and place together; order the stops so you never walk back over the same ground; give each place **one** fact and **one** adjective; end somewhere useful. The route words: _we meet at… · before we reach… · on the way… · once X is behind us… · we come out at… · the walk ends at…_
