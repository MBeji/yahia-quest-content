# 📜 Summary: Education for All

- **An acronym** is a name folded into the first letter of each of its important words (*World Health Organization* → **WHO**), and the machine runs backwards: the letters, read one by one and in order, are your first clue to the name. It is **not** a shortened word (*Prof.* is a tail cut off, not an acronym), and it does **not** imply membership of anything — UNESCO, UNICEF and UNO are United Nations bodies; WWF, OPEC and the IMF are not.
- **The eleven of the lesson** — **UNO** United Nations Organization · **UNESCO** United Nations Educational Scientific and Cultural Organization · **UNICEF** United Nations International Children's Emergency Fund · **WHO** World Health Organization · **WFP** World Food Programme · **WTO** World Trade Organization · **WWF** World Wide Fund · **FAO** Food and Agriculture Organization · **IMF** International Monetary Fund · **OPEC** Oil Producing and Exporting Countries · **NOW** National Organization of Women. Four open on the same **W**: it is the **second** letter that separates health, food, trade and a fund.
- **The three the book explains** — **UNESCO** (1945): peace and security through educational, scientific and cultural relations between nations. **UNICEF** (1946): children's rights, and food, medicines, health care, **education** and a decent living. **WFP** (1963, Rome): the fight against hunger. ⇒ children out of school → **UNICEF and UNESCO**; a class that arrives hungry → **the WFP**. For the other eight the expanded name is all you are given, and all you may reason from.
- **Reading a graph: the title and the date first.** *Regional distribution of children out of school, 2001/02* tells you what the circle is a picture of (every out-of-school child of primary age in the world — **115 million**) and when the count was made. Only then the slices. **Check**: the shares of a pie are parts of one whole, so they must come to **100**.
- **The eight slices** — South Asia **36.7 %** / 42.3 M · West and Central Africa **20.8 %** / 24.0 M · Eastern and Southern Africa **18.6 %** / 21.4 M · East Asia and the Pacific **8.7 %** / 10.0 M · Middle East and North Africa **7.6 %** / 8.8 M · Latin America and the Caribbean **2.8 %** / 3.3 M · Central and Eastern Europe **2.5 %** / 2.9 M · Industrialized countries **2.3 %** / 2.6 M.
- ⚠️ **Distribution ≠ rate.** 2.3 % beside the industrialized countries means **2.3 % of the world's out-of-school children live there** — not that 2.3 % of their own children are out of school. For that you would need a figure the graph never gives: how many children the region holds altogether.
- **The two columns are the same thing in two units** (8.7 % of 115 M = 10.0 M), so they rank the regions identically — and a missing box can be worked out: 21.4 out of 115 = **18.6 %**. Copying 21.4 into the percentage column is the classic slip.
- **out of school** counts every child **who is of an age to be at school and whom no school anywhere holds on its register today** — not children who have gone home, and not children sent into the corridor. The report calls being kept outside the system **exclusion**.
- **The three new words** — **an acronym** = a name folded into initials · **enrolment** = registration, putting a name on a register · **therefore** = consequently, what follows from what has just been said (not *however*, which turns a sentence round; not *for example*, which stays on the same idea).
- ⚠️ **enrolment ≠ attendance.** The register counts **names**, once a year; attendance counts **faces**, day by day. Enrolment up from 54 % to 90 % proves that more names went down, and nothing at all about who is in the room in March.
- **From the ad's page**: to **donate** = to give money to a cause · a **recreation kit** = a boxed set of things to play with · **exercise books** = the empty ones you fill with your own handwriting (**not** books about sport, **not** the printed volumes a teacher sets).
- **The UNICEF ad** — what stops the eye in the middle of the page is **an equation**: *Grade 2 + $1 = Grade 3*. Unfolded, it promises that **a gift of one dollar is the whole distance between the class a child is in and the class above it**. A sum is taken in at a glance and looks proved, which is why it is there. **The purpose of the page is to persuade the reader to donate money** — not to inform him: the equation stops him, the small figures make the gift look easy, the address says where to send it.
- **Words printed unfinished** (first letters given, one dot printed for every letter left out): **two** things must agree — **the letters and the length**, then **the sense the sentence needs**. *Continuing, contacting, conducting, containing* all fit *con……* in ten letters; only the meaning picks **containing**. Same trap with *chance* and *choice*: a chance is an opportunity, a choice is a menu.
- **if + negative verb = unless + positive verb** — _**If** we **do not** give, children will not have a chance._ = _**Unless** we **give**, children will not have a chance._ The *not* has gone because **unless is carrying it** (*unless* = **if not**).
  **1)** never a second negative after it (~~unless we don't give~~ says the opposite). **2)** its reach stops at the clause it introduces — the other half keeps the verb it had. **3)** *unless* is **not** *if*: the two promise contrary things. **4)** either clause may come first (comma when *unless* opens). **5)** an *unless* sentence settles **both** cases at once: what happens if the condition fails, and what happens if it is met.
- **Hypothesizing** is what these sentences are for: _Unless these children are enrolled, the region will lose a generation._ And the saying attributed to Aristotle says it with an image — knowledge is a fountain that **runs dry unless** fresh water keeps arriving: whoever stops learning does not stand still, he loses what he had.
