# 📜 Summary: Exploring a Song

- **What there is to hear in a song** — four things you can like separately: the **singer** (the voice), the **music** (instruments, arrangement, rhythm), the **melody** (the tune you hum once the words are gone) and the **lyrics** (the words that are sung). The **topic** is what the song talks about; the **message** is what it asks you to believe or to do once it is over — find it in the words that **come back** and in the **last lines**. The message the lesson reads in _Immortality_ (sung by Celine Dion) is that **we must do everything to fulfil our dreams**.
- **Feelings, and how to prove them** — six words to have ready: **sadness**, **hope**, **fear**, **determination**, **jealousy**, **pessimism** (the lesson hears **hope** and **determination** in _Immortality_). Naming the feeling is half the work: **point at the words that carry it**. And before the music starts, **read the task** — you cannot search for a word you have never read, and a song will not wait for you.
- **Saying how much you like it** — the ladder, from coldest to warmest: _I **can't stand** it_ · _I **don't really like** it_ · _I **quite like** it_ (mild approval, not enthusiasm) · _I **like** it very much_ · _I'm **crazy about** it_. Beside the top rung: _I'm **fond of** …_, _I'm **keen on** …_, _I'm **mad about** …_ — and after **of / on / about** a verb takes the **-ing** form (_keen on singing_ ✓, "keen on to sing" ✗), while _like_ and _love_ accept both. **mad / crazy about** carries no anger at all.
- **Asking about tastes** — _What **type** of hits do you like?_ (a kind of music) · _What do you like **in** a hit?_ (the singer, the music, the melody or the lyrics) · _**Which song** is your favourite?_ (a title) · _**Why**?_ (a reason).
- **The words** — from the life story: **stardom** (being famous as a performer), **superstar status** (level of importance), **prowess** (great skill), to **convene** (to come together), to **mortgage** (to give a bank the right to own your house for a time against a loan), **renown** (fame). From the song: **faith** (what you believe in), **fate** (what is meant to happen whatever you do), **immortality** (living for ever), **memory**, **sorrow** (deep sadness), **soul**, **eternity**, to **fulfil** a dream. Two traps: **faith ≠ fate**, and **prowess** (what you can do) ≠ **renown** (what people know).
- **Modals — form** — base form after the modal (no _to_, no _-ing_), **no -s** in the third person (_he can play_), **no do/does/did** (question by inversion, negative with _not_).
- **Modals — meaning** — **can**: ability (_she can hold a note_), possibility (_you can hear the bass from the street_), permission, usually as a question (_Can I take this bag?_). **can't**: inability, impossibility, permission refused. **must**: obligation, necessity (_we must hurry_). **mustn't**: **prohibition** (_you mustn't use a flash_). ⚠️ **mustn't never means "it isn't necessary"** — that is _don't have to_. In the affirmative, **have to** says what **must** says (_they have to wait_ = _they must wait_); the two only part company in the negative. And an obligation turns into a prohibition by forbidding the **opposite** action: _You must show your ticket at the door_ = _You mustn't go in without your ticket._
- **Minimal pairs** — two words separated by **one single sound**, everything else identical: _them / then_, _faith / fate_, _love / luck_, _storm / store_. Words that merely sound close are **not** minimal pairs (_history / memory_, _destination / destiny_, _may / might_ — two differences, not one), and two words that sound exactly alike are **homophones**, not a minimal pair (_write / right_ — no difference at all).
- **The `[ou]` column** — `[ou]` as in **home**, spelt o, oa, ow, o…e, ou (_so, only, won't, road, grow, follow, hope, soul_), sorted against the `[ei]` of _Space Tourism_ (_faith, makes, say_). Three spellings lie: **ow** is /aʊ/ in _now, how, down_; **o…e** is /ʌ/ in _love, come, done_ and /uː/ in _prove, move, lose_; **ou** is /aʊ/ in _loud, round, about_. Same or different? _through – choose_ (same), _journey – eternity_ (same), _wind – wild_ (different), _vision – joker_ (different).
- **Metaphor and paraphrase** — a **metaphor** speaks of one thing as if it were another, with no _like_ and no _as_ (_her sunny smile_, _a mountain of homework_, _time flies_); in _Immortality_ eternity is spoken of as a road, so the journey stands for a life that does not stop at death. To **paraphrase**: replace the image by what it means, replace words by synonyms, change the structure — _The whole family convened in front of the television set_ → _Everybody in the family came together in front of the television._ A paraphrase keeps **all** the meaning and adds **nothing**.
